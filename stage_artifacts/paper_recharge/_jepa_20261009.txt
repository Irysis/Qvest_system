                                                                                  ABSTRACT

                                                 Learning effective representations helps characterize the structure and dynamics
                                                 of equity markets from financial data with a low signal-to-noise ratio. Black-box
                                                 deep models can capture complex patterns but may overfit sample noise and lack
                                                 explicit economic structure. Meanwhile, classic linear financial models provide
                                                 interpretable references, but their oversimplified assumptions leave non-linear sig-
                                                 nals uncaptured. To combine the strengths of these two directions, we propose
                                                 STOCK-JEPA, a joint-embedding predictive framework that learns predictable in-
                                                 cremental revisions relative to a point-in-time financial prior. First, we leverage
                                                 a low-complexity financial model to produce fixed statistics summarizing multi-
                                                 horizon return and risk. A prior projector then maps these statistics into the target
                                                 encoder's latent space as an anchor. Second, we design a context-conditioned re-
                                                 vision predictor to estimate the future representation's predictable displacement
                                                 from the anchor. Separate losses update the two branches: the anchor learns
                                                 from prior statistics, while the revision captures additional predictable informa-
                                                 tion from historical context. Third, we freeze all representation modules and train
                                                 a downstream readout, evaluating its forecasts through cross-sectional ranking and
                                                 portfolio performance. Theoretically, we prove that optimal revision reduces the
                                                 prior anchor's expected squared error for the same future representation by exactly
                                                 E[22]. This non-negative gain is the expected squared magnitude of the addi-
                                                 tional signal predictable from historical context. Experimentally, STOCK-JEPA
                                                 outperforms 13 strong baselines across large-scale China and U.S. equity uni-
                                                 verses on 5 key evaluation metrics. Ablation studies and representation analysis
                                                 further demonstrate the value of the learned revisions for representation learning
                                                 in equity markets.

                                       1 INTRODUCTION

                                       Financial representation learning aims to characterize market structure and return-risk dynamics
                                       from noisy financial data, providing reusable representations for relevant tasks such as return fore-
                                       casting and portfolio management. However, the low signal-to-noise nature of financial data leads
                                       to poor predictive signals, and future outcomes contain insider information unavailable to the pub-
                                       lic (Kyle, 1985). Advanced deep learning models have the ability to capture complex non-linear
                                       patterns, but the learned relationships do not automatically admit a clear economic interpretation
                                       (Gu et al., 2020; Giglio et al., 2022). End-to-end training using realized future outcomes as targets
                                       may also overfit sample-specific noise, weakening out-of-sample generalization (Wang et al., 2021).
                                       These limitations motivate enhancing deep models with traditional financial models, which provide
                                       structured and interpretable priors.

                                       Traditional financial factor models describe each stock's return as a weighted combination of a few
                                       shared factors, such as broad market movements, plus an asset-specific component (Ross, 1976).
                                       These weights, called factor exposures, indicate how strongly the stock responds to each factor.
                                       Conditional factor models allow the weights to change with market conditions and firm characteris-
                                       tics available at prediction time (Ferson & Harvey, 1999; Kelly et al., 2019). These models provide a
                                       compact, financially grounded prior, but they are linear in form and can leave non-linear interactions

unexplained. Residual learning offers a natural extension: a financial model supplies a baseline,
while a deep model uses historical context to learn additional predictive structure (Avellaneda & Lee,
2010; Guijarro-Ordonez et al., 2025). However, the observed residual can mix omitted predictive
signals with unpredictable noise (example in Figure 1). If we instead predict a learned representation
of the future state, the target can summarize return and risk patterns, giving the model flexibility to
emphasize predictable structure over the details of realized outcomes. Joint-Embedding Predictive
Architectures (JEPAs) support this approach by predicting encoded targets without reconstructing
complete observations (LeCun, 2022; Assran et al., 2023). To adapt JEPAs to equity markets, one
major challenge is that the vanilla generic objectives do not explicitly distinguish what a financial
prior can predict from what historical context can add.

This motivates a simple question: can we start from a financial prior and directly learn predictable
revisions to future representations? We propose STOCK-JEPA, a joint-embedding predictive frame-
work that learns incremental latent revisions relative to a point-in-time financial prior. A low-
complexity financial model produces fixed statistics of multi-horizon return and risk, which a prior
projector maps into the target encoder's latent space to form an anchor. A context-conditioned re-
vision predictor then uses historical observations to estimate the future representation's predictable
displacement from this anchor. To prevent the two branches from compensating for each other, we
introduce separate objectives: the prior branch predicts the future representation from prior statistics,
while the context branch learns the remaining predictable component. We freeze all representation
modules and train only a downstream readout to assess their predictive and portfolio utility. When
predicting the same future representation, adding historical context can only maintain or reduce the
minimum achievable mean squared error relative to the prior alone. The optimal revision captures
this additional predictability, reducing the error by exactly the non-negative quantity E[22].

We conduct experiments on full-size China and U.S. equity universes with consideration of realistic
settings (e.g., chronological splits, transaction cost and long-short constraints). Among 13 base-
lines, STOCK-JEPA achieves the best performance across 4 out of 5 evaluation metrics in each
market, improving RankIC by 14.0% and 15.9% over the second-best method. Ablation studies
support the value of the prior-anchored pipeline, the incremental contribution of revisions, and the
portfolio benefits of combining separate objectives with an EMA anchor. Structural and temporal
diagnostics reveal stock-level variation and broad revision adjustments around market events. Con-
trolled simulation further supports our formulation: the anchor captures predictable structure from
the prior, while the revision learns complementary signals from historical context, even with noisy
future targets.

Our contributions are fourfold:

        · We formulate financial representation learning as learning predictable revisions beyond a
          structured prior, separating what history can help predict from what remains unpredictable.

        · We prove that, to forecast the same future representation, adding historical context reduces
          the best possible squared prediction error by exactly E[22] when history helps predict.

        · We introduce STOCK-JEPA, a joint-embedding framework that combines structured finan-
          cial priors with deep predictive learning through distinct anchor and revision objectives.

        · Frozen STOCK-JEPA representations achieve the highest mean RankIC and net Sharpe
          among evaluated methods on large-scale China and U.S. equity universes, with ablations
          supporting the revisions' incremental value and controlled simulation showing that the an-
          chor and revision learn complementary return signals from noisy data.

2 RELATED WORK

2.1 FINANCIAL MODELS AND RESIDUAL LEARNING

Conditional factor models provide a structured prior for returns. IPCA uses firm characteristics to
model time-varying exposures to latent common factors (Kelly et al., 2019). Residual-based strate-
gies examine temporal signals left outside this structure. Avellaneda and Lee model idiosyncratic
returns as mean-reverting processes (Avellaneda & Lee, 2010), while Deep Learning Statistical Ar-
bitrage extracts trading signals from the histories of factor-residual portfolios (Guijarro-Ordonez

                                                           2
Preprint

et al., 2025). These strategies derive trading signals from factor-adjusted returns, but operate in re-
turn space and do not define a prior for predicting future-state representations. STOCK-JEPA instead
uses a financial anchor to define predictable revisions in representation space.

2.2 DEEP FINANCIAL FACTOR MODELS

Deep factor models combine financial structure with neural flexibility. Conditional autoencoders
learn non-linear mappings from firm characteristics to factor exposures (Gu et al., 2021), while Fac-
torVAE models latent factor distributions through variational inference (Duan et al., 2022). Other
methods explicitly combine financial priors with learned factors. FactorGCL extracts complemen-
tary factors from residual stock embeddings using hypergraphs and temporal contrastive learning
(Duan et al., 2025), while PRISM-VQ combines expert factors with vector-quantized latent factors
for stock ranking (Kim & Song, 2026). These methods demonstrate how financial structure can
guide deep learning, but organize the learned representations as factors or exposures for return pre-
diction. STOCK-JEPA instead keeps a financial prior as an anchor, without requiring the learned
future-state revisions to admit a factor interpretation.

2.3 PREDICTIVE REPRESENTATION LEARNING

JEPAs predict target embeddings from observable context without reconstructing raw observations
(Assran et al., 2023). TS-JEPA adapts this principle to masked time-series patches (Ennadir et al.,
2024). Financial applications learn market-level states from equity cross-sections (Mahns et al.,
2026) or temporal representations of individual stocks (Wang, 2026). Phys-JEPA partitions latent
states into physical and residual components, using observed physical variables to constrain the
physical component and its temporal changes (Nie et al., 2026). Predicting latent targets or parti-
tioning latent states does not itself distinguish what a prior can predict from what richer context adds.
STOCK-JEPA addresses this gap through a financial anchor and separate objectives for prediction
from the prior and revision from historical context.

4 METHODOLOGY

STOCK-JEPA in Figure 2 learns future-state representations by combining a structured financial
prior with a prediction of what historical context can add. Following Section 3, it first constructs
financial prior statistics, then learns a latent anchor and its predictable revision. A downstream return
readout evaluates the resulting features with all representation modules frozen.

The anchor loss updates only A, while the revision loss updates only E and Q. The anchor is

4.3 RETURN READOUT WITH FROZEN REPRESENTATIONS

The downstream readout maps the prior-anchored representations to return forecasts. The historical
summary si,t and financial prior Pi,t provide prior features. The anchor expresses the prior in

the target latent coordinates, while the predicted revision i,t summarizes the learned context-
dependent adjustment relative to that anchor. A single MLP combines the four descriptors:

          ui,t = si,t; Pi,t; Zia,ntc; i,t ,            yi,t = f(ui,t).            (11)

Here, si,t  R40 is the coordinate-wise mean of the standardized input features over the 126-day
context window. Providing both the prior and its anchor exposes the financial descriptors and their
learned latent representation to the readout. The non-linear MLP can use these prior features to
condition how the predicted revision contributes to the return forecast.

After selecting the representation checkpoint, we freeze representation modules and train only the
MLP on 21-day log-return labels. This separates representation learning from return-supervised

5 EXPERIMENT

5.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP

Datasets and experimental setup. We conduct experiments on the CN ALL and US ALL
datasets collected from Wind and Norgate Data, respectively, covering 5,669 and 10,152 histori-
cally tradable securities in the China and U.S. stock markets. We use 2014­2015 for initial prior
fitting, 2016­2021 for representation and readout training, 2022­2023 for validation, and January
2024­June 2026 for testing. We exclude cross-boundary target windows and use training-only nor-
malization statistics. Experiments run on a server with eight NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPUs. Following
the expanding-window estimation principle of Gu et al. (2020), financial priors use historical out-
comes and remain fixed within each prediction block. After representation learning, we freeze the
prior and representation modules and train only the MLP return readout, selecting checkpoints on
validation data. Dataset details and implementation settings are provided in Appendices F and C.

5.2 CROSS-SECTIONAL RANKING AND PORTFOLIO EVALUATION

Baselines and evaluation. We compare STOCK-JEPA with 13 baselines from 4 model families.
Traditional financial models include FMB-style (Fama & MacBeth, 1973) and IPCA (Kelly et al.,
2019). General time-series models include LSTM (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997), Transformer
(Vaswani et al., 2017), iTransformer (Liu et al., 2024), and TimeMixer (Wang et al., 2024). Finance-
specific models include FactorVAE (Duan et al., 2022), StockMixer (Fan & Shen, 2024), MASTER
(Li et al., 2024), and PRISM-VQ (Kim & Song, 2026). JEPA baselines include T-JEPA (Thimonier
et al., 2025), TS-JEPA (Ennadir et al., 2024), and Fin-JEPA (Wang, 2026). All baselines are re-
trained using predefined hyperparameter configurations, with common validation and test periods
and checkpoints selected by validation loss. Evaluation uses a common 21-day horizon and reports
Pearson IC, Spearman RankIC, and portfolio performance. We form equal-weighted top-30 long-
only batches in China (Li et al., 2024) and top-30/bottom-30 long­short batches in the U.S. Methods
share market-specific rules, charging 5 bps (Conlon et al., 2025) per unit of executed notional at en-
try and exit. Portfolio metrics derive from daily closing NAV. See Appendices E and G.

Results. Table 1 shows that STOCK-JEPA ranks first in four of five metrics in each market. Ran-
kIC reaches 11.22% on CN ALL and 12.96% on US ALL, exceeding MASTER and FactorVAE
by 14.0% and 15.9%, respectively. The strongest JEPA baselines achieve RankIC of 4.09% in China
and 0.69% in the U.S., both well below STOCK-JEPA. Mean net annualized returns and net Sharpe
ratios are also highest in both markets: 45.66% and 1.50 in China, and 68.20% and 1.24 in the U.S.
The broad U.S. universe includes small-cap and highly volatile stocks. Selecting 30 stocks on each
side can expose individual batches to sharp price moves, particularly losses from upward jumps in
short positions. Under this common U.S. portfolio protocol, STOCK-JEPA records the least severe
mean maximum drawdown (-40.12%), complementing its leading return and Sharpe. Traditional
financial models remain competitive: FMB-style and IPCA attain CN ALL RankIC of 8.76% and
8.69%, exceeding all general deep-learning baselines and supporting our motivation to combine
financial structure with deep learning.

5.3 UNDERSTANDING THE PRIOR-ANCHORED REPRESENTATION

Our framework aims to turn historical information into useful revisions to a financial prior. We
examine whether it achieves this through three questions: (i) do the framework's components and
training choices improve downstream performance? (ii) do revisions distinguish stocks and adjust
as market conditions change? and (iii) can known predictive signals be recovered from the anchor
and revision when learned from noisy data?

learning. We use known component labels only to fit and evaluate Ridge probes on frozen features
(Figure 4, right). For Separate-EMA, the anchor achieves a mean test R2 of 66.14% on the slow

Although the slow state is more persistent, its return contribution depends on both the state and asset-
specific exposures; persistence alone does not ensure linear recovery from learned features. A related
finding in asset pricing is that incorporating expected-return information helps recover economically
relevant factors missed by covariance-based extraction (Lettau & Pelger, 2020). Our results suggest
that the calibrated prior helps preserve persistent return structure, while revision learning retains
complementary faster signals. This role for financial structure is consistent with the forecasting
benefits of economically motivated restrictions (Campbell & Thompson, 2008). Together, these
findings support the idea behind Equation 3: the anchor predicts from the prior, while the revision
uses historical context to learn predictable corrections from noisy future targets.

6 CONCLUSION

We introduced STOCK-JEPA, which learns prior-relative revisions through separate anchor and
revision objectives. Population analysis characterizes the risk reduction from historical context.
Frozen representations lead evaluated methods in mean RankIC and net Sharpe in both markets.
Ablations support incremental predictive value; diagnostics show stock-level and temporal variation,
and simulation demonstrates complementary signal recovery from noisy targets.

C REPRESENTATION ARCHITECTURE AND OPTIMIZATION

Sequence encoders and masking. STOCK-JEPA uses the same sequence-encoder architecture
for the 126-day historical context and the 21-day future target window. Each 40-dimensional daily
observation is linearly projected to a 128-dimensional token. A learnable summary token and po-
sitional embeddings are then added, followed by four pre-normalized Transformer layers. Each
layer uses eight self-attention heads, a 512-dimensional feed-forward network, GELU activation,
and dropout of 0.1. The final summary token is layer-normalized to produce a 128-dimensional
representation.

During training, contiguous temporal blocks of 5­21 days are sampled until at least 30% of the
context positions are masked, and their projected tokens are replaced by a learnable mask token.
Independently, each predefined feature group is zeroed across the context window with probability
0.25. The six groups cover returns and momentum, volatility, price ranges, trading activity and
liquidity, market-relative features, and market descriptors. The future target window is not masked,
and all context masking is disabled during validation and inference.

Prior projector and revision predictor. The prior projector takes the 16-dimensional prediction­
log-scale vector defined in Appendix B. It consists of a linear map from 16 to 128 dimensions,
followed by LayerNorm, GELU, and a second 128-dimensional linear layer. The revision predictor
concatenates the 128-dimensional context representation and the detached EMA anchor, then applies
a 256  256  128 MLP with GELU and dropout of 0.1 between its linear layers.

of the detached training displacement Dti,rtn. At update k, let mk and vk denote the running mean

Optimization and checkpoint selection. Two AdamW optimizers separately update the online

no gradients. We select the representation checkpoint that minimizes the sum of the anchor and
normalized revision losses on the predefined validation subset.

E BASELINE IMPLEMENTATION

Common data and prediction task. The 13 baselines share the prepared stock panels, evaluation
targets, and validation and test periods with STOCK-JEPA. All baselines are trained on 2014­2021
data, whereas STOCK-JEPA uses 2014­2015 for initial S1 calibration and 2016­2021 for represen-
tation and readout training. Canonical inputs contain 126 trading sessions with 28 stock features and
12 market features. The prepared training split contains samples with prediction date  January
1, 2014 and future end < January 1, 2022. The prepared validation split contains samples with
prediction date  January 1, 2022 and future end < January 1, 2024. Test forecast dates begin
on January 1, 2024. Samples whose target windows cross split boundaries are excluded. These dates
define the prepared splits.

Return-regression models predict the standardized 21-day cumulative log return. Predictions are
transformed back to log-return units before evaluation. Model-specific adapters organize the com-
mon inputs into sequences, feature tokens, patches, or same-date cross-sections.

Baseline training configurations. The supervised neural baselines are trained for six epochs with
at most 1,000 updates per epoch, AdamW with learning rate 3 × 10-4, weight decay 0.01, gradient-
norm clipping at 1.0, and a nominal batch size of 256. Model configurations are fixed before test
evaluation, and checkpoints are selected by the lowest validation SmoothL1 loss. FactorVAE and
PRISM-VQ additionally retain their implemented training-only auxiliary objectives.

The FMB-style baseline (Fama & MacBeth, 1973) fits regularized cross-sectional regressions sepa-
rately on each training date and averages their coefficients for out-of-sample prediction, with regu-
larization selected by validation MSE. IPCA (Kelly et al., 2019) estimates characteristic-based factor
loadings and latent factors by alternating least squares on training data, selects the factor count by
validation MSE, and forecasts using the training-period mean factor rather than factors inferred from
held-out returns.

T-JEPA, TS-JEPA, and Fin-JEPA first learn representations and then train a linear return readout with
the encoder frozen. Pretraining uses at most 12 epochs of 1,000 updates, AdamW with learning rate
3 × 10-4 and weight decay 0.04. The lowest validation representation loss selects the pretraining
checkpoint. The downstream readout follows the six-epoch validation-selection protocol above.

T-JEPA forms feature tokens from the 126-session channel histories. TS-JEPA partitions the input
into 18 patches of seven sessions. Fin-JEPA uses the last 30 context sessions and a 10-session
future representation target within the canonical windows; its downstream return horizon remains
21 sessions. Stock-JEPA architecture and readout settings are specified in Appendices C and D.

Configurations. Separate-EMA implements our representation-learning mechanism under the
controlled ablation protocol: the online prior projector receives separate anchor supervision, while
revision prediction uses an EMA anchor without propagating gradients to the projector. Separate-
online retains this objective separation and gradient routing but uses the online anchor during train-
ing and inference. Joint-online instead jointly optimizes total-representation prediction, removing
separate anchor supervision and gradient blocking. Ordinary-JEPA directly predicts the future rep-
resentation, with prior conditioning zeroed and the unused projector frozen. The separate objectives
share one batch and backward pass.

Controlled training. For each market and seed, all mechanisms continue from the same S2 check-

with batch size 256. We use AdamW (learning rate 10-4, weight decay 0.01), gradient clipping at
1.0, target-encoder EMA decay 0.996, and anchor EMA decay 0.99 where applicable. Additional
variance, covariance, and orthogonality regularizers are disabled; the epoch-four checkpoint is re-
tained. All mechanisms use unclipped diagonal EMA whitening with detached, mechanism-specific
statistics. For z = a + Du + µ, the joint loss is
