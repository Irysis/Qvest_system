# P106 — Kyle (1985): Continuous Auctions and Insider Trading

**Citation:** Kyle, A.S. (1985). Continuous auctions and insider trading. *Econometrica*, 53(6), 1315–1336.

---

## Core Thesis

Seminal microstructure paper establishing the **Kyle model** of trading with asymmetric information. A single informed trader (insider) strategically reveals information through trades in a market where noise traders provide cover. Market makers set prices competitively using order flow. The key output is **Kyle's lambda (λ)** — the price impact per unit of net order flow — which defines market depth.

Three trading scenarios analyzed:
1. Single batch auction (one round)
2. Sequential batch auctions (multiple rounds)
3. Continuous-time limit

The informed trader optimally "camouflages" trades within noise trader order flow, exploiting information slowly to maximize total profit.

---

## Factor Construction

**Model setup:**
- Informed trader observes fundamental value ṽ ~ N(p_0, Σ_0)
- Noise traders have aggregate order flow ũ ~ N(0, σ_u²)
- Market maker observes only total order flow y = x̃ + ũ (informed + noise)
- Market maker sets price: P(y) = p_0 + λ * y

**Single auction equilibrium:**
- Informed trader optimal order: x̃ = β(ṽ - p_0), where β = (σ_u²/Σ_0)^(1/2) / 2... actually:

    β = (1/2) * (σ_u / Σ_0^(1/2))
    λ = (1/2) * (Σ_0^(1/2) / σ_u)

**Interpretation of λ:**
- λ = price impact per unit order flow
- Higher λ = LESS liquid (each unit of order flow moves prices more)
- **Market depth** = 1/λ (how many units of order flow needed to move price by $1)

**In continuous auction limit:**
- Prices follow Brownian motion
- λ remains **constant** throughout the trading day (depth is uniform over time)
- Informed trader exhausts all profitable trades exactly at the end of trading

**Key insight:** λ² = (Σ_0) / (4 * σ_u²) ... or λ = Σ_0^(1/2) / (2 * σ_u)

Higher fundamental uncertainty (Σ_0) or lower noise trading (σ_u) → HIGHER λ → LESS liquid

---

## Key Results

- In equilibrium, price incorporates 1/2 of private information in single auction (perfect learning in continuous limit)
- Market efficiency: prices converge to fundamental value as trading progresses
- **Price efficiency** and **market depth** are inversely related: deeper markets are less informationally efficient
- Noise trader participation is NECESSARY for informed trading to exist (otherwise no one to trade with)
- Residual variance: posterior Σ_1 = Σ_0 / 2 (half of fundamental uncertainty resolved in single auction)

The paper does NOT directly test asset pricing implications — it is a pure microstructure model. The pricing implications (that λ should predict returns) come from subsequent empirical papers (Brennan-Subrahmanyam 1996, Amihud 2002).

---

## Korea-Specific Implications

- Korean market has high retail participation (noise traders) which should theoretically imply LOW λ (deeper market) — yet empirically illiquidity premiums exist, suggesting noise trader identification may be imperfect
- Price limit system (+/-30% daily) in Korea creates artificial liquidity: stocks hitting limits have effectively infinite λ during limit periods — this must be handled in any Kyle-lambda estimation
- Informed trading (사모 펀드, institutional block trades) creates permanent price impacts — the permanent component of lambda (Sadka 2006, P112) is more relevant for Korean context than transitory component
- KOSDAQ small-caps: noise trading may be lower relative to informed trading → higher λ expected

## Actionable Implementation

Kyle's lambda is not directly estimable from daily OHLCV data without tick data. However, the theoretical framework motivates:

```r
# Kyle-lambda proxy: price impact ratio (variant of Amihud ILLIQ)
# Theoretical: λ = (price change) / (order flow)
# Empirical proxy: |ret| / (volume * price) = ILLIQ

# For Korean market, Kyle's framework motivates:
# 1. ILLIQ as lambda proxy (most practical with daily data)
# 2. Price impact asymmetry: buys vs sells (requires T&S data)
# 3. Information share measure (Hasbrouck 1995) if multiple exchanges exist

# Amihud ILLIQ is the best available Kyle-lambda proxy with daily data
illiq <- abs(ret) / (volume_krw / 1e8)  # normalize by 100M KRW
```

**Theoretical anchor:** Kyle (1985) provides the formal model foundation for all price-impact based liquidity measures. Amihud ILLIQ (P100), Brennan-Subrahmanyam lambda (P103), and Sadka's measure (P112) all derive from this framework.
