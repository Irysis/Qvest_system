# P129: The Volatility Effect: Lower Risk without Lower Return (Blitz & van Vliet 2007)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Low_Volatility
**Relevance to STR_011 improvement:** Critical

## Core Claim
Low-volatility stocks globally deliver higher risk-adjusted returns than high-volatility stocks. Using a simple 3-year weekly return volatility sort with monthly rebalancing and equal weighting, the effect is pervasive across developed markets. The volatility effect is comparable in magnitude to the value and momentum premiums and is SEPARATE from them.

## Factor Construction (exact formula if different from basic idio-vol)
**Main specification:**
- Universe: FTSE World Developed Index constituents (~2,000 stocks)
- Period: 1986-2006 (20 years)
- Sorting variable: 3-year (36-month) trailing weekly total return volatility
  - Calculated as standard deviation of weekly returns over the prior 156 weeks
- Portfolio: decile portfolios, equally weighted
- Rebalancing: monthly
- Returns: raw (no FF adjustment initially), then FF-3 adjusted

**Key formula:**
Vol_i,t = std(weekly_returns_i, t-156 weeks to t)

**Alternative lookbacks tested (Exhibit 9):**
- 1-year vol: global alpha spread = 11.2% per annum
- 3-year vol: global alpha spread = 12.0% per annum (winner, used as main)
- 3-year vol vs 1-year vol: similar results but 3-year has lower turnover
- Beta sort: weaker and less clean than volatility sort

## Key Results
**Exhibit 1 — Decile portfolio statistics (Global, 1986-2006):**
| Decile | Excess Return | Std Dev | Sharpe | Beta | Alpha | MDD |
|--------|--------------|---------|--------|------|-------|-----|
| D1 (Low Vol) | 7.3% | 10.1% | 0.72 | 0.56 | 4.0% | -26% |
| D10 (High Vol) | 1.4% | 27.5% | ~0.05 | 1.58 | -8.0% | -86% |
| D1 - D10 spread | 5.9% | — | +0.67 | -1.02 | 12.0% | 60pp better MDD |

**Regional breakdown (Sharpe D1 vs D10):**
- US: D1 Sharpe = 0.58 vs D10 near 0
- Europe: D1 Sharpe = 0.49 vs D10 negative
- Japan: D1 Sharpe = 0.34 vs D10 near 0

**FF-3 alpha analysis:**
- After controlling for market, size, value: 8.1% global alpha remains unexplained
- Volatility effect is SEPARATE from size (small-cap), value (BtM), and momentum effects
- Double-sorting shows that within each size/value/momentum quintile, low-vol beats high-vol

**Regime analysis:**
- Low-vol UNDERPERFORMS in BULL markets (price to pay)
- Low-vol strongly OUTPERFORMS in BEAR markets (anti-bubble behavior)
- This is the fundamental tradeoff — low-vol = defensive, not offensive

**Lookback window comparison:**
- 3-year weekly vol > 1-year monthly vol in alpha (12.0% vs 11.2%)
- 3-year weekly vol has LOWER turnover (fewer false signals)
- Beta sort: alpha = 7.0% globally (weaker than vol sort)

## STR_011 Improvement Insights
- CAGR improvement: Switching from 1-month IVOL (Ang et al. specification) to 3-year weekly total return volatility as the PRIMARY sort may increase the raw return premium (12.0% alpha spread vs the standard measure)
- MDD improvement: The low-vol D1 portfolio showed MDD of -26% vs -86% for D10 — the 3-year lookback inherently selects structurally stable companies, reducing portfolio-level MDD substantially
- Sharpe improvement: The volatility effect produces Sharpe 0.72 vs ~0.05 for high-vol, a spread of 0.67 — blending 3-year weekly vol with the current IVOL/Beta composite should improve portfolio Sharpe beyond current 0.907

## Korea Applicability
- The paper covers developed markets (US, Europe, Japan) — Korea not explicitly in the 1986-2006 sample
- However, the effect is demonstrated in ALL developed markets with large enough universes
- Blitz, Pang & van Vliet (2012) later extended to emerging markets including Korea — see Baker & Haugen (2012) for Korea evidence
- For STR_011: 3-year weekly vol is easily computable from RAWDATA.parquet (weekly returns from daily OHLCVS data)
- The equal-weighting of decile portfolios vs value-weighting: equal-weight slightly amplifies small-cap effects; for concentrated 20-30 stock Korean strategy, equal/cap-blend is appropriate

## Actionable Modifications for STR_011
1. Add 3-year (156-week) weekly total return volatility as an additional factor signal alongside current 1-month IVOL — this captures the longer-term structural volatility that is more stable across rebalancing periods
2. Weight composite: 40% 1-month IVOL (Ang et al.) + 40% 3-year weekly vol (Blitz/van Vliet) + 20% Beta = new composite
3. The anti-bubble property of low-vol (underperforms in strong bull, outperforms in bear) suggests increasing low-vol tilt when macro regime turns defensive — perfectly aligning with STR_011's existing macro gating framework
4. Monthly rebalancing of the factor SIGNAL (even if portfolio rebalances quarterly) reduces signal staleness

## Notes
- Journal of Portfolio Management, Fall 2007, Vol. 34, No. 1, pp. 102-113
- Authors: David Blitz (Robeco) and Pim van Vliet (Robeco)
- This paper is the standard reference for the "simple ranking" approach to LowVol investing
- The 3-year weekly vol lookback is a practitioner-friendly specification that avoids over-trading
- The paper predates the IVOL puzzle discussion and uses total return vol, not residual vol — complementary to Ang et al. (2006)
- Equal-weighting the decile portfolios amplifies the signal (small stocks benefit more from vol sorting) vs. Ang et al.'s value-weighting
