# P118 — Pontiff & Singla (2019): Liquidity Risk?

**Citation:** Pontiff, J., & Singla, R. (2019). Liquidity risk? *Critical Finance Review*, 8(1-2), 223–275. (First draft: February 5, 2019)

---

## Core Thesis

The second replication paper commissioned by Critical Finance Review (alongside Li-Novy-Marx-Velikov). Successfully replicates the PS (2003) gamma index with near-perfect fidelity (correlation = 1.0 up to 5 significant digits). **In-sample results confirm the PS premium** (3.91% vs. PS's original 4.15% — difference attributable to CRSP data revisions). However, various modifications intended to strengthen the gamma index all **fail to produce significant premia**, leading the authors to question whether the PS gamma truly measures liquidity risk.

Key distinction from Li-Novy-Marx-Velikov: Pontiff-Singla are **more skeptical** about what gamma actually measures, invoking the "asymmetric information" interpretation (Kyle 1985) under which γ ≠ 0 would represent a trading opportunity rather than compensation for liquidity risk.

---

## Factor Construction

**PS gamma replication:**
    r^e_{i,d+1,t} = θ_{i,t} + Φ_{i,t} * r_{i,d,t} + γ_{i,t} * sign(r^e_{i,d,t}) * v_{i,d,t} + ε_{i,d+1,t}

All filters identical to PS:
- NYSE/AMEX only (no NASDAQ — volume conventions differ)
- Price: $5 ≤ price ≤ $1,000
- Non-zero volume only (v_{i,d,t} > 0)
- ≥ 16 observations per stock-month
- Special exception: September 2001 (≥ 11 obs accepted due to market closure)

**Aggregate index construction:**

    Δγ̂_t = (m_t / m_1) * (1/N_t) * Σ_i (γ̂_{i,t} - γ̂_{i,t-1})

where m_t = market cap of index stocks, m_1 = market cap in August 1962 (base period).

**Replication correlation:** r = 1.000 (to 5 significant digits) with PS's posted series.

**Four modifications tested (all intended to increase power):**
1. Include days with zero trading volume (v = 0 allowed)
2. Include all price levels (stocks below $5 and above $1,000)
3. Value-weighted index instead of equal-weighted
4. Zero-intercept restriction in gamma regression

**All four modifications yield LOWER premia** and none is significant at 5% level.

**Five alternative liquidity measures tested:**
1. Proportion of zero returns (Lesmond et al. 1999)
2. Amihud ILLIQ
3. Estimated relative bid-ask spread (Corwin-Schultz)
4. Hybrid measure (combination)
5. Double variation of PS original

**Result:** None of the 10 specifications (4 modifications + 5 alternatives + 1 PS-zero-intercept) produces a statistically significant risk premium.

## Key Results

**In-sample replication (1968-1999):**
| Version | Liquidity Premium | t-stat |
|---|---|---|
| PS original (from website) | 4.15%/year | 2.08 |
| Pontiff-Singla replication | 3.91%/year | 2.00 |
| Difference | 0.24% | — |

**Post-sample (2000-2017):**
| Period | PS gamma premium | t-stat |
|---|---|---|
| PS original (their data) | 4.45%/year | 2.67 |
| Pre-sample (1932-1967) | **-2.72%/year** | -1.36 |
| Full sample (1932-2017) | positive but **insignificant** | — |

**Gamma critique:**
- In Kyle (1985) and Glosten-Milgrom (1985): γ = 0 in equilibrium (asymmetric information models predict CONTEMPORANEOUS, not LAGGED, return-volume relation)
- Negative γ in dealer inventory models: if γ < 0, it implies price reversal after volume — which is a trading opportunity in asymmetric information framework, not a risk
- PS gamma may be capturing a **market inefficiency** (short-term price pressure reversal) rather than a systemic liquidity risk factor
- This interpretation aligns with short-term reversal literature (Lehman 1990, Jegadeesh 1990) rather than liquidity risk literature

---

## Korea-Specific Implications

- The pre-1962 evidence (-2.72%/year) is highly cautionary: the PS premium may be period-specific
- Korean market has no pre-1997 deep bear market analog to US pre-1962 — limited ability to test out-of-sample validity
- The zero-intercept critique is especially relevant for Korean implementation: Korean stocks have different intercept structures due to price limits and thinner markets
- For Korean implementation: use PS gamma as ONE of many signals, not as a standalone liquidity risk factor
- The "10 of 10 failures" finding (no specification produces significant premium) is sobering but partly reflects Pontiff-Singla's choice of modifications

  # Δγ̂_t = (m_t/m_1) * (1/N_t) * Σ_i (γ̂_{i,t} - γ̂_{i,t-1})

**Verdict:** Pontiff-Singla argue more strongly than LNV that PS liquidity risk may not exist as a generalizable phenomenon. The 10-of-10 failure is a strong result. However, PS's own response (P116) provides compelling counter-arguments. Use PS gamma as a weak regime signal, not a primary return predictor.
