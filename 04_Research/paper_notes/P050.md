# P050 — Lev & Nissim (2004)

**File:** accrual2.pdf
**Citation:** Lev, B., Nissim, D. (2004). The persistence of the accruals anomaly. Working paper, April 2004. (Published in *Contemporary Accounting Research*, 2006)

---

## Core Claim

The accruals anomaly did not decline between 1965 and 2002 despite growing institutional awareness. The anomaly persists because: (1) institutional investors react to accrual information promptly but only weakly; (2) transient institutions (who react most) hold only ~20% of total institutional ownership; and (3) firms with extreme accruals have characteristics (small size, low price, low book-to-market) that institutional investors systematically avoid due to regulatory and reputational constraints.

---

## Factor Definition

Two measures used (both from Sloan 1996 framework):

**Balance Sheet Accruals (BS_ACC):**
```
BS_ACC = [(ΔCA − ΔCash) − (ΔCL − ΔSTD − ΔITP) − Dep] / AvgTA
```

**Cash-Flow Statement Accruals (CFS_ACC):**
```
CFS_ACC = (EBXI − CFO) / AvgTA
```
Where:
- EBXI = Earnings before extraordinary items
- CFO = Cash from operations (SFAS 95, post-1988)
- AvgTA = Average total assets

Institutional trading measure:
- Quarterly ownership data from 13-F filings
- Institutions classified as: transient, quasi-indexer, dedicated (Bushee 1998 classification)
- Abnormal institutional trading = change in institutional ownership around accrual formation

---

## Key Empirical Results

- Sample: 1965–2002, CRSP/Compustat
- Annual abnormal return to accrual hedge portfolio: **7.5–8.9%** throughout the period — no time trend decline
- Institutions react to accruals in the *same* quarter as formation (prompt) but with weak magnitude
- Transient institutions show significant negative correlation with accruals (sell high-accrual stocks) but hold only ~20% of total institutional ownership
- Quasi-indexers (largest institutional group) show essentially zero response to accruals
- High-accrual firms have:
  - Small market cap (institutional preference for large cap)
  - Low price (institutional avoidance)
  - Low book-to-market (growth firms — institutions prefer)
  - High idiosyncratic volatility (Mashruwala et al. 2006)
  - High transaction costs (bid-ask spread)
- Transaction cost estimates: ~4–6% round-trip for extreme accrual stocks → nearly eliminates individual investor profits
- The anomaly is thus a "rational" equilibrium from institutional investors' perspective

---

## Korea Applicability

- **High conceptual relevance** — explains WHY the anomaly persists in Korea despite being documented in Korean-market papers (P046, Leippold & Lohre 2012)
- Korean institutional landscape is dominated by quasi-indexing behaviors (NPS, Samsung Asset, etc.) — the same pattern as US quasi-indexers not reacting to accruals
- Korean retail investor dominance (higher than US) means more unsophisticated traders who ignore accrual signals
- Small-cap accrual effect may be concentrated in KOSDAQ stocks, which have higher transaction costs and lower institutional ownership — persistence mechanism is strong in Korea
- Expected anomaly strength: 40–60% of US (3–5% annual) but persistence mechanisms are even stronger

## Actionable Strategy Ideas

1. **Institutional Ownership Interaction Signal:** Interact accrual rank with institutional ownership level. Go long low-accrual stocks with LOW institutional ownership (most mispriced, least arbitraged). This is a directly implementable refinement.

```r
# Accrual × Institutional ownership interaction
# Requires: ownership data (KRX or KIS data)
# Low accrual + low institutional ownership = most attractive
accrual_inst_signal <- stocks %>%
  mutate(
    acc_rank = rank(BS_ACC) / n(),  # 0=best, 1=worst
    inst_rank = rank(inst_ownership) / n(),
    # Combined signal: low accrual AND low inst ownership
    combo_score = acc_rank * (1 - inst_rank)
  )
```

2. **Size-Conditional Accrual Portfolio:** Given that small-cap, low-price, low-BM firms drive the anomaly, construct accrual portfolio that overweights these characteristics. In Korea: focus on KOSDAQ universe for long positions.

3. **Persistence Verification Test:** Verify that the Korean accrual anomaly has not declined since Leippold & Lohre (2012) documented it. Run rolling 5-year alpha tests to check for decay — if no decay, the institutional impediment explanation applies in Korea too.

## Notes

- This paper is primarily a "why it persists" paper rather than a "new factor definition" paper
- No new factor definition beyond Sloan (1996) — same BS_ACC and CFS_ACC measures
- Key insight for Korea: do NOT expect the anomaly to be arbitraged away quickly; institutional structure in Korea is even less responsive than US
- The transaction cost argument is especially relevant for KOSDAQ small-caps
- **Feasibility: High** (same factor as STR_012; institutional ownership data needed for interaction test)
- **Novelty vs STR_012: Low** (same factor, but interaction with institutional ownership is new)
