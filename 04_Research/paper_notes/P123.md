# P123: Pursuing the Low Volatility Equity Anomaly: Strategic Allocation or Active Decision? (Knutzen 2013)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Low_Volatility
**Relevance to STR_011 improvement:** Medium

## Core Claim
Low-volatility equity investing constitutes a genuine anomaly — low-risk stocks have historically delivered equal or superior returns to high-risk stocks. The paper evaluates whether LowVol should be treated as a strategic (permanent) allocation or as an active (tactical) decision, and whether the anomaly will persist given increased institutional awareness.

## Factor Construction (exact formula if different from basic idio-vol)
- Sorting variable: realized volatility (total return volatility, lookback unspecified in intro pages)
- Portfolio construction: decile/quintile sorting, value-weighted or equally-weighted portfolios
- Key comparison: low-vol quintile vs. high-vol quintile, long-horizon compound returns

## Key Results
- The anomaly is documented as robust across multiple time periods and market environments
- Strategic allocation to low-vol is justified even under conservative return assumptions (Carvalho, Xiao & Moulon 2012 referenced: min-var provides the greatest Sharpe ratio among cap-weight alternatives)
- Key debate: is low-vol a risk premium (systematic, persistent) or a behavioral mispricing (potentially arbitrageable)?
- Paper argues for a STRATEGIC allocation component given persistence of causes (benchmark constraints, agency issues, behavioral biases)
- Increased institutional adoption noted but not sufficient to eliminate the premium

## STR_011 Improvement Insights
- CAGR improvement: The strategic framing supports permanent LowVol exposure regardless of macro regime — suggests STR_011's macro gating may be unnecessarily reducing exposure during low-vol's underperformance phases
- MDD improvement: Strategic LowVol allocation provides structural drawdown protection; combining with macro gating provides layered defense
- Sharpe improvement: The paper's argument for permanence of the anomaly supports maintaining LowVol exposure as a core holding rather than a satellite tactical bet

## Korea Applicability
- Paper covers developed markets primarily; Korea context not specifically mentioned
- Agency issues and benchmark mandates are equally present in Korean institutional investor community
- Korean fund managers face similar benchmark constraints (vs. KOSPI200/IKS200), supporting persistence of LowVol in Korea

## Actionable Modifications for STR_011
1. Reconsider fully shutting off LowVol in risk-on regimes — instead reduce position sizing by 30-50% rather than 100% exit
2. The strategic framing suggests LowVol should always have a minimum floor allocation (e.g., 20% of portfolio), with macro regime determining the tilt magnitude
