# Paper Note: P188 — A Half Century of Macro Momentum (2017)

**Authors:** Mariani, Mattu, Moore, Polbennikov (PIMCO / others)
**Source:** Journal of Portfolio Management / SSRN
**Category:** Asset_Allocation
**Priority:** 1

## Core Thesis

Macro momentum — directional signals constructed from macroeconomic variables — has generated persistent risk-adjusted excess returns across a half century of data (1965-2015). Four macro themes (Business Cycle, International Trade, Monetary Policy, Risk Sentiment) each independently predict asset returns with positive Sharpe ratios. Macro momentum is distinct from, and complementary to, pure price momentum.

## Strategy Description

**Signal Construction:**
- 4 macro themes, each composed of multiple series:
  1. **Business Cycle**: GDP growth forecasts, CPI forecasts (directional change)
  2. **International Trade**: FX rate changes, trade balance directional signals
  3. **Monetary Policy**: Front-end yield curve slope changes, central bank rate signals
  4. **Risk Sentiment**: Equity excess returns, credit spread direction
- Each theme generates a directional portfolio signal (long/short or 0/1)
- Signals are combined with equal weighting across 4 themes
- Portfolio targets 10% annualized volatility via position scaling
- Monthly rebalancing

**Universe:** Global bonds, equities, currencies, commodities
**Signal Evaluation Period:** Monthly
**Lookback:** 1-3 months for macro data direction

**Performance (Full Sample 1965-2015):**
- Composite macro momentum: SR ~0.6-0.8 depending on specification
- Business Cycle theme: SR ~0.5
- Monetary Policy theme: SR ~0.6 (strongest individual theme)
- Low correlation between themes: diversification benefit is substantial
- Positive performance in inflationary and deflationary regimes
- Consistent Sharpe ratios across sub-periods

## Key Results

- All 4 macro themes show positive Sharpe ratios individually
- Combined 4-theme portfolio: SR significantly higher than individual themes
- Theme correlations typically below 0.3 → genuine diversification
- Macro momentum is distinct from: (1) price momentum, (2) value/carry, (3) volatility strategies
- Performance persists in out-of-sample tests
- Particularly strong: Monetary Policy theme (yield curve direction)
- Business Cycle theme captures recession/expansion timing
