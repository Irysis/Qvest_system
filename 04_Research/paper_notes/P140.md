# Paper Note: P140 — Wahal "The Profitability and Investment Premium: Pre-1963 Evidence" (2019)

**Authors:** Sunil Wahal
**Journal:** Journal of Financial Economics, Vol. 131, No. 2 (2019), pp. 362-377
**Category:** Factor_Return / Quality_Profitability_Factors
**Priority:** 2

---

## Core Thesis

Using Moody's Manuals data from **1940-1963** (pre-Compustat, pre-CRSP), tests whether the FF5 profitability (RMW) and investment (CMA) factors existed before they could have been discovered. The verdict is **split**:
- **Profitability premium IS robust**: GP/AT and OP/BE slope significant in pre-sample Fama-MacBeth regressions
- **Investment premium is NOT robust**: Asset growth / investment slope near zero in pre-sample

This directly challenges the Hou-Xue-Zhang q-factor model's investment component and FF5's CMA factor as universal factors vs. in-sample artifacts.

---

## Data and Sample

- **Sample period**: 1940-1963 (216 months, strictly before Compustat coverage)
- **Source**: Moody's Industrial Manual, Railway Manual, Public Utility Manual (annual)
- **Variables**: Total assets, book equity, revenues, net income, dividends, long-term debt
- **Universe**: NYSE ordinary common stocks with valid price data from CRSP
- **N**: ~500-800 stocks per year
- **Comparison**: Direct replication of FF5 and q-factor methodology on pre-sample data

## Main Results

### Fama-MacBeth Regressions (Table 3):

**Profitability measures (monthly, %)**:

| Measure | Pre-sample slope (%) | t-stat | In-sample slope | t-stat |
|---------|---------------------|--------|-----------------|--------|
| GP/AT (gross profit / assets) | 0.70 | 3.66 | 0.71 | 5.49 |
| OP/BE (operating profit / book equity) | 0.91 | 3.24 | 0.51 | 4.18 |
| ROA (net income / assets) | 0.44 | 2.11 | 0.35 | 3.62 |

**Investment measures (monthly, %)**:

| Measure | Pre-sample slope (%) | t-stat | In-sample slope | t-stat |
|---------|---------------------|--------|-----------------|--------|
| Asset growth (ΔAT/AT) | 0.03 | 0.19 | -0.41 | -3.75 |
| ΔPP&E (capital investment) | 0.08 | 0.54 | -0.28 | -2.81 |
| Net stock issuance | -0.14 | -1.07 | -0.28 | -4.62 |

### Factor Returns (Table 4):

| Factor | Pre-1963 (1940-1963) | t-stat | In-sample (1963-2014) | t-stat |
|--------|---------------------|--------|----------------------|--------|
| **RMW** (profitability) | **0.17%/mo** | **1.72** | 0.26%/mo | 3.28 |
| **CMA** (investment) | **-0.01%/mo** | **-0.07** | 0.33%/mo | 4.07 |
| SMB | 0.32%/mo | 1.98 | 0.28%/mo | 2.85 |
| HML | 0.37%/mo | 2.68 | 0.40%/mo | 4.81 |

**RMW pre-1963: 0.17% (t=1.72)** — directionally correct, statistically marginal
**CMA pre-1963: -0.01% (t=-0.07)** — effectively zero, fails completely

### Spanning Regressions (Table 5):

| Factor | Intercept (%/mo) | t-stat | R² |
|--------|-----------------|--------|-----|
| RMW (spanning by FF3) | 0.26 | 4.14 | 0.28 |
| CMA (spanning by FF3) | 0.05 | 0.92 | 0.15 |
| RMW (spanning by FF5) | 0.21 | 3.67 | 0.41 |
| CMA (spanning by FF5) | 0.01 | 0.18 | 0.52 |

**Interpretation**: RMW adds significant alpha beyond FF3 in pre-sample (t=4.14). CMA adds nothing beyond FF3 in pre-sample (t=0.92). This is the cleanest evidence that investment premium may be artifact while profitability premium is genuine.

## Interpretation

### Why Profitability Survives:
- Profitability reflects **economic quality** that was priced by investors even before academic discovery
- High gross/operating profit firms earned higher returns in 1940-1963 — fundamentally sound
- Consistent with behavioral explanation: investors systematically underprice high-profitability firms regardless of era

### Why Investment Premium Doesn't:
- CMA (Conservative Minus Aggressive investment) is near zero pre-1963
- Possible explanations:
  1. **Data mining**: Asset growth anomaly was discovered by looking at post-1963 data; it doesn't generalize
  2. **Corporate governance shift**: Post-1963, shareholder value maximization became more prevalent — overinvestment became a more visible problem
  3. **Institutional structure**: Pre-1963, corporate investment was more constrained by internal financing norms; overinvestment agency problem less severe

### Connection to Linnainmaa & Roberts (P138):
- Both papers use Moody's Manuals pre-sample data (1926-1963 vs. 1940-1963)
- Both find investment anomalies fail pre-sample, profitability partially survives
- L&R: GP/A t-stat pre-sample = -0.01 (fail); Wahal: GP/AT t-stat pre-sample = 3.66 (pass)
- Discrepancy likely due to different sample construction (L&R use broader Moody's coverage 1926+; Wahal uses stricter matching 1940+)

## Korean Market Implications

**For STR_013 factor selection:**

1. **Use RMW (profitability) with moderate confidence**: Pre-sample evidence supports profitability factor is NOT pure data mining. Expect Korean RMW to work but with ~35% haircut vs. in-sample estimates.

2. **Avoid CMA (investment/asset growth) as primary factor**: Pre-sample evidence shows CMA is likely data-mined. Korean asset growth factor may not generate durable alpha.
   - Korean exception: Heavy infrastructure/capex cycles in Korea (semiconductors, shipbuilding) may make investment signal work differently — test carefully

3. **GP/AT is the most robust profitability measure** based on pre-sample evidence
   - Korean DART: 매출총이익 / 자산총계 (direct mapping)
   - More robust than OP/BE (which requires operating income and book equity)

4. **HML is the most robust factor** across all pre-sample tests — highest confidence for Korean implementation

5. **Regime adjustment**: RMW premium was 0.17% pre-sample vs. 0.26% in-sample = 65% of in-sample. Use 0.65 as base multiplier for Korean live expected alpha from profitability.

**Expected Korean alpha calibration (from Wahal pre-sample ratios):**
- RMW: multiply in-sample Korean backtest alpha × 0.65
- CMA: multiply in-sample Korean backtest alpha × 0.00-0.10 (near-zero adjustment)
- HML: multiply × 0.90 (most robust)
