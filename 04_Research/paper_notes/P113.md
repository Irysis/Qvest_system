# P113 — Datar, Naik & Radcliffe (1998): Liquidity and Stock Returns: An Alternative Test

**Citation:** Datar, V.T., Naik, N.Y., & Radcliffe, R. (1998). Liquidity and stock returns: An alternative test. *Journal of Financial Markets*, 1(2), 203–219.

---

## Core Thesis

Tests whether liquidity is priced in the cross-section of stock returns using **share turnover** (volume / shares outstanding) as the liquidity proxy — avoiding the need for microstructure (bid-ask spread) data. Provides a clean, large-sample confirmation of the Amihud-Mendelson (1986) hypothesis: less liquid stocks (lower turnover) earn significantly higher returns. The relationship is robust, has no January seasonality, and is independent of size, B/M, and beta.

**Key methodological advantage:** Turnover rate is computable from basic CRSP data (no need for transactions data or specialist data), making the test accessible and replicable.

---

## Factor Construction

**Liquidity measure:** Monthly turnover rate

    TURN_{i,m} = Share volume_{i,m} / Shares outstanding_{i,m}

where volume and shares outstanding are monthly averages.

Higher TURN = MORE liquid = LOWER expected return
Lower TURN = LESS liquid = HIGHER expected return

**Data:** NYSE stocks 1962–1991; ~880 stocks per month; Fama-MacBeth cross-sectional regressions.

**Controls:** Beta (CAPM), ln(size), B/M ratio, dividend yield

**Regression specification:**
    R_{i,m+1} = a + b₁*TURN_{i,m} + b₂*ln(SIZE) + b₃*B/M + b₄*BETA + ε

Monthly Fama-MacBeth: estimate cross-sectional coefficients each month, then average.

---

## Key Results

| Specification | TURN coefficient | t-statistic |
|---|---|---|
| Univariate | -0.040 | -8.86 |
| + Size | -0.046 | -10.58 |
| + Size + B/M | -0.044 | -10.21 |
| + Size + B/M + Beta | -0.043 | -9.97 |

- Turnover coefficient consistently **negative** (t ≈ -9 to -11): low turnover → high returns
- Coefficient of -0.043 means 1% increase in monthly turnover → 4.3 bps lower next month return
- Effect persists in ALL months including January (unlike Eleswarapu-Reinganum 1993 for bid-ask spread, which only found January effect)
- No January seasonality confirms this is a genuine year-round premium, not a tax-loss selling artifact
- Result: turnover captures illiquidity compensation distinct from size, value, and systematic risk

## Korea-Specific Implications

- Monthly turnover is directly computable from RAWDATA.parquet (daily volume / shares_outstanding, summed monthly)
- Korean KOSDAQ has notoriously high turnover (monthly turnover 50-200% for popular stocks) → Korean retail "gambling" stocks have HIGH turnover → predict LOWER returns
- The no-January-seasonality finding may not hold in Korea where year-end tax effects are smaller than in the US
- Korean institutional investors tend to have lower turnover (longer holding periods) and often hold less popular stocks → this should reinforce the premium

**Korean market calibration:**
- KOSPI200 average monthly turnover: ~5-15%
- KOSDAQ150 average monthly turnover: ~20-80%
- Expected premium: stocks with monthly turnover < 5th percentile vs. > 95th percentile

## Actionable Implementation

**Most directly implementable measure in the liquidity cluster:**

```r
# Monthly turnover computation from RAWDATA.parquet
compute_monthly_turnover <- function(daily_data) {
  daily_data %>%
    group_by(ticker, ym = format(date, "%Y-%m")) %>%
    summarise(
      monthly_volume = sum(volume, na.rm = TRUE),
      avg_shares_out = mean(shares_outstanding, na.rm = TRUE),
      n_days = sum(!is.na(volume) & volume > 0),
      .groups = "drop"
    ) %>%
    mutate(
      monthly_turnover = monthly_volume / avg_shares_out,
      # Winsorize at 1% and 99% to handle outliers
      monthly_turnover_w = pmin(
        pmax(monthly_turnover, quantile(monthly_turnover, 0.01, na.rm = TRUE)),
        quantile(monthly_turnover, 0.99, na.rm = TRUE)
      )
    )
}

# Factor signal: low turnover = bullish (illiquidity premium)
# Use as negative signal: lower turnover rank → higher expected return
turnover_signal <- function(df) {
  df %>%
    group_by(date) %>%
    mutate(
      # Invert: high rank = low turnover = bullish
      illiquidity_score = 1 - percent_rank(monthly_turnover_w)
    )
}
```

**Best use in composite:** Monthly turnover is the most practical liquidity signal for RAWDATA.parquet. Use alongside ILLIQ for a two-measure liquidity composite. Coefficient of -0.043 per unit of monthly turnover provides calibration for signal weighting.
