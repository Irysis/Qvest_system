# P172: How Dumb Is Smart Beta? Analyzing the Growth of Fundamental Indexing — Dubil (2015)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Analyzes US smart beta ETFs (14 funds, 2003–2014) using Fama-French 4-factor decomposition. Key finding: "The claim that smart beta funds are theoretically optimal and therefore superior to cap-weighted funds is not supported by theory. It is the result of statistical experiments." Table 2 (annualized stats): Best performing equal-weight RSP 5-year Sharpe = 1.46. Table 3 shows risk factor loadings — most smart beta ETFs have market betas > 1.0 and significant SMB loadings. Table 4 (return attribution): More than half of pure style (RPG, RPV) excess returns explained by market and size factors; dividend strategies have unexplained alpha components driven by HML and momentum. The "claim to theoretical optimality" fails for fundamental indexation.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
Korean fundamental index products (dividend-weighted, book-value-weighted ETFs) face the same risk: their outperformance is largely explained by size tilt and value tilt, not by fundamental weighting superiority. The Dubil finding that dividend yield strategies have "unexplained" alpha components (Table 4: DVY unexplained = 0.29%, VYM = 0.03%) suggests that dividend strategies in Korea may have genuine quality-based alpha — worth investigating using DART dividend coverage and payout stability data.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. Apply the Dubil 4-factor attribution to every Korean strategy: decompose return into Rm-Rf, SMB, HML, and MOM components; require that unexplained "alpha" is positive and significant before claiming genuine outperformance.
2. The finding that dividend growth (VIG, 7-year Sharpe 0.41, beta 0.83) offers lower market risk than dividend yield (DVY, 7-year Sharpe 0.16, beta 0.69 but poor performance) suggests preferring DART-based dividend growth screens over simple yield screens in the Korean value composite.
