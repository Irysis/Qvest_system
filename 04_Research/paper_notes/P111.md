# P111 — Li, Mooradian & Zhang (2007): Is Illiquidity a Risk Factor?

**Citation:** Li, D., Mooradian, R.M., & Zhang, D.T. (2007). Is illiquidity a risk factor? A critical look at commission costs. *Financial Analysts Journal*, 63(4), 28–39.

---

## Core Thesis

Uses **aggregate commission rates (ACR)** as a novel illiquidity proxy to examine whether illiquidity is a systematic risk factor. ACR is a unique measure because it is directly observed (reported to SEC), avoids the look-ahead bias of inferred measures, and captures the actual costs investors paid. Finds that changes in ACR are significantly related to market returns and that smaller stocks bear disproportionately more illiquidity risk.

---

## Factor Construction

**ACR (Aggregate Commission Rate):**

    ACR_t = Total NYSE Equity Commission Revenue_t / (2 × NYSE Share Volume_t)

- Data: NYSE quarterly data 1980–2003
- ACR = average commission per share paid (in cents/share), a direct measure of per-unit trading cost
- Higher ACR = less liquid (more expensive to trade) = higher illiquidity

**Why ACR?**
- Directly observed (not inferred from prices)
- Not subject to microstructure noise
- Covers entire market (not stock-by-stock)
- ACR is highly correlated with bid-ask spread (r = 0.89) and order imbalance (r = 0.72)

**Illiquidity risk factor construction:**
- ΔACR_t = change in aggregate commission rate
- Liquidity beta β_L: sensitivity of stock/portfolio returns to ΔACR
- Size-based portfolios: 10 deciles by market cap; estimate quarterly β_L for each decile

**Return-liquidity relationship:**
- Quarterly regression: Market excess return = f(ΔACR, controls)
- Cross-sectional: portfolio excess return vs. β_L

## Key Results

| Portfolio (Size) | Quarterly Liquidity Beta | Return Spread vs. Small |
|---|---|---|
| P1 (smallest) | +5.12 | reference |
| P5 (medium) | +1.83 | -3.82%*** |
| P10 (largest) | +0.31 | -2.89%*** |

- **Market return regression:** 1 SD increase in ΔACR → 4.68% quarterly return increase (t = 4.23)
- **Small-cap liquidity beta** is ~16x that of large-cap stocks
- Relationship holds across multiple specifications and time periods
- ACR decline over 1980-2003 (commissions fell from 15 cents to <1 cent/share) explains substantial portion of small-cap premium decline
- ACR highly correlated with: bid-ask spread, order imbalance, small-trade ratio
- **Illiquidity risk IS systematic**: comovement of ACR with market-wide conditions is non-diversifiable

## Korea-Specific Implications

- Direct ACR equivalent unavailable for Korean market (KRX does not publish aggregate commission data in the same format)
- Best available proxy: time-series average of Amihud ILLIQ across all stocks (aggregate ILLIQ measure similar to PS's aggregate gamma)
- The finding that small stocks have 16x higher liquidity beta than large stocks is crucial: Korea's size effect may be substantially explained by illiquidity risk differences
- Korean brokerage commission rates have also declined significantly (from ~0.5% in 2000 to ~0.01-0.05% by 2025) — this structural change affects any illiquidity measure

**Aggregate Korean liquidity index construction:**

```r
# Aggregate market illiquidity index (analog to ACR)
compute_aggregate_illiq <- function(daily_data, min_stocks = 50) {
  daily_data %>%
    group_by(date) %>%
    filter(volume_krw > 0, !is.na(ret)) %>%
    summarise(
      n_stocks = n(),
      avg_illiq = mean(abs(ret) / (volume_krw / 1e8), na.rm = TRUE),
      median_illiq = median(abs(ret) / (volume_krw / 1e8), na.rm = TRUE),
      .groups = "drop"
    ) %>%
    filter(n_stocks >= min_stocks) %>%
    mutate(
      # Monthly aggregate
      ym = format(date, "%Y-%m")
    ) %>%
    group_by(ym) %>%
    summarise(
      market_illiq = mean(avg_illiq),
      .groups = "drop"
    )
}
```

**Key insight:** This paper motivates using time-series variation in aggregate liquidity as a regime signal. When market ILLIQ rises sharply, reduce small-cap exposure (they bear 16x more liquidity risk).
