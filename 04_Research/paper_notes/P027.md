# P027: Representativeness Heuristic — Kahneman & Tversky (1973)

## Core Finding
Kahneman & Tversky (1973) document the representativeness heuristic: people judge the probability of an event by how closely it resembles the typical features of a category, ignoring base rates and sample size. Classic demonstrations: "Linda the bank teller" (conjunction fallacy — Linda is more likely described as feminist bank teller than bank teller alone despite logical impossibility); gambler's fallacy (expecting HTHTTH to be more probable than HHHHHH); overestimation of small samples representing population properties.

## Key Metrics
- Alpha/Return: N/A (cognitive psychology, Psychological Review 1973)
- Statistical strength: Large-scale experimental studies with thousands of subjects
- Sample: Israeli university students, Oregon Research Institute

## Korea Applicability
HIGH — Representativeness explains why Korean investors overweight recent earnings trends as representative of future earnings: three consecutive positive earnings surprises makes the company "look like" a growth company, causing overreaction. Post-earnings-announcement drift and subsequent mean-reversion are partly driven by this heuristic.

## Strategy Implication
Representativeness creates two signals: (1) EARNINGS STREAK bias: companies with 3+ consecutive positive earnings surprises are perceived as permanently improved quality — this creates overvaluation. Signal: earnings_streak_count = consecutive quarters of EPS beat. After 3+ beats, mean-reversion risk increases. Short high-streak names with declining sales growth. (2) BASE RATE NEGLECT for growth: high-P/E "growth" stocks are priced by representativeness (they look like growth companies) rather than base rates (most growth companies revert to mean ROIC). Short extreme P/E names where implied growth rates exceed historical base rates for their industry.
