# P026: Regret Theory (1982)

## Core Finding
Loomes & Sugden (1982) independently develop Regret Theory parallel to Bell (1982). The theory modifies expected utility by adding a regret/rejoicing term that depends on the comparison between chosen and unchosen outcomes. Key prediction: preference reversals and violations of transitivity can arise rationally under regret theory — choices are not transitive when regret is considered, resolving apparent paradoxes in expected utility theory.

## Key Metrics
- Alpha/Return: N/A (decision theory)
- Statistical strength: Analytical theoretical paper with experimental predictions
- Sample: Economic Journal, 1982

## Korea Applicability
MEDIUM — The intransitivity of regret-based preferences explains some patterns in Korean market cycles: investors who regret buying at a peak may form different preferences in the next cycle, creating non-stationary return patterns.

## Strategy Implication
Regret theory (like Bell 1982) supports benchmark-relative portfolio construction. For a Korean KOSPI200-benchmarked portfolio: ex-post regret is minimized by staying close to benchmark. BUT: investors with absolute return mandates (no benchmark) should not exhibit regret-based herding. Regime-dependent factor: in bull markets (low regret environment), investors take larger active positions; in bear markets (high regret), revert to benchmark. Use VIX/market trend as regime indicator to modulate active risk budget. High regret regime = reduce active factor bets and move toward factor-neutral.
