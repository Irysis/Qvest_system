# P114 — Kyle & Obizhaeva (2018): The Market Impact Puzzle

**Citation:** Kyle, A.S., & Obizhaeva, A.A. (2018). The market impact puzzle. Working paper (SSRN 3124502). First draft: February 4, 2018.

---

## Core Thesis

Reviews two complementary approaches to deriving a **universal market impact formula** applicable to all assets:
1. **Meta-model approach** (economic restrictions): uses volume, volatility, and invariance to constrain the functional form
2. **Dimensional analysis approach** (physics-inspired): applies leverage neutrality and microstructure invariance

Both approaches converge on the same general formula:

    G ≈ (σ²/PV)^((1+β)/3) * |PQ|^β

where the **square root model** (β = 1/2) is the knife-edge special case where all unknown distributional parameters cancel out (only σ and V are needed).

The paper shows that for β ≠ 1/2, additional information about order flow composition (number of bets γ, average bet size E{|Q|}) is needed — making the square root model uniquely tractable empirically.

---

## Factor Construction

**Meta-model system of equations:**
- Volume equation: V = γ * E{|Q|} (volume = frequency × average size)
- Volatility equation: σ² = γ * E{G²} (variance = frequency × squared impact)
- Market impact equation: E{G²} = α² * E{|Q|^{2β}} (mean squared impact)
- Transaction cost invariance: C = α * P * E{|Q|^{1+β}} = constant

**Solution for market impact formula:**

    G = (m_β/m)^(C^{1-2β}/3) * (σ²/PV)^{(1+β)/3} * |PQ|^β     ... (equation 23)

**Special cases:**
- β = 0: bid-ask spread model G₀ = σ/√γ (fixed cost, relevant for retail traders)
- β = 1: linear impact model G₁ = |Q|/V * σ√(P E{|Q|}) (institutional, theory models)
- β = 1/2: square root model G_{1/2} = σ√(|Q|/V) (empirically most robust)

**Invariance constant C ≈ $2,000**: expected dollar cost per institutional bet, approximately constant across all assets in US equity markets.

**Key finding:** For the square root model (β = 1/2), the exponent of γ (number of bets per day) is exactly zero — which is why only V and σ are needed. This mathematical cancellation is why the square root model is "knife-edged."

---

## Key Results

- Square root model G = σ√(|Q|/V) is self-consistent without invariance assumptions
- For β ≠ 1/2 (e.g., empirical finding of β ≈ 0.60 by Almgren et al. 2005), additional microstructure information is needed
- **Empirical exponent puzzle:** Theory predicts linear (β = 1), empirics find square root (β = 1/2 to 0.6), econophysics literature finds β ≈ 0.5; the source of this discrepancy is unresolved
- Transaction cost estimates using the formula: for 2% daily volatility, 1M share daily volume, executing 5% of daily volume costs ~45 basis points (from G = 0.02 * √(0.05) ≈ 0.0045)
- Dimensional analysis shows bid-ask spread ∝ σ^(2/3) / (PV)^(1/3) — cube root scaling
- The general formula nests both square root and linear models as special cases

## Korea-Specific Implications

- Square root impact model is the **standard industry model for transaction cost estimation** — Korean institutional investors should use G = σ√(Q/V) for pre-trade analysis
- Korean stocks have smaller daily volumes than US large-caps → expected impacts are higher for same % of ADV
- Price limits in Korea create discontinuities in the impact function: near the ±30% limit, the model breaks down
- For Moltbot's 20-30 stock concentrated portfolio: position sizing should use G = σ√(Q/V) to ensure execution costs don't erode alpha

**Portfolio sizing using square root model:**

```r
# Maximum position size based on acceptable market impact
max_position_size <- function(sigma_daily, adv_shares, max_impact_bps = 20) {
  # sigma_daily: daily return volatility (decimal)
  # adv_shares: average daily volume in shares
  # max_impact_bps: maximum acceptable one-way impact in basis points

  max_impact_decimal <- max_impact_bps / 10000

  # G = sigma * sqrt(Q/V) <= max_impact
  # Q <= V * (max_impact/sigma)^2
  max_Q <- adv_shares * (max_impact_decimal / sigma_daily)^2
  return(max_Q)
}
```
