# P071: Standing Out From the Crowd: Measuring Crowding in Quantitative Strategies (Cahan & Luo 2013)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Crowding
**Feasibility:** medium (securities lending not available in Korea; RMPC feasible)
**Novelty:** high (foundational paper — most-cited crowding measurement framework)

## Core Claim
Factor crowding in quantitative strategies can be measured through two complementary metrics: (1) securities lending utilization spread between the most-attractive and least-attractive factor portfolios, and (2) intraday residual median pairwise correlation (RMPC) of stocks in the factor basket. Both metrics successfully identified the 2007–2008 quant crowding crisis in advance and are applicable to ongoing monitoring.

## Crowding Detection Method (exact metrics if available)

**Metric 1 — Securities Lending Utilization Spread:**
- Source: DataExplorers (now IHS Markit Securities Finance)
- Definition: Utilization(Q10 factor stocks) minus Utilization(Q1 factor stocks)
  - Utilization = shares on loan / shares available to lend (%)
- High spread → more short selling pressure on unattractive stocks = more leveraged long/short factor exposure by quants
- Tested on: E2P (Earnings-to-Price value factor) and PM12 (12-month price momentum)
- Peaked notably in 2006–2007 before quant crisis

**Metric 2 — Residual Median Pairwise Correlation (RMPC):**
- Construction:
  1. Remove market factor from each stock return (residualize against market)
  2. Form factor basket (top quintile by factor score)
  3. Compute all pairwise return correlations within the basket on intraday (30-min) data
  4. RMPC = median of those pairwise correlations
- High RMPC → stocks in the factor basket are moving together more than market explains = crowding
- **For long-only strategies: RMPC is the appropriate primary metric** (no short selling data needed)
- Threshold: RMPC above historical 80th percentile = crowded; above 95th = severely crowded

**Combined signal**: Both metrics showed elevated readings 6–12 months before August 2007 quant crisis.

## Key Results
- Both metrics peaked in 2006–early 2007, signaling rising crowding before the crash
- After quant crisis, both metrics collapsed and recovered slowly (2008–2010)
- RMPC is more timely (intraday data) but requires intraday prices; utilization spread has longer look-ahead
- E2P (value) and PM12 (momentum) showed similar crowding dynamics
- Crowding leads to crash risk: when RMPC is in top decile, forward 1-month factor returns are -2.3% (vs +0.9% in bottom decile)

## Korea Applicability
- **Securities lending data**: KRX publishes daily short sale data (공매도 잔고) — can proxy utilization spread using short balance / float shares for high-score vs low-score stocks
- **RMPC**: Fully feasible with daily (not intraday) data from RAWDATA.parquet
  - Use daily residuals from market regression
  - Compute pairwise correlations within LowVol basket (top quintile)
  - Roll over 21-day window, take median
- **Korea short sale constraint**: Limited short selling means utilization spread may be structurally lower; RMPC is better primary metric
- **Data lag**: KRX short balance data available T+1 publicly; DART quarterly ownership adds additional info
