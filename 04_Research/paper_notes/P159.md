# P159: Timing Smart Beta — Arnott et al., Research Affiliates (2016)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Contrarian factor timing using relative valuation signals works: the three least expensive factors (by current P/B vs. historical P/B) earn 6.1% per year with Sharpe 0.66 over 39.5 years, while the three most expensive earn -1.1% with Sharpe -0.16. Average cross-factor correlation = 0.04, providing strong diversification benefit. Valuation spread (current factor P/B / historical average P/B) is the key timing signal — a 1-SD cheapness score predicts significant outperformance over subsequent 3–5 years.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
Factor valuation data for Korea is sparse and noisy pre-2010. Computing a reliable historical average P/B for Korean factor portfolios requires at minimum 15 years of data, which limits the signal to post-2016 reliability. Additionally, Korean market structure changes (stewardship code 2016, MSCI rebalancing effects) create structural breaks that contaminate long-term historical valuation averages.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. Implement factor valuation z-scores in ensemble_engine.R: compute val_ratio = current_factor_PB / rolling_10yr_avg_factor_PB; z-score this cross-sectionally across all active factors; overweight factors with val_z < -0.5 (cheap), underweight factors with val_z > +0.5 (expensive).
2. Use the macro_regime signals as a gate: only apply factor timing overlay when regime is "normal" (not extreme risk-off); during crisis regimes, default to equal-weight across factors to avoid value traps.
