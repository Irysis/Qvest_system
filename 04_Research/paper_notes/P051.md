# P051 — Bradshaw, Richardson & Sloan (2001)

**File:** accrual3.pdf
**Citation:** Bradshaw, M.T., Richardson, S.A., Sloan, R.G. (2001). Do analysts and auditors use information in accruals? *Journal of Accounting Research*, 39(1), 45–74.

---

## Core Claim

Neither sell-side equity analysts nor external auditors incorporate accrual information into their outputs. Analysts issue systematically optimistic earnings forecasts for high-accrual firms, and auditors do not issue going-concern opinions at higher rates for high-accrual firms. This establishes that the accrual mispricing is not corrected by professional intermediaries — consistent with a genuine market inefficiency rather than a risk premium.

---

## Factor Definition

**Working Capital Accruals (WCAcc) — Cash Flow Based:**
```
WCAcc = -(ΔRECT + ΔINVT + ΔACT_other - ΔAP - ΔITP)
```
Using COMPUSTAT items: #302 (ΔAR), #303 (ΔINV), #304 (ΔOTHER_CA), #305 (ΔAP), #307 (ΔITP)
This is the statement-of-cash-flows derived version.

**Total Accruals (TAcc):**
```
TAcc = EBXI (#123) - TCF (#308)
```
Where:
- EBXI = Earnings before extraordinary items
- TCF = Total cash from operations (SFAS 95)

**Rational persistence coefficient on WCAcc:** 0.624 (from earnings regression)
**Market-implied persistence coefficient:** 0.825 (from returns regression via Mishkin 1983 test)
- The market overestimates WCAcc persistence by 32% (0.825 vs 0.624)

**Analyst Forecast Error:**
```
AFE = (Actual EPS - Forecast EPS) / |Stock Price|
```
Regressed on lagged accruals to measure analyst bias.

---

## Key Empirical Results

- Sample: 1988–1998, 66,762 firm-years
- **Analyst result:** High-accrual firms have significantly MORE optimistic analyst forecasts in subsequent year (positive coefficient on accruals predicting AFE — analysts do NOT adjust downward for high accruals)
- **Auditor result:** Audit opinions (going concern, qualified) are not correlated with high accruals — auditors do not flag high-accrual firms as higher risk
- Mishkin (1983) test: confirms market overestimates accrual persistence; rational coefficient = 0.624, market-implied = 0.825 (LR statistic highly significant)
- Hedge portfolio (long low-accrual, short high-accrual): 14.9% annual size-adjusted return
- Results hold for both cash-flow-statement and balance-sheet accrual measures
- Both analyst and auditor failures hold across all firm size quintiles

## Korea Applicability

- **High conceptual relevance** — Korean sell-side analysts are well-documented to be optimistically biased (sell-side culture in Korea is even more optimistic than US due to relationship banking and underwriting ties)
- Korean auditors face similar independence issues — Big4 dominance, but client relationship incentives
- The analyst-accrual result suggests that in Korea, analyst earnings forecasts for high-accrual firms will be even more optimistic, creating larger earnings disappointments when accruals reverse
- **Potential Korea-specific factor:** Combine accrual rank with analyst forecast revision — high accrual + upward analyst revision = maximum mispricing signal

---

## Actionable Strategy Ideas

1. **Accrual + Analyst Revision Composite Signal:** High accruals + analyst upgrade = double-negative signal (both accruals and analyst are wrong in the same direction). Long stocks where analysts are revising DOWN for low-accrual firms; short stocks where analysts are revising UP for high-accrual firms.

```r
# Accrual × Analyst revision signal
# Uses: accrual from fundamental_dart.parquet
#       analyst revisions from FnGuide or KIS consensus data
accrual_analyst_signal <- stocks %>%
  mutate(
    acc_rank = percent_rank(CFS_ACC),  # 0=low (good), 1=high (bad)
    # Analyst revision rank (positive revision = bad for high-accrual firm)
    rev_rank = percent_rank(eps_revision_3m),
    # Combined: high accrual + positive revision = most overpriced
    overpriced_score = acc_rank * rev_rank,
    # Low accrual + negative revision = most underpriced
    underpriced_score = (1 - acc_rank) * (1 - rev_rank)
  )
```

2. **Earnings Surprise Prediction:** Use WCAcc to predict the magnitude of future analyst earnings surprises. Firms with high accruals that have upward analyst revisions are candidates for negative earnings surprises 1–2 quarters forward.

3. **Auditor-Accrual Non-Signal Exploitation:** In Korea, since auditors do not flag high-accrual firms, there is no "auditor discount" priced into these stocks. The full accrual anomaly should be exploitable without being offset by audit risk premium.
