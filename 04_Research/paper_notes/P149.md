# P149: The Valuation of Risk Assets and the Selection of Risky Investments in Stock Portfolios and Capital Budgets (1965)
**Category:** Portfolio_Theory | **Priority:** 3
**Author:** John Lintner | **Journal:** Review of Economics and Statistics, Vol. 47, No. 1, pp. 13-37

## Core Contribution to Factor Investing
Lintner independently derived the CAPM security market line (SML) and extended Sharpe's framework to handle non-zero covariances between stocks and to the corporate capital budgeting problem. More important for our pipeline: Lintner proved the **Separation Theorem** algebraically — that the optimal proportionate composition of the risky-asset portfolio is independent of the ratio of gross stock investment to total net investment. This is the formal justification for treating market beta as a single-number risk adjustment and for separating alpha generation (factor selection) from risk management (beta hedging). Without this result, there would be no clean basis for residual decomposition.

## Key Formula/Result
The minimum expected return required to justify allocating to a risky project depends on five factors simultaneously:
1. The risk-free rate r*
2. The "market price of dollar risk" λ
3. The variance of the project's own aggregate present value return
4. The project's return-covariance with assets already held
5. The project's total covariance with other concurrent projects

Central equilibrium equation (SML form):
```
E_i = r* + λ · Cov(R_i, R_m) / Var(R_m)
     = r* + λ · β_i
```
where λ = (E_m - r*) is the market excess return and β_i = σ_im / σ_m².

Lintner's key refinement over Sharpe: relevant risk for each stock is measured not by its standard deviation alone but by the **sum of its own variance and its total covariance** with all other stocks — i.e., the full covariance contribution to portfolio risk.

## Modern Relevance for Korea Strategy
The Separation Theorem is the foundational warrant for our residual decomposition step: stripping market beta before computing any factor signal is not a preference but a theoretical necessity. In KOSPI200, the market factor accounts for ~40-60% of return variance in individual stocks; skipping beta neutralization before computing GPA, ROE, or momentum signals would conflate factor alpha with market-timing beta — exactly the error Lintner's framework rules out. Lintner also shows there is "no risk-discount rate" applicable uniformly across projects: this validates our regime-conditional expected return adjustments rather than using a single hurdle rate.
