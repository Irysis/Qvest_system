# P068: Zooming In on Equity Factor Crowding (Volpati, Benzaquen, Eisler, Mastromatteo, Toth, Bouchaud 2020)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Crowding
**Feasibility:** medium (microstructure data needed; volume proxy feasible in Korea)
**Novelty:** high

## Core Claim
Factor crowding is directly measurable from market microstructure: the order flow of institutional investors is correlated with their factor signals. Using trade imbalance metrics, the authors show roughly 1–2% of total market order flow is systematically correlated with Momentum factor signals, and this fraction has increased since 2012. Crowding creates "co-impact" — amplified market impact when many investors rebalance the same factor simultaneously — which degrades net factor returns.

## Crowding Detection Method (exact metrics if available)
Three trade imbalance measures, all computed at daily frequency:

1. **I_trade** = signed number of trades (buy minus sell) as fraction of total trades
2. **I_volume** = signed volume imbalance as fraction of total volume
3. **I_book** = order book imbalance (bid size minus ask size) / total size

**Metaorder imbalance (I_meta)**: institutional order flow from Ancerno database, matched to factor signals.

Key regression: `I_trade(t) ~ beta * signal(t)` where signal uses EMA-slowed factor scores with timescale D.
- Momentum (12m-1m): beta ≈ 0.01–0.02 (1–2% of order flow correlated with signal)
- Optimal EMA timescale D ≈ 3–6 months for momentum
- HML (value): smaller but statistically significant
- SMB (size): detectable but smaller

**Co-impact degradation**: Factor return decay proportional to crowding fraction squared × market impact coefficient.

## Key Results
- Momentum crowding has increased substantially since 2012 (post-QE era, growth of quant funds)
- 1% signal-correlated order flow creates measurable co-impact that reduces realized Sharpe
- Value (HML) also crowded but less so; Size (SMB) least crowded
- Crowding is persistent (EMA timescale months, not days)
- Longer-horizon signals are more crowded because more managers can track them

## Korea Applicability
- **Data needed**: tick-level trade imbalance from KRX (available via KRX market data feeds)
- **Volume imbalance proxy**: daily OHLCVS already has volume; can compute buy/sell imbalance approximation using Lee-Ready rule on intraday data, or use KOSPI turnover ratio as crude proxy
- **Korea constraint**: No Ancerno equivalent for Korea. Institutional order flow not publicly available at stock level.
- **Practical proxy**: I_volume approximation using daily price change direction × volume (Kyle lambda approach) is feasible with existing RAWDATA.parquet
- **EMA-slowed signal correlation**: Computable from RAWDATA.parquet — correlate 3-month EMA of factor ranks with next-month volume imbalance direction
