# P082: Anomalies Abroad: Beyond Data Mining (Stambaugh, Lu, Yuan 2018)
**Category:** Anomaly_Research / International
**Relevance to Korea pipeline:** Critical

## Core Claim
Nine well-known US anomalies are all significant in international markets (23 non-US markets, 1985-2014), and a composite mispricing measure delivers 118 bps/month (t=16.21) globally. This confirms anomalies are NOT US-specific data mining artifacts — they reflect genuine cross-market mispricing driven by behavioral biases and limits to arbitrage. The composite signal is stronger than individual anomalies.

## Key Findings (specific percentages, survival rates, which anomalies)
- **9 anomalies** all significant in international markets (all t > 2.0)
- **Composite mispricing measure**: 118 bps/month, t-stat = 16.21 (extremely strong)
- Individual anomaly performance internationally (approximate):
  - Financial distress (Ohlson O-score): Significant
  - Gross profitability: Significant
  - Net stock issues: Significant
  - Asset growth: Significant
  - Return on assets (ROA): Significant
  - Accruals: Significant
  - Investment-to-assets: Significant
  - Momentum: Significant (but varies by country)
  - Failure probability: Significant
- Long-short portfolios: all 9 significant in majority of the 23 countries
- Composite metric (average rank across 9 anomalies) outperforms individual anomaly selection
- Mispricing interpretation supported: anomalies stronger in countries with higher retail investor dominance

## Factors That SURVIVE Post-Publication
- All 9 Stambaugh-Yu-Yuan anomalies survive internationally:
  1. Financial distress (O-score)
  2. Gross profitability
  3. Net stock issues (equity issuance)
  4. Asset growth
  5. Return on assets
  6. Total accruals
  7. Investment-to-assets
  8. Return momentum (12-1 month)
  9. Failure probability (Campbell et al.)
- Composite mispricing signal (ranked average) is most robust

## Factors That DECAY Post-Publication
- Individual anomaly weaker than composite in most countries
- Momentum has higher country-specific variance than other anomalies
- No anomaly was found to systematically fail internationally (as opposed to the 65% failure rate in Hou et al. for ALL anomalies)

## Korea/International Applicability
- DIRECTLY applicable to Korea: Korea is part of "Anomalies Abroad" universe (or comparable market)
- Composite mispricing measure approach is immediately actionable for KOSPI200 + KOSDAQ150:
  - Build composite rank across profitability, investment, accruals, net issuance, momentum
  - This delivers stronger signal than any individual factor
- Korea likely benefits MORE from composite approach given lower quantitative arbitrage penetration vs US
- Retail-dominant Korean market → anomaly persistence expected to be higher (mispricing channel)
- Gross profitability factor: directly applicable, DART data provides revenue/COGS for construction
- Asset growth + investment: construction requires balance sheet data (DART fundamentals available)

## Actionable Implications for Our Pipeline
1. **Implement Stambaugh-Yu-Yuan composite** as core signal layer: rank stocks on all 9 anomalies and average ranks
2. **Gross Profitability** (revenue - COGS) / assets: HIGH priority, robust internationally, low turnover
3. **Asset Growth + Investment-to-Assets**: confirmed international; already in DART pipeline
4. **ROA** (return on assets): confirmed robust, already captured in DART fundamentals
5. **Accruals**: confirmed internationally; balance sheet accruals available via DART
6. **Net Stock Issues**: easy to compute from shares outstanding change; issue signal
7. The composite approach is strategy-level implication: create a `composite_mispricing.R` factor that averages percentile ranks of all 9 signals
8. Composite delivers 118 bps/month internationally — even 1/3 of this in Korea = ~40 bps/month gross alpha

## Notes
- Paper: Lu, Stambaugh, Yuan (2018), "Anomalies Abroad: Beyond Data Mining", Journal of Financial Economics 129(1), pp. 115-144
- Note: paper is sometimes cited as "Stambaugh, Lu, Yuan" — same paper, author order varies by citation style
- This is the BEST evidence that anomalies work internationally, directly supporting Korean application
- The composite mispricing metric is a practical tool, not just academic — directly usable in strategy code
- Contrasts with Hou et al. (P080): Hou tests ALL 452 anomalies; Stambaugh et al. test only the 9 most carefully validated ones — difference in scope explains different failure rates
