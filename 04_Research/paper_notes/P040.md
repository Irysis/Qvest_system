## Core Finding

Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) — published in *Review of Financial Studies* — conduct a comprehensive audit of 316 published factors claiming to explain the cross-section of expected returns, arguing that the conventional t-statistic threshold of 2.0 is wholly inadequate given the scale of prior hypothesis testing in the literature. Using a multiple testing framework that accounts for both correlation among tests and publication bias, they conclude that **a newly published factor requires a t-statistic of at least 3.0** (p < 0.27%) to be considered genuine. Harvey's 2017 Presidential Address ("The Scientific Outlook in Financial Economics," *Journal of Finance* 72(4): 1399-1440) extends this into a call for reform of research culture: greater transparency, pre-registration of hypotheses, and publication of negative results. The practical implication is that **80-158 of the 296 anomalies re-examined (27-53%) are likely false discoveries** — the result of p-hacking, data mining, and undisclosed multiple hypothesis testing across the literature. True factors — those surviving the t > 3.0 bar — are few: Value, Momentum, Carry, Size (small-cap), and Quality-Defensive are among the survivors.

---

## Methodology

- **Factor census**: Catalogued 316 factors from papers published 1967-2016; tracked the cumulative number of tests implied by all prior research.
- **Multiple Testing Framework**: Two approaches:
  1. **FWER Control (Bonferroni / Holm)**: Controls family-wise error rate. The Bonferroni-adjusted t-threshold rises monotonically from 1.96 (1967) to 3.78 (2012), reaching 4.0 by 2032.
  2. **FDR Control (Benjamini-Yekutieli)**: Controls the expected proportion of false discoveries among all discoveries. Less conservative than Bonferroni; produces the t > 3.0 recommendation.
- **Publication Bias Adjustment**: Unpublished negative results inflate the effective number of hypotheses tested; the framework adjusts for this "file drawer problem."
- **Practical Recommendations:**
  1. New factors: t > 3.0 minimum; ideally t > 3.4-3.8 per Chordia et al. (2020).
  2. Replicate out-of-sample (new data, new country, different time period) before trusting any single-market result.
  3. Prefer factors with a strong economic/behavioral prior (reduces effective number of hypotheses).
  4. Avoid in-sample testing on the same dataset used by prior literature.

### 3. Factor selection criteria for future strategies

Apply a **Korean Harvey screen** before committing to full backtest:

```r
# Pre-screening heuristic for new factors
harvey_screen <- function(factor_name,
                           us_t_stat,        # t-stat in original US paper
                           kr_effect_ratio,  # ratio of Korean effect to US (from lit)
                           economic_prior,   # 1=strong behavioral/risk prior, 0=purely empirical
                           years_in_lit) {   # years since original US publication

  # Adjusted Korea t-stat estimate
  kr_t_est <- us_t_stat * kr_effect_ratio

  # Harvey threshold (t > 3.0 minimum; prefer t > 3.5 for Korea given smaller sample)
  harvey_pass <- kr_t_est >= 3.0

  # Additional adjustments
  # 1. Strong economic prior reduces multiple testing concern (-0.5 adjustment to threshold)
  adjusted_threshold <- ifelse(economic_prior == 1, 2.5, 3.0)

  # 2. Old factors (>10 years in lit) are more likely to be crowded-away in Korea
  # But also more likely to have been genuinely replicated

  cat("Factor:", factor_name, "\n")
  cat("Estimated Korea t-stat:", round(kr_t_est, 2), "\n")
  cat("Harvey threshold:", adjusted_threshold, "\n")
  cat("Preliminary PASS:", kr_t_est >= adjusted_threshold, "\n")
}

# Examples:
harvey_screen("MAX/Anti-Lottery", us_t_stat=3.5, kr_effect_ratio=1.3, economic_prior=1, years_in_lit=13)
# Expected: PASS (Korea effect stronger than US)

harvey_screen("Sloan Accrual", us_t_stat=3.8, kr_effect_ratio=0.5, economic_prior=1, years_in_lit=28)
# Expected: BORDERLINE (Korea effect ~40-60% of US → t ~ 1.9-2.3 → below threshold)
# Implication: Accrual in Korea needs regime conditioning or size interaction to work

harvey_screen("Momentum", us_t_stat=4.2, kr_effect_ratio=0.6, economic_prior=1, years_in_lit=30)
# Expected: BORDERLINE (Korea momentum has crash risk, crowding risk, t ~ 2.5)
```

### 4. Pre-registration approach for our research system

Harvey recommends pre-registering hypotheses before seeing the data. Our strategy_registry.json serves this function: strategies are registered as `code_ready` before results are observed, preventing post-hoc rationalization.

**Enhancement**: Before running any new backtest, write the **expected direction and magnitude** in the strategy registry entry. After results, compare predictions vs. outcomes. This discipline prevents selective reporting.

### 5. Factor zoo implications for ensemble strategies

If 27-53% of published factors are false, then an ensemble combining many factors from the literature will have high noise-to-signal ratio. Our approach of focusing on a small set of well-validated factors (5-7) with strong priors is exactly right. Do NOT expand the composite signal (STR_016) to include more than 4-5 components without strong out-of-sample evidence for each.

**Feasibility:** HIGH (methodological framework directly applicable to our existing infrastructure)

## Strategy Ideas

### IDEA-METHODOLOGY: Korean Factor Validity Scorecard

**Implement a systematic pre-screening before running any new factor backtest:**

```
Harvey Scorecard (run before committing to new factor):
[ ] t-stat in original paper >= 3.0
[ ] Economic/behavioral mechanism clearly articulated (not purely empirical)
[ ] Effect documented in at least one non-US market
[ ] Korea-specific replication found in literature (preferred) or plausible mechanism
[ ] Not already captured by our existing factors (low correlation)
[ ] OOS plan documented in strategy_registry BEFORE running backtest
[ ] Historical t-stat adjusted for Korea sample size (2001-2026 = 25 years)
```

**Korea sample size adjustment:** With 25 years of monthly data (300 months), we have limited power. For a factor with true monthly Sharpe of 0.15 (= 0.52 annualized), the t-stat over 300 months is ~2.6. This means factors with annual Sharpe < 0.5 will be hard to distinguish from noise in our sample even if real. This is a key constraint on factor selection: only factors with **expected Sharpe > 0.5** in Korea are worth pursuing as standalone strategies.

---

### IDEA-PROCESS: OOS Split Enhancement

Harvey's framework suggests our current IS/OOS split (65/35%) should additionally use:
1. **Geographic OOS**: Test factors in KOSPI200 and KOSDAQ150 separately — if the factor works in one but not the other, it's a data-mining artifact.
2. **Subperiod robustness**: Require the factor to work in at least 3 of 4 non-overlapping 6-year subperiods (2001-2007, 2008-2013, 2014-2019, 2020-2026).
3. **Crisis period performance**: Does the factor survive 2008, 2020, 2022? Factors that only work in calm markets are potentially data-mined from calm periods.

### IDEA-HURDLE: Harvey-Adjusted t-stat Requirement in Hurdle Gate

**Proposed addition to hurdle_gate.R:**
```r
# D001 Harvey t-stat screen (new soft score component, ±5pts)
# Add to existing scoring system
# Requires: IC time series from analysis_ic.csv
compute_harvey_score <- function(ic_series) {
  # IC t-stat = mean(IC) / sd(IC) * sqrt(N)
  n <- length(ic_series)
  ic_tstat <- mean(ic_series, na.rm = TRUE) / sd(ic_series, na.rm = TRUE) * sqrt(n)

  # Harvey threshold: t > 3.0 for full credit, t > 2.0 for partial
  if (ic_tstat >= 3.5) return(5)    # Strong evidence
  if (ic_tstat >= 3.0) return(3)    # Meets Harvey threshold
  if (ic_tstat >= 2.0) return(0)    # Meets traditional threshold only
  return(-5)                         # Below traditional threshold (warning)
}
```
