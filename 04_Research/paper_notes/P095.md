## 1. Core Thesis

Fama and MacBeth develop and apply a rigorous econometric methodology to test the two-parameter (mean-variance) portfolio model of market equilibrium. The paper simultaneously (1) proposes the Fama-MacBeth regression procedure as a standard method for cross-sectional asset pricing tests, and (2) finds that the NYSE data from 1935-1968 cannot reject the two-parameter model: beta is the only priced risk measure, the return-beta relationship is linear, and non-beta risk (idiosyncratic volatility) carries no significant premium. These findings largely support CAPM (with the exception that the zero-beta return exceeds the risk-free rate, confirming Black 1972).

The Fama-MacBeth methodology became the dominant approach for testing factor pricing and remains the standard in empirical asset pricing.

## 2. Factor Construction (Fama-MacBeth Methodology)

**Data**: All NYSE common stocks, January 1926 – June 1968 (Fisher Arithmetic Index as market proxy). Final testing period: 1935-1968, approximately 396 months.

**Three-step procedure**:

**Step 1: Portfolio formation (beta estimation from independent data)**
- Use first 4 years (1926-29) to estimate individual stock betas: beta_i = cov(r_i, r_M) / var(r_M)
- Sort stocks by beta into 20 portfolios (to reduce errors-in-variables from individual stock betas)
- Use next 5 years (1930-34) as estimation period: recompute portfolio betas beta_p,t-1 and non-beta risk s(e_p) annually

**Step 2: Monthly cross-sectional regression**
Each month t, run:
```
R_pt = gamma_0t + gamma_1t * beta_p,t-1 + gamma_2t * beta^2_p,t-1 + gamma_3t * s_p,t-1(e) + eta_pt
```

where:
- R_pt = monthly return on portfolio p at time t
- beta_p,t-1 = portfolio beta (estimated from prior data)
- beta^2_p,t-1 = squared beta (tests linearity: H0: E(gamma_2t) = 0)
- s_p,t-1(e) = non-beta risk (std of residuals from market model, tests C2: H0: E(gamma_3t) = 0)
- gamma_0t, gamma_1t, gamma_2t, gamma_3t = stochastic coefficients (vary each month)

**Step 3: Time-series inference on gammas**
Collect time series of monthly gamma_jt estimates. Compute means gamma_j and t-statistics:
```
t(gamma_j) = gamma_j / (s(gamma_j) / sqrt(n))
```
where n = number of months.

**This is the core Fama-MacBeth innovation**: by averaging across months, the procedure automatically accounts for cross-sectional correlation in residuals (which OLS would ignore). Standard errors are the time-series standard errors of the gamma_jt estimates.

**Market efficiency test (fair game)**:
Also tests whether time series of (gamma_1t - [E(r_M) - E(r_0)]), gamma_2t, gamma_3t, and portfolio residuals are fair games (zero mean, no serial correlation).

## 3. Key Results

**Main findings (1935-6/68 overall period)**:

| Hypothesis | Coefficient | Mean | Std | t-stat | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C3 (positive risk-return) | gamma_1 | +0.0085 | 0.113 | 2.57 | SUPPORTED |
| C1 (linearity) | gamma_2 | +0.0002 | 0.116 | +0.02 | NOT REJECTED |
| C2 (no non-beta risk) | gamma_3 | -0.0008 | 0.118 | -0.29 | NOT REJECTED |
| S-L hypothesis | gamma_0 - r_f | +0.0048 | 0.098 | 3.24 | REJECTED (gamma_0 > r_f) |

**Key conclusions**:
- **C1 (linearity)**: gamma_2 coefficient is insignificant — beta-return relationship is linear. High-beta stocks are not systematically over/underpriced relative to a linear SML.
- **C2 (no non-beta risk)**: gamma_3 coefficient is insignificant — idiosyncratic volatility (non-beta risk) is NOT priced. Investors do NOT require compensation for specific risk.
- **C3 (positive risk-return)**: gamma_1 > 0 with t = 2.57 — beta IS priced positively. Higher systematic risk earns higher expected return.
- **S-L CAPM rejected**: gamma_0 = 0.0061/month >> r_f ≈ 0.004/month. The intercept is too high, consistent with Black's zero-beta model.
- **Market efficiency (ME)**: Serial correlations of gamma_jt are all near zero. The time series of risk premia behave as fair games — consistent with market efficiency.

**Sub-period stability**:
- C3 supported in most sub-periods but not all (e.g., 1956-60 is an exception — the poor statistical power period)
- C1 and C2 consistently not rejected across all sub-periods
- The zero-beta premium (gamma_0 - r_f) is always positive post-WWII

## 4. Korea / International Applicability

**Methodology directly applicable**:
- The Fama-MacBeth procedure is the standard methodology for Korean factor testing. Any new factor for our pipeline should be tested via FM regression.
- **Korean implementation note**: Use KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 portfolios (not individual stocks) to reduce EIV. Form 20 beta-sorted portfolios analogously to FM.
- **Non-beta risk (C2 test) for Korea**: In Korean context, the C2 result is contested. With retail-dominated market and many undiversified investors, idiosyncratic risk may carry a premium for KOSDAQ stocks. Should test explicitly.
- **Linearity (C1 test) for Korea**: Korean market shows some evidence of beta non-linearity during stress (high-beta stocks lose more than linear model predicts) — suggests gamma_2 may be significantly negative in Korean market recessions.

**Practical methodology**:
- Use FM regression monthly (not annual) for Korean data given monthly rebalancing strategy
- Include industry dummies as controls in the cross-sectional regression
- Use Newey-West standard errors (not just time-series std/sqrt(n)) to account for autocorrelation in gamma_jt (Korean gamma_1t is more autocorrelated than US due to momentum effects)

## 5. Actionable Implications for Pipeline

1. **FM Regression in R** (implementation for Korean pipeline):
```r
# Fama-MacBeth regression procedure
run_fm_regression <- function(returns_panel, factors_panel) {
  # Step 1: Estimate betas (rolling 60-month time-series)
  betas <- estimate_rolling_betas(returns_panel, factors_panel, window = 60)

  # Step 2: Monthly cross-sectional regression
  gamma_monthly <- returns_panel %>%
    left_join(betas, by = c("ticker", "date")) %>%
    group_by(date) %>%
    do({
      fit <- lm(ret ~ beta + I(beta^2) + ivol + factor(sector), data = .)
      broom::tidy(fit) %>% select(term, estimate)
    }) %>%
    ungroup()

  # Step 3: Time-series inference
  gamma_summary <- gamma_monthly %>%
    group_by(term) %>%
    summarise(
      mean_gamma = mean(estimate),
      se_gamma = sd(estimate) / sqrt(n()),  # Fama-MacBeth SE
      t_stat = mean_gamma / se_gamma,
      nw_se = NeweyWest_se(estimate)  # Newey-West for robustness
    )
  return(gamma_summary)
}
```

2. **Non-beta risk test**: Always include s(e) (specific volatility) in FM regression as a check. If significantly positive in Korea, it means KOSDAQ retail-driven idiosyncratic risk is priced — add it to the factor model.

3. **Market efficiency (fair game) test**: After estimating monthly gamma_1t for Korean data, test the autocorrelation of gamma_1t. If the risk premium is autocorrelated, it implies predictable time-variation — use for regime-conditional factor allocation.

4. **t-threshold**: FM t-statistics of 2.57 (as reported for C3) is the minimum threshold for claiming a factor is priced. Consistent with Hou-Xue-Zhang recommendation of t ≥ 2.5-3.0.
