# P170: Smart Beta Trends — Bernstein Quick Take, August 2017
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 2

## Core Finding
Smart beta ETF AUM = $660bn (4.2% of total global AUM, 16% of ETF AUM) as of August 2017. Factor AUM concentration: Value 24.3%, Dividend Yield 24.0%, Growth 22.4%, Small Cap 17.3%, Low Vol 6.9%, Quality 2.5%, Multi-Factor 1.8%, Momentum 0.8%. Six-month growth leaders: Momentum +59%, Multi-Factor +44%. Low Vol suffered sharp outflows after H1 2016 peak, declining from 1.2% to ~1.0% of global AUM. Fee trends mostly flat or declining, except Momentum and Dividend yield up 2bps.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
The AUM concentration data reveals which factors are most crowded globally: Value (24.3%), Dividend (24.0%), and Growth (22.4%) together represent 70% of smart beta AUM. These are precisely the most crowded factors and thus the most susceptible to mean-reversion and crowding-driven drawdowns. For Korea, the local equivalent concentration is even more severe because the institutional base is narrower — Korean pension funds and insurance companies dominate smart beta allocations, creating cluster risk.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. Use the inverse of global AUM concentration as a crowding-adjusted factor weight: underweight Value and Dividend (crowded, 24%+ AUM share each) relative to Momentum and Quality (undercrowded, <3% AUM share) in the factor composite.
2. Monitor the trajectory of Momentum and Multi-Factor AUM growth: at the 2017 pace (+59%/+44% per 6 months), these factors will become crowded within 2–3 years of this writing (i.e., approximately 2019–2020) — suggesting that current (2026) Momentum crowding may already be elevated.
