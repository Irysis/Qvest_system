# P028: Human Inference — Nisbett & Ross (1980) [Review]

## Core Finding
Nisbett & Ross's seminal synthesis of the attribution and judgment literature demonstrates that human inference is governed by a limited set of cognitive heuristics — representativeness, availability, anchoring/adjustment — that produce systematic and predictable errors. Particularly, people dramatically overweight vivid, concrete, personal (case study) evidence relative to abstract, statistical, base-rate information, even when the statistical evidence is more diagnostic.

## Korea Applicability
MEDIUM — The vivid-vs.-statistical evidence imbalance explains why Korean investors overreact to company site visits and management presentations (vivid, concrete) relative to systematic financial analysis (abstract, statistical). This creates post-event drift following company investor days and analyst visits.

## Strategy Implication
Overvivid evidence bias predicts that stocks with recent media attention (news stories, Bloomberg features, investor day presentations) are temporarily overvalued as vivid concrete stories override statistical base rates. Signal: abnormal news count in past 30 days (if news data available) as a negative factor. Alternatively, companies recently added to indices (get vivid media attention from index addition) temporarily overshoot and then revert — the index addition anomaly. In Korean context: KOSPI200 and KOSDAQ150 additions create short-term vivid-evidence-driven price jumps followed by reversal.
