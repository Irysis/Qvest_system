# P202: Dependency Structure and Volatility Connectedness among China-ASEAN Stock Market, Cryptocurrencies, and Crude Oil (2026)
**Category:** Risk_Portfolio | **Priority:** 3
**Authors:** Hongjun Zeng, Abdullahi D. Ahmed | **Journal:** Financial Innovation, 12:11 (2026)

## Core Thesis
Using GARCH-EVT-Vine-Copula and QVAR (Quantile VAR) connectedness methods, this paper maps dependency structures and volatility spillovers among China, ASEAN-6 (Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Philippines, Indonesia) equity markets, crude oil (WTI), cryptocurrencies (CCi30), and MSCI developed markets across three regimes: pre-COVID (Jun 2016 - Jan 2020), COVID period (Feb 2020 - Feb 2022), and Russia-Ukraine war period (Feb 2022 - Mar 2023).

**Central finding:** Tail dependencies are asymmetric, time-varying, and regime-dependent. The MSCI developed markets index exposure **significantly increases** lower-tail risk for Chinese and Indonesian equities during COVID. QVAR analysis shows connectedness is stronger at extreme quantiles (upper and lower tails) than at the median — normal-based risk models systematically understate crisis-period dependence.

## Key Quantitative Results
- During COVID: MSCI developed markets exposure created strong **lower tail dependence** for China + Indonesia — conventional portfolios underestimated tail risk
- Russia-Ukraine war: Risk to Thailand-Malaysia pairs was **overestimated** by conventional methods; Singapore showed stronger hub connectedness than other ASEAN markets
- QVAR: Total connectedness at median quantile exceeded lower-quartile connectedness during COVID, meaning COVID triggered a breakdown of conventional hedge structures
- Chinese and ASEAN equities showed **insufficient hedging effectiveness** against crude oil, cryptocurrencies, and MSCI at any point — they cannot serve as effective hedges for developed-market portfolios
- Propensity to allocate to China-ASEAN as a hedging strategy persisted across all market regimes regardless of prevailing conditions
- Portfolio analysis: Chinese + ASEAN equities functioned as **safe-haven assets** for intra-regional investors during both crises

## Application to Korean Factor Strategy
Korea (KOSPI200) is structurally similar to the China-ASEAN cluster in this study — an Asian emerging/developed hybrid market with strong trade links to China and exposure to global risk-off episodes. Key applications:

1. **Vine copula for Korean factor tail risk**: Use GARCH-EVT-Vine-Copula to model tail dependencies between our factor portfolio and KOSPI market index rather than assuming Gaussian correlation. In Korean crises, factor portfolio returns likely show asymmetric lower-tail dependence with the market (factors crash together when market crashes).

2. **QVAR connectedness for factor regime detection**: Monitor connectedness of Korean market returns at extreme quantiles vs. median — a spike in extreme-quantile connectedness is an early warning signal of regime transition (crowding unwind or market stress), triggering defensive position reduction.

3. **China risk transmission channel**: Korean equities are highly exposed to China through export and trade linkages (Korea's China export share ~25%). This paper shows China-ASEAN tail connectedness spikes during crises; Korean portfolio downside risk therefore has a China-channel component that standard KOSPI beta does not capture.

4. **Asymmetric correlation adjustment**: Our `backtest_harness.R` currently uses symmetric covariance. Add regime-specific correlation matrices: normal-regime vs. stress-regime, where stress-regime uses upper-tail and lower-tail copula parameters.
