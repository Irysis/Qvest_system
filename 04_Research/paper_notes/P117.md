# P117 — Pastor & Stambaugh (2003): Liquidity Risk and Expected Stock Returns [SEMINAL]

**Citation:** Pastor, L., & Stambaugh, R.F. (2003). Liquidity risk and expected stock returns. *Journal of Political Economy*, 111(3), 642–685.

---

## Core Thesis

The seminal paper establishing **aggregate market liquidity as a priced state variable**. The key innovation is measuring liquidity by the **temporal price reversal following volume-driven moves** (not by the level of trading costs). If a stock is illiquid, a large buy order drives the price up temporarily, but the price later reverses — a higher gamma means larger reversal per dollar of volume. Stocks with high sensitivity to aggregate liquidity innovations (high "liquidity beta") earn significantly higher expected returns: 7.5% per year after controlling for size, value, momentum, and market risk.

---

## Factor Construction

**Individual stock liquidity measure (gamma):**

    r^e_{i,d+1,t} = θ_{i,t} + φ_{i,t} * r_{i,d,t} + γ_{i,t} * sign(r^e_{i,d,t}) * v_{i,d,t} + ε_{i,d+1,t}

where:
- r^e_{i,d+1,t} = excess return (vs. value-weighted market) for stock i on day d+1 in month t
- r_{i,d,t} = stock i's total return on day d (lagged control for non-volume-related reversals)
- sign(r^e_{i,d,t}) = direction of excess return on day d (proxy for "order flow direction")
- v_{i,d,t} = dollar volume on day d in month t (in millions of dollars)
- **γ_{i,t}** = stock i's monthly liquidity measure (expected to be negative: volume-driven return reverses)
- **CRITICAL**: estimate only on days with v_{i,d,t} > 0 (exclude zero-volume days)
- Require D > 15 observations; exclude stocks with price < $5 or > $1,000; equal-weight

**Aggregate market liquidity (level):**

    γ̄_t = (1/N_t) * Σ_i γ̂_{i,t}  (cross-sectional average)

**Liquidity innovation (PS factor):**

    Δγ̄_t = a + b * Δγ̄_{t-1} + c * (m_{t-1}/m_t) * γ̄_{t-1} + μ_t

The residual μ_t is the (unexpected) liquidity innovation. Scaled by 100 to form the aggregate index L_t.

**Traded liquidity factor construction:**
1. Each December, estimate liquidity betas β^L_i from 60-month rolling regression:
    r_{i,t} = β^0_i + β^L_i * L_t + β^MKT_i * MKT_t + β^SMB_i * SMB_t + β^HML_i * HML_t + ε_{i,t}
2. Sort stocks into 10 equal-size portfolios by predicted β^L
3. Traded factor = D10 (high β) minus D1 (low β) portfolio, monthly rebalanced

## Key Results

**Aggregate liquidity time-series:**
- Largest drops in liquidity: October 1987 (crash), September 1998 (LTCM), November 1973 (oil embargo)
- All financial crises register sharp drops — confirms γ captures market liquidity

**Cross-sectional return tests (1966-1999):**
| Specification | Liquidity Beta Premium |
|---|---|
| vs. MKT only | 9.2%/year*** |
| vs. FF3 (size + B/M) | 8.1%/year*** |
| vs. Carhart 4-factor | 7.5%/year*** |

- Spread between top and bottom liquidity beta decile: **7.5% per year** (t ≈ 2.5)
- Split-sample: first half (1966-1982) 9.1%/year; second half (1983-1999) 5.4%/year
- **Momentum link:** Liquidity risk accounts for half of momentum profits; adding PS factor to FF3+momentum model reduces momentum alpha by 50%

**Traded factor statistics:**
- FF3 alpha of traded factor: ~4.5%/year (t ≈ 2.08) in 1968-1999

## Korea-Specific Implications

- The gamma regression is directly estimable from Korean RAWDATA.parquet (has daily return and volume)
- Must use KRX value-weighted market return as r_m (IKS200/KOSPI200 return)
- Volume in Korean data: v_{i,d,t} should be in KRW (not shares) to match dollar-volume convention
- Critical filters for Korean market:
  1. Exclude v = 0 days (even more important in Korea)
  2. Price filter: 1,000 KRW < price < 1,000,000 KRW
  3. Require ≥ 15 trading days per month
- Expected aggregate Korean liquidity drops: Oct 1997, Aug 1998, Oct 2008, Nov 2008, Mar 2020
- If Korean aggregate liquidity is estimated, use it as a macro regime signal: when L_t drops sharply → reduce exposure to high-β_L stocks (or short them)

# Step 1: Estimate monthly gamma for each stock

estimate_ps_gamma_monthly <- function(stock_daily) {
  stock_daily %>%
    filter(volume_krw > 0, !is.na(ret), !is.na(ret_excess)) %>%
    arrange(date) %>%
    mutate(
      ret_excess_next = lead(ret_excess),
      signed_vol = sign(ret_excess) * (volume_krw / 1e8)  # KRW 100M units
    ) %>%
    filter(!is.na(ret_excess_next)) %>%
    group_by(ym = format(date, "%Y-%m")) %>%
    group_modify(~{
      d <- .x
      n <- nrow(d)
      if (n < 15) return(tibble(gamma = NA_real_, n_obs = n))

      X <- cbind(1, d$ret, d$signed_vol)
      Y <- d$ret_excess_next

      tryCatch({
        coef <- solve(crossprod(X), crossprod(X, Y))
        tibble(gamma = coef[3], n_obs = n)
      }, error = function(e) tibble(gamma = NA_real_, n_obs = n))
    })
}
