# P010: On the Failure to Eliminate Hypotheses in a Conceptual Task (1960)

## Core Finding
Wason's classic "2-4-6 task" experiment demonstrates confirmation bias: subjects prefer to test hypotheses by seeking confirming evidence rather than disconfirming evidence (eliminative induction). Only 6 of 29 subjects reached the correct conclusion without prior incorrect conclusions; 13 reached two or more incorrect conclusions. Subjects systematically fail to generate tests that could falsify their beliefs.

## Key Metrics
- Alpha/Return: N/A (cognitive psychology experiment)
- Statistical strength: N = 29 subjects; Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology 1960
- Sample: UK subjects, 1960

## Korea Applicability
MEDIUM — Confirmation bias is universal. In the context of quantitative strategy development, it warns against cherry-picking in-sample results and the danger of only testing scenarios that confirm a strategy hypothesis.

## Strategy Implication
Practical anti-confirmation-bias protocol for strategy development: (1) pre-register the hypothesis and test conditions BEFORE seeing results; (2) actively seek disconfirming periods and market regimes; (3) in the Maxwell Protocol, require out-of-sample testing on held-out periods before strategies pass hurdle_gate.R; (4) for each factor, test explicitly in bear regimes, small-cap vs. large-cap, and high-turnover vs. low-turnover periods to find failure modes. The hurdle gate's "hard fail" conditions represent formalized attempts to falsify.
