# P163: Smart Beta Funds (2020)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
By Q1 2019, smart beta AUM reached $3.5 trillion globally. Critically, approximately one-third of products labeled "smart beta" are effectively market funds (R-squared > 0.99 vs. cap-weight index) — mislabeled passive products sold as factor strategies. Of the genuine factor products, only HML (value, long-short) is practically tradable at scale; other factors (SMB, momentum, quality) face severe implementation frictions. The paper concludes that the "factor zoo" is much smaller in practice than in theory.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
The same mislabeling problem exists in Korea: several KOSPI200-linked ETFs branded as "smart beta value" have R-squared > 0.95 with the plain KOSPI200 index. Investors are paying smart beta fees for index-equivalent exposure. For our pipeline, this means that any benchmark comparison against Korean "smart beta" ETFs is potentially misleading — we need to compare against the genuine factor long-short return, not the labeled product.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. When benchmarking strategy performance in summarise_perf(), add R-squared vs. IKS200 as a required output metric; if R-squared > 0.90, classify as "benchmark-hugging" and require evidence of genuine factor tilt before claiming active alpha.
2. The practical tradability finding validates our KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 universe restriction: the only factors with sufficient liquidity depth in Korea are value (P/B, P/E based) and momentum; quality and low-vol require careful size filtering to be executable.
