# P056: The Implications of Accounting Distortions and Growth for Accruals and Profitability (Richardson, Sloan, Soliman & Tuna 2006)

**Category:** Factor_Return / Accrual
**Feasibility:** high
**Novelty:** medium

---

## Core Claim

The lower persistence of the accrual component of earnings is primarily caused by **temporary accounting distortions** (accrual estimation error, including opportunistic earnings manipulation), NOT by growth-related factors such as diminishing marginal returns to new investment (the competing "FWY hypothesis"). The paper demonstrates this through three complementary approaches: (1) numerical examples showing permanent conservative accounting + growth is inconsistent with empirical patterns, while temporary aggressive/conservative accounting IS consistent; (2) decomposing accruals into "growth" and "efficiency" components and showing BOTH have low persistence; and (3) documenting that firms subject to SEC enforcement actions for earnings manipulation have abnormally high accruals at the time of violation that subsequently reverse.

The key insight: **the efficiency component of accruals** (change in asset turnover = delta_AT, NOT attributable to real investment growth) has the MOST negative coefficient on future RNOA. This component captures accounting distortions. The growth component (sales growth = SG) also matters but less so. This contradicts the FWY/growth-only explanation.

## Factor Definition (exact formulas)

**Total Operating Accruals (ACC):**
```
ACC_t = (NOA_t - NOA_{t-1}) / NOA_{t-1}
```
Where:
- NOA = Net Operating Assets = Operating Assets (OA) - Operating Liabilities (OL)
- OA = Current Operating Assets (COA) + Noncurrent Operating Assets (NCOA)
- OL = Current Operating Liabilities (COL) + Noncurrent Operating Liabilities (NCOL)

**Balance sheet items (Compustat mappings):**
```
COA  = Current Assets (#4) - Cash and ST Investments (#1)
NCOA = Total Assets (#6) - Current Assets (#4) - Investments & Advances (#32)
COL  = Current Liabilities (#5) - Debt in Current Liabilities (#34)
NCOL = Total Liabilities (#181) - Current Liabilities (#5) - Long-Term Debt (#9)
NOA  = OA - OL = (COA + NCOA) - (COL + NCOL)
```

**Growth vs. Efficiency Decomposition (algebraic identity, no estimation needed):**
```
ACC_t = ΔNOA_t / NOA_{t-1}
      = ΔSales_t/Sales_{t-1}   -   ΔAT_t/AT_t   -   (ΔSales_t/Sales_{t-1}) * (ΔAT_t/AT_t)
        ──────────────────        ───────────────        ──────────────────────────────────────
           Sales Growth              Change in                      Interaction
               (SG)                 Efficiency (ΔAT)
```
Where:
```
AT_t = Sales_t / NOA_t          (Asset Turnover ratio)
SG   = (Sales_t / Sales_{t-1}) - 1
ΔAT  = (Sales_t/NOA_t - Sales_{t-1}/NOA_{t-1}) / NOA_t
```

**Earnings Persistence Regression (main test):**
```
RNOA_{t+1} = γ_0 + γ_1*RNOA_t + γ_2*ACC_t + v_{t+1}        ...(1)
```
- H: γ_2 < 0 (accruals are less persistent than cash flows)
- Result: γ_2 = -0.131, t-stat = -14.85

**Growth-Efficiency Decomposition Regression:**
```
RNOA_{t+1} = γ_0 + γ_1*RNOA_t + γ_2*SG_t - γ_3*ΔAT_t - γ_4*(SG_t * ΔAT_t) + v_{t+1}  ...(5)
```
- Results: γ_2 (SG) = -0.102, t = -13.65; γ_3 (ΔAT) = -0.151, t = -17.06 (LARGER)
- ΔAT coefficient is significantly MORE negative than SG coefficient (consistent with accounting distortions, not growth, being the primary driver)

## Key Results (returns, t-stats, IC)

**Data:** 106,423 firm-years, 1962-2001, Compustat

**Persistence Tests (Table 4):**
- Baseline: ACC coefficient on future RNOA = **-0.131** (t = -14.85)
- After growth/efficiency decomposition:
  - SG coefficient: **-0.102** (t = -13.65) — moderate
  - ΔAT coefficient: **-0.151** (t = -17.06) — LARGER and more significant
  - Interaction term: -0.007 (t = -0.93) — insignificant
- Both components separately significant, ruling out growth-only explanation
- ΔAT > SG effect in magnitude is the central evidence

**Descriptive Stats (Table 2):**
- Mean ACC = 0.164, Std Dev = 0.342
- Mean SG = 0.159, Mean ΔAT = -0.036
- Correlation ACC-SG = 0.493; ACC-ΔAT = -0.621

**SEC Enforcement Results (Table 5):**
- High accruals strongly predict SEC enforcement (γ_2 = 1.28, p < 0.0001)
- Both working capital AND noncurrent operating accruals predict enforcement
- Both growth and efficiency components predict enforcement
- Accruals REVERSE in the 2-3 years after enforcement action (Table 6) — consistent with temporary aggressive distortions, not permanent accounting bias

**Event-Time Pattern (Figure 1, Table 3):**
- High-ACC firms: ACC spikes to 0.863 in year 0, falls to 0.259 in year +1
- RNOA: High ACC → RNOA=0.235 year 0, falls to RNOA=0.106 year +1
- This sharp reversal is consistent with TEMPORARY aggressive accounting, not permanent conservative accounting

## Korea Applicability

**Applicability: HIGH** with nuances.

1. **NOA-based accrual measure is DART-computable.** All components (COA, NCOA, COL, NCOL) map directly to K-IFRS balance sheet line items. The NOA-scaled accrual is more comprehensive than Sloan's working capital accrual (already in STR_012).

2. **ΔAT (efficiency component) is especially valuable for Korea.** Korean chaebol firms are notorious for asset efficiency games — revenue inflation via related-party transactions changes asset turnover without real growth. The ΔAT component would flag such distortions.

3. **Earnings manipulation context is highly relevant.** Korea has a code-law system with weaker enforcement than the US. The SEC enforcement angle suggests that in markets with weaker enforcement (like Korea), the accounting distortion component may be LARGER as a proportion of total accrual effect, since manipulation is less frequently punished.

4. **Magnitude reduction expected:** Korea is a code-law country; expect the return effect to be ~40-60% of the US magnitude. But the ΔAT factor's relative dominance over SG should still hold.

5. **Implementation note:** K-IFRS may complicate the exact NCOA/NCOL mapping vs. Korean GAAP. Use:
   - Operating Assets = Total Assets - Cash & Equivalents - Financial Assets (투자금융자산)
   - Operating Liabilities = Total Liabilities - Financial Debt (차입금/사채)

## Notes

- This is the Richardson-Sloan 2006 paper, NOT to be confused with Richardson et al. 2005 (JAE accrual reliability paper). The 2006 paper is specifically about discriminating accounting distortions vs. growth as drivers of accrual anomaly.
- The algebraic decomposition (Equation 4) is a clean identity — no model estimation error. This makes it more robust than Jones-model discretionary accruals.
- The "temporarily aggressive" vs "temporarily conservative" accounting taxonomy (Panels D and E of Table 1) is a useful conceptual framework: write-downs = temporary conservative (low ACC, high future RNOA); revenue manipulation = temporary aggressive (high ACC, low future RNOA).
- For R implementation: the SG/ΔAT decomposition requires only Sales, NOA, and lagged values — all available from DART fundamentals.
