# P157: ERI Scientific Beta — Robustness of Smart Beta Strategies (2016)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Distinguishes relative robustness (outperforming cap-weight in most sub-periods) from absolute robustness (statistically significant alpha). Of 6 factors tested (B/M, E/P, momentum, size, low-vol, investment), only B/M and E/P pass statistical significance for absolute robustness. Composite scoring approaches that average many factors show strong relative robustness but fail on absolute robustness due to data-snooping bias from combining weak individual signals. Pure factor portfolios with strong theoretical foundations outperform ad-hoc multi-factor aggregations.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
Korea's shorter factor history (reliable data from ~2001) means most factor t-statistics are inflated vs. the US 50-year record. A composite score built from 6+ DART fundamental factors may pass relative robustness tests in backtests while failing absolute robustness — the exact data-snooping trap described here. With only 20–25 years of history, statistical power is insufficient to distinguish luck from structural alpha for most individual Korean factors.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. Apply the ERI absolute robustness test in hurdle_gate.R: require minimum t-stat > 2.0 on factor IC (information coefficient) over rolling 10-year windows before including any factor in production composite.
2. Prioritize B/P and E/P as anchor factors in the Korean value composite, as these show cross-market robustness; treat other fundamental factors (GPA, Accrual) as secondary tilts with lower weight.
