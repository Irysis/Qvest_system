# P023: Prob Distort Preston Baratta Auctions 1948

## Core Finding
Preston & Baratta (1948) provide early experimental evidence that people systematically distort objective probabilities when making decisions: they overweight low probabilities and underweight high probabilities. This probability distortion was observed in auction bidding behavior and is one of the earliest empirical demonstrations of the non-linearity in probability weighting that Kahneman & Tversky (1979) later formalized in Prospect Theory.

## Strategy Implication
Probability overweighting of small chances creates the "lottery anomaly": stocks with high maximum daily return over the past month (MAX factor, Bali et al. 2011) are overpriced and have lower subsequent returns. Signal in R: max_ret = rollapply(daily_returns, 21, max). Short high-MAX stocks (lottery-like KOSDAQ names); the expected return is negative net of the lottery premium investors pay. Combine with low analyst coverage and high retail volume for strongest signal. This is a high-priority factor for Korean KOSDAQ universe.
