# P011: Biased Assimilation and Attitude Polarization (1979)

## Core Finding
Lord, Ross & Lepper demonstrate that people with strong prior beliefs evaluate identical mixed evidence in a biased manner — accepting confirming evidence at face value while subjecting disconfirming evidence to critical scrutiny. Exposure to the same body of evidence increases, rather than decreases, polarization of opposing views. Capital punishment study: both pro and anti groups rated confirming studies as better designed and more convincing.

## Key Metrics
- Alpha/Return: N/A (social psychology experiment)
- Statistical strength: Experimental with attitude polarization measured; Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 1979
- Sample: Stanford University students, pro- and anti-capital punishment groups

## Korea Applicability
MEDIUM — Biased assimilation explains analyst behavior around Korean earnings seasons: analysts who are bullish/bearish maintain their views despite contradictory evidence, creating prolonged mispricings. Strategies exploiting analyst revision momentum (direction of analyst target price changes) work because of the lagged, biased incorporation of evidence.

## Strategy Implication
Analyst revision momentum factor: rank stocks by direction and magnitude of analyst EPS estimate revisions over the past 1-3 months. Biased assimilation predicts that analyst revisions are gradual and directional — they move in the same direction as earnings surprises over multiple periods. Signal construction: delta_EPS_estimate = (current_consensus_EPS - EPS_estimate_3m_ago) / abs(EPS_estimate_3m_ago). Long top quintile, short bottom quintile. Can be proxied using DART financial disclosure data.
