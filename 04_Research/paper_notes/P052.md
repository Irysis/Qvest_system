---

## Core Claim

Mutual funds as a whole do NOT exploit the accruals anomaly — their average accrual investing measure (AIM) is neutral (5.55, close to the midpoint of 5.50). However, a subset of funds with the lowest AIM (D1 decile = most actively tilted toward low-accrual stocks) do earn significantly positive abnormal returns of ~2% per year before fees and ~2.1% per year after fees. These funds deliberately trade to maintain low-accrual portfolios, but their actual strategy is "relatively low accruals" (overweight P2-P3 deciles) rather than pure P1 (extreme low accruals).

---

## Factor Definition

**Accruals Investing Measure (AIM) — Portfolio-Level Accrual Score:**
```
AIM_{i,t} = sum_{j=1}^{N} w_{i,j,t} * AccRank_{j,t}
```
Where:
- `AccRank_{j,t}` = decile rank (1–10) of stock j based on Sloan (1996) accruals for fiscal year ending in t−1
- `w_{i,j,t}` = portfolio weight of stock j in fund i at June-end of year t
- Low AIM = fund tilts toward low-accrual (low-rank) stocks

**Individual Stock Accruals (Sloan 1996 measure):**
```
Accruals = [(ΔCA - ΔCash) - (ΔCL - ΔSTD - ΔTP) - DEP] / ATA
```
(Compustat items: ΔCA=#4, ΔCash=#1, ΔCL=#5, ΔSTD=#34, ΔTP=#71, DEP=#14, ATA=avg of #6)

**Portfolio Construction:**
- 99,871 firm-year observations, 1984–2003
- 2,587 unique mutual funds
- Fund deciles sorted annually on AIM
- D1 (lowest AIM) = most accrual-anomaly-aware funds

## Key Empirical Results

- **Accrual anomaly exists:** P1-P10 return spread = **12.31%** annualized (t=4.33) in CRSP universe; **12.46%** (t=4.19) for mutual fund stocks — anomaly is NOT smaller for large-cap mutual fund holdings
- **Mutual funds as a whole:** Average AIM = 5.55 ≈ neutral; cannot reject hypothesis that funds do not trade on accruals
- **D1 funds (active accrual traders):**
  - Net return before fees: 15.31% vs INACTIVE funds: 13.19% (spread: +2.22%)
  - 3-factor alpha before fees: +2.83% (t=2.81)
  - 4-factor alpha before fees: +2.68% (t=2.71)
  - Net return after fees: +2.16% (t=2.07)
  - **D1 funds earn positive alpha even after transaction costs and fees**
- **D1 trading behavior:** Deliberately maintain low-accrual exposure (actual AIM stays below passive AIM); correlation between ΔAccRank and Δweight = −3.71% (t=−4.58) — actively buying falling-accrual stocks
- **D1 strategy is NOT pure P1:** D1 funds overweight P2-P3 (12-18% allocation) more than P1 (6%) — they avoid tiny illiquid P1 stocks
- Key size insight: D1 funds with D1 AIM prefer larger P2-P3 low-accrual stocks over P1 to avoid liquidity costs

## Actionable Strategy Ideas

1. **Liquidity-Adjusted Accrual Portfolio:** Do NOT concentrate in the extreme P1 (lowest) accrual decile. Instead, go long P2–P3 (low but not extreme accruals) and short P9–P10 (high accruals). This is more practical and avoids the liquidity trap.

```r
# Liquidity-adjusted accrual portfolio
# Focus on P2-P3 long, P9-P10 short
accrual_liquidity_adj <- stocks %>%
  mutate(acc_decile = ntile(BS_ACC, 10)) %>%
  filter(
    # Long: 2nd and 3rd lowest accrual deciles (liquid low-accrual)
    acc_decile %in% c(2, 3) |
    # Short: 9th and 10th highest accrual deciles
    acc_decile %in% c(9, 10)
  ) %>%
  mutate(
    direction = if_else(acc_decile <= 3, 1L, -1L)
  )
```

2. **AIM-Based Fund Monitoring:** If Korean fund holdings data is available (quarterly 13-F equivalent from FSS), compute AIM for Korean mutual funds to identify which funds actively exploit the accrual anomaly. These funds can serve as smart money signal.

3. **Accrual + Size Double Sort:** Sort by accrual rank then by size within each accrual decile. Overweight larger stocks in the low-accrual deciles (better liquidity, more realistic to implement). Avoid tiny KOSDAQ stocks in P1.
