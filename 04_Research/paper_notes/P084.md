# P084: Factors — Theory, Statistics, and Practice (Ross 2017)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Theory / Methodology
**Relevance to Korea pipeline:** Medium

## Core Claim
Stephen Ross (inventor of APT) warns against the "factor zoo" proliferation: the tendency to keep adding factors is irresistible but dangerous. A legitimate factor must satisfy two criteria: (1) it explains differential cross-sectional returns, AND (2) it explains the covariance matrix of returns. APT's advantage over CAPM is that it requires only absence of arbitrage, not rational investors — but it still requires theoretical grounding, not just empirical fitting. Pure data mining without theory will produce spurious factors.

## Key Findings (specific percentages, survival rates, which anomalies)
- **Two criteria for a legitimate factor (APT)**:
  1. Factor explains differential cross-sectional returns (average return differences across stocks)
  2. Factor explains the covariance structure of returns (systematic risk loadings)
  - A characteristic that only satisfies criterion 1 is NOT a factor — it may be a stock characteristic
- **APT vs CAPM distinction**:
  - CAPM: derived from rational investor equilibrium → vulnerable to behavioral violations
  - APT: derived from absence of arbitrage → more robust, doesn't require rational investors
  - But APT still requires factors to be economically meaningful, not just statistical constructs
- **Factor zoo problem**: "the tendency to add factors is irresistible" — each researcher adds their favorite
- Endorses **Harvey, Liu, Zhu (2016)** higher t-stat hurdles:
  - Naive 5% threshold (t=1.96) too low given multiple testing across papers
  - Suggested hurdle: t ≥ 3.0 for new factors (accounting for data mining across papers)
- **On momentum**: "After decades of searching without compelling answers, we cannot be entirely comfortable that empirical results are either valid or enduring"
  - Ross is SKEPTICAL of momentum despite its empirical robustness
  - Key concern: no compelling APT-style theoretical explanation after 30+ years
- **Sovereign bond market**: 3 factors explain 96% of cross-correlation matrix — minimal factor set needed
- **For equities**: Fama-French factors (market, size, value, momentum) + some additional = sufficient
- Key quote: "We still need theory as well as statistics"
- Distinguishes factors (priced, explain covariance) from characteristics (predict returns but don't load on systematic risk)

## Factors That SURVIVE Post-Publication
- **Market (beta)**: survives — both criteria met (differential returns + covariance)
- **Size (SMB)**: survives — both criteria met
- **Value (HML)**: survives — both criteria met
- **Profitability (RMW)**: emerging consensus; meets criteria
- **Investment (CMA)**: q-factor logic supports both criteria

## Factors That DECAY Post-Publication
- **Momentum**: passes criterion 1 (explains returns) but criterion 2 is debated; Ross skeptical
- Most "zoo" factors: pass criterion 1 only, fail criterion 2 — these are characteristics, not factors
- Any factor without theoretical foundation: likely to decay as it's a statistical artifact

## Korea/International Applicability
- The APT framework is universal — no US-specific data
- Ross's two-criteria test is the correct filter for Korean factor selection
- Apply Ross test to each candidate Korean factor: does it explain covariance matrix (i.e., stocks with high loading move together)?
- Korean application: covariance-explaining factors for Korea include: market beta, size (KOSPI vs KOSDAQ), sector, leverage
- The "characteristic vs factor" distinction matters for Korean implementation:
  - Accruals: explains cross-sectional returns (criterion 1) but is it priced risk (criterion 2)?
  - Evidence suggests: characteristic, not factor → alpha is behavioral/informational, not risk-based
  - This means it can be arbitraged away more easily (no compensation for risk-taking)

## Actionable Implications for Our Pipeline
1. **Ross filter for every new factor**: apply two criteria before adding to pipeline
   - Q1: Does it explain cross-sectional return differences? (run Fama-MacBeth regression)
   - Q2: Does it load on a systematic risk dimension? (compute factor covariance contribution)
2. **Factors that fail criterion 2** (characteristics only): use but monitor for decay — they are behavioral anomalies, not permanent risk premia
3. **Momentum skepticism**: consistent with our pipeline philosophy (no raw price momentum); use RESIDUAL momentum (after regime adjustment) to address Ross's theoretical critique
4. **t-stat hurdles**: use t ≥ 3.0 threshold for new Korean factors (Harvey, Liu, Zhu standard endorsed by Ross)
5. **Minimum factor set**: Market + Size + Value + Profitability + Investment = 5 factors. Everything beyond this requires justification.
6. For ensemble_engine.R: implement Ross test as a factor validation check:
   - Fama-MacBeth cross-sectional t-stat (criterion 1 check)
   - Factor covariance contribution check (criterion 2 check)
7. **APT framework**: our residual decomposition philosophy (SOUL.md) is consistent with APT — decompose returns into systematic factor loadings + residual alpha

## Notes
- Paper: Ross S.A. (2017), "Factors — Theory, Statistics, and Practice", Journal of Portfolio Management Special Issue 2017, pp. 1-5 (complete 5-page paper)
- Title in registry: "Factors_Steve_Ross_JPM_2017.pdf" — P084
- Stephen Ross passed away March 3, 2017 — this JPM Special Issue was in his honor, and this paper was one of his last publications
- 5-page editorial/essay format — not empirical, purely theoretical perspective from APT founder
- The paper's value is methodological GUARDRAILS rather than empirical findings
- Ross's momentum skepticism: note that Fama (of Fama-French) has also expressed ambivalence about momentum theory — two most respected asset pricing theorists both lack compelling theory for momentum
- The characteristic vs factor distinction is the deepest theoretical point — valuable for pipeline design:
  - Anomalies (accruals, gross profitability) are likely CHARACTERISTICS that predict returns via behavioral channels
  - This explains why they persist (risk-based arbitrageurs don't HAVE to trade them away)
  - But also explains why they're vulnerable to hedge fund learning (once known, anyone can trade)
