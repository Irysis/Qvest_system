# P131: Low Risk Stocks Outperform within All Observable Markets of the World (Baker & Haugen 2012)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Low_Volatility
**Relevance to STR_011 improvement:** Critical

## Core Claim
The low-risk anomaly (low-risk stocks earn HIGHER returns than high-risk stocks) holds universally across ALL 33 developed and emerging markets studied. No country is an exception. The anomaly is explained by agency theory: manager compensation structures (option-like bonuses, career risk) create institutional demand for high-volatility stocks, which overprices them.

## Factor Construction (exact formula if different from basic idio-vol)
**Methodology (intentionally simple and replicable):**
- Sorting variable: 24-month trailing total return volatility (annualized standard deviation of monthly returns)
- Universe: top 99.5% of market capitalization in each country (excludes micro-caps)
- Non-survivors INCLUDED (no survivorship bias)
- Period: January 1990 to December 2011 (264 months, 22 years)
- Portfolio formation: monthly, stocks ranked by 24-month vol into deciles/quintiles/halves
- Return measurement: total return for each decile the following month
- Rebalancing: monthly (re-rank each month, observe next month's return)

**Key measure:** Lowest Risk Decile minus Highest Risk Decile for:
1. Risk Difference (lower risk in low-vol decile — "risk is persistent")
2. Return Difference (higher return in low-vol decile)
3. Sharpe Ratio Difference (much higher Sharpe in low-vol decile)

## Key Results
**Developed Markets (Figure 1, 21 countries, 1990-2011):**
- In ALL 21 developed countries: Low-vol decile has LOWER risk than high-vol decile
- In ALL 21 developed countries: Low-vol decile has HIGHER return than high-vol decile
- In ALL 21 developed countries: Low-vol decile has SUBSTANTIALLY HIGHER Sharpe ratio

**Emerging Markets (Figure 4, 12 countries, 1990-2011):**
- KOREA is explicitly included and shows one of the HIGHEST Sharpe ratio differences
- In ALL 12 emerging markets: risk-return relationship is inverted
- Korea bars in Figure 4 show: Risk Difference (negative — low-vol has lower risk), Return Difference (positive — low-vol has higher return), Sharpe Ratio Difference (large positive)
- The Korean Sharpe ratio difference is among the top 3-4 emerging markets

**Turnover:**
- US: average turnover = 84% per year
- All Countries universe: average turnover = 96% per year
- Implication: monthly rebalancing with 24-month vol lookback generates high turnover — may need frequency reduction

**Persistence of risk:**
- Past 24-month volatility is a strong predictor of future volatility (first bar in Figure 1/4)
- Risk is the most persistent characteristic in the cross-section

**Agency theory explanation:**
- Manager compensation is option-like (base salary + bonus for outperformance)
- Expected compensation increases with portfolio volatility (wider distribution = higher bonus probability)
- Committee-based stock selection: analysts pick "noteworthy" (high-vol, high-media) stocks to impress CIO
- Evidence:
  - Institutional ownership is HIGHER for more volatile stocks (Figure 6, all size deciles except smallest)
  - Analyst coverage is SIGNIFICANTLY HIGHER for volatile stocks (Table 1: t-stat 33.6 for All Years)
  - News coverage is MORE INTENSE for volatile stocks (Table 2: t-stat 30.8 for All Years)
