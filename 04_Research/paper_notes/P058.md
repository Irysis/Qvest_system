## Core Claim

The asset growth (AG) anomaly documented by Cooper et al. (2008) for the US exists in **international equity markets** — but the critical finding for implementation is HOW and WHY its magnitude varies across countries. The paper tests two competing explanations:

1. **Optimal Investment (q-theory):** AG effect should be STRONGER in more informationally efficient markets (prices more accurately reflect investment fundamentals)
2. **Mispricing:** AG effect should be STRONGER in markets where stocks are LESS efficiently priced and arbitrage is MORE costly

**Key finding:** The AG effect is **stronger in more developed, more informationally efficient markets** (lower R2, higher FERC, developed market status, larger stock market). This is INCONSISTENT with the mispricing hypothesis and CONSISTENT with the optimal investment explanation. In contrast, limits-to-arbitrage measures (idiosyncratic volatility, trading volume, short-sale restrictions) have little explanatory power for cross-country variation in AG effect.

**Implication for Korea:** Korea is a **developed market** (DEV=1) with moderate stock market importance (MKT) but relatively high stock return synchronicity (R2) compared to US/Europe. The AG effect should exist in Korea but be **weaker** than in the US due to lower information efficiency (higher R2 → less firm-specific information in prices → q-theory mechanism less effective).

## Factor Definition (exact formula)

**Asset Growth Rate (AG) — same as Cooper et al. 2008:**
```
AG = (Total Assets_{t-1} - Total Assets_{t-2}) / Total Assets_{t-2}
```
Where t is the calendar year of portfolio formation, measured from the fiscal year ending in calendar year t.

**Worldscope data item:** Total assets = item 02999 (in local currency)

**Portfolio formation:** End of June year t, based on AG observed at that time. One-year holding period July t to June t+1. Annual rebalancing.

**Country-level AG effect measures:**
- **SPREAD** = annual return difference between bottom and top AG bucket portfolios (equal- and value-weighted)
- **SLOPE** = negative one times the coefficient from annual Fama-MacBeth regression of annual returns on AG within each country

**Profitability-controlled measure (SPREAD2, SLOPE2):**
- Double-sort on ROE quintiles × AG quintiles, compute spread within each ROE quintile
- Controls for the q-theory prediction that investment-return relation is conditional on profitability

## Key Results (returns, t-stats, IC)

**Data:** 54 countries, July 1982 - June 2010, Datastream-Worldscope. 291,725 firm-year obs including US; 222,418 excluding US. Standard: 30-stock minimum per country-year → 43-country restricted sample.

**Global AG Effect (Table 2, Panel C):**
| Measure | EW SPREAD | t-stat | VW SPREAD | t-stat |
|---------|-----------|--------|-----------|--------|
| Global (43 countries, incl. US) | 6.10% | 3.82 | 4.17% | 1.90 |
| Global excl. US | 6.43% | 3.79 | 4.04% | 1.71 |
| Country-neutral | 3.55% | 3.94 | 3.77% | 2.22 |
| Country-neutral excl. US | 3.50% | 3.50 | 3.81% | 2.24 |

All significant at 1-5% level. Effect is pervasive internationally — does NOT depend on the US.

**Robustness to size, BM, momentum controls (Table 3):**
- AG coefficient remains significantly negative (-0.027, t = -3.27 EW) after controlling for size, BM, momentum
- Effect holds over 3 years (first year: t = -3.27, second year: t = -2.77, third year: t = -2.41 EW)
- Profitability-controlled (ROE double sort, Table 4): effect persists within ALL 5 ROE quintiles, spreads range 5.41% - 6.31% (EW)

**Country-level results for ASIA — DEVELOPED (Table 2, Panel A, most relevant for Korea):**
| Country | AGSPREAD | t-stat | EW SPREAD (LC) | SLOPE (LC) |
|---------|----------|--------|----------------|------------|
| Hong Kong | 139.76 | 8.60 | 3.31 (0.88) | 11.22 (1.86) |
| Japan | 45.14 | 21.62 | 0.55 (0.22) | 2.95 (0.41) |
| Singapore | 100.26 | 11.18 | 3.28 (0.62) | 2.28 (0.43) |
| **South Korea** | **102.88** | **11.15** | **9.21 (2.19)** | **12.44 (2.84)** |
| Taiwan | 84.24 | 11.35 | -7.23 (-0.56) | -25.98 (-1.18) |

**South Korea specific results:**
- AGSPREAD = 102.88 (high cross-sectional variation in AG rates)
- EW SPREAD = **9.21%/year** (t = 2.19) — statistically significant at ~5%
- EW SLOPE = **12.44** (t = 2.84) — statistically significant at 1%
- Value-weighted (Panel B): VW SPREAD = 0.82 (t = 0.12) — NOT significant VW
- VW SLOPE = 14.55 (t = 2.79) — significant

**Critical Korea interpretation:**
- Equal-weighted AG effect in Korea: **~9.2% annualized return spread** — meaningful but smaller than US (13%)
- Value-weighted effect weak → AG anomaly in Korea is concentrated in smaller stocks within the KOSPI/KOSDAQ universe
- The VW SLOPE of 14.55 (t = 2.79) suggests the per-unit AG effect is actually LARGER in Korea than in some developed markets once you control for cross-sectional variation in AG

**Cross-Country Analysis (Table 7) — Market Efficiency and AG effect:**
- Lower R2 (more informative prices) → STRONGER AG effect (consistent, t = -2.15 EW, -2.12 VW)
- Higher FERC (more future earnings in prices) → stronger AG effect (t = 2.12 EW)
- Developed market status → stronger AG effect (t = 2.35 EW)
- Larger stock market → stronger AG effect (t = 2.42 EW)
- These findings support OPTIMAL INVESTMENT explanation over mispricing

**Limits-to-arbitrage proxies (Table 9):**
- Idiosyncratic volatility (IRISK): marginal explanatory power (t = ~1.5)
- Trading volume (DVOL): consistently insignificant
- Short-sale restriction (SHORT): consistently insignificant
- **Conclusion: Arbitrage frictions do NOT explain cross-country AG effect variation**

## Korea Applicability

**Applicability: DIRECT and QUANTIFIED.**

This paper provides the most important calibration data for implementing AG in Korea:

1. **Korea EW effect is confirmed at ~9.2%/year** (t = 2.19 on SPREAD, t = 2.84 on SLOPE). This is approximately 70% of the US magnitude — better than the 40-60% expected for a code-law country under the mispricing hypothesis.

2. **The EW vs VW divergence is critical.** VW SPREAD is insignificant while VW SLOPE is significant. This suggests:
   - The AG anomaly in Korea is partially concentrated in smaller stocks
   - But the SLOPE result indicates AG has predictive power even accounting for market cap differences
   - For a concentrated 20-30 stock portfolio, focus on mid-to-large stocks where VW SLOPE is informative

3. **Why Korea has stronger-than-expected AG effect:**
   - Korea is a "developed market" (DEV=1) — this is the main driver of AG strength per Table 7
   - Korea's stock market is important to the economy (MKT is moderate-high) — also positive for AG
   - However, Korea has relatively HIGH stock return synchronicity (R2 ~0.47 in this dataset) — this works AGAINST AG strength
   - Net result: moderate-to-strong AG effect, which is exactly what the data shows

4. **Optimal investment interpretation.** The finding that AG works through the q-theory/optimal investment channel (not mispricing) has a critical implication: AG is not an "easy to arbitrage away" anomaly but reflects fundamental investment economics. This makes it more robust and durable for strategy purposes.

5. **DART data implementation:** Identical to Cooper et al. 2008 — use `자산총계` (total assets) from annual consolidated financial statements. Note that Worldscope uses consolidated data for Korean firms.

6. **Double-sort with ROE:** The paper shows AG effect persists within ROE quintiles. For Korean implementation, a double-sort of AG × ROE (or RNOA) would sharpen the signal while controlling for the q-theory profitability dimension.

- NEW IDEA: **Profitability-Controlled AG (SPREAD2)** — the double-sort on ROE × AG controls for the q-theory profitability dimension. For a Korean implementation: form quintile portfolios on ROE, then within each ROE quintile sort on AG. The spread within ROE quintiles is the "pure" AG effect. This avoids inadvertently going long distressed low-growth firms.

  ```r
  # Double sort: ROE quintile x AG quintile
  # Within each ROE quintile, long low-AG, short high-AG
  double_sort_ag <- dart_fundamentals %>%
    group_by(year) %>%
    mutate(
      roe_quintile = ntile(ROE, 5),
      ag_rank = percent_rank(AG)
    ) %>%
    group_by(year, roe_quintile) %>%
    mutate(
      ag_within_roe = percent_rank(AG)
    ) %>%
    ungroup()
  # Signal: -ag_within_roe (lower within-ROE-quintile AG → more attractive)
  ```

## Notes

- This is the definitive international replication and explanation paper for the AG anomaly. The Korea data (Table 2) is directly usable for strategy calibration.
- The paper's key contribution beyond confirming international evidence is the theoretical discrimination between q-theory and mispricing. The q-theory interpretation means AG is more durable as a factor.
- The country-level analysis covers South Korea from 1982-2010 with a large cross-section (102.88 AGSPREAD = very wide distribution of asset growth rates), making the Korean estimates reliable.
- The Worldscope-based estimates use the same AG definition as Cooper et al. (2008) — direct comparability.
- Japan shows much weaker AG effect (SPREAD near zero) while Korea and South-East Asia show stronger effects. This is consistent with Japan's lower ROE environment and different investment culture.
- For the QEPM hurdle: the Korean AG factor has t > 2 on SLOPE, sufficient for inclusion in a multi-factor model.
