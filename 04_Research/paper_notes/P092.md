# P092: Taming the Factor Zoo (Feng, Giglio, Xiu 2020)
**Category:** Anomaly_Research / Factor_Selection / Methodology
**Relevance to Korea pipeline:** High

## Core Claim
Using double-selection LASSO — a machine learning method that controls for multiple testing while selecting the most relevant factors — the vast majority of the 150+ proposed factors in the literature are redundant. Only a small subset of factors contain independent information after controlling for the others. SMB (small-minus-big size factor) is the single most robust factor, selected in 70%+ of specifications. Most "new" factors are subsumed by existing ones.

## Key Findings (specific percentages, survival rates, which anomalies)
- **150 candidate factors** tested in double-selection LASSO framework
- **SMB (size factor)**: most robust, selected in >70% of all specifications — the single most important factor
- Most factors are **redundant** when controlling for other factors simultaneously
- Surviving independent factors (approximate from paper):
  - SMB (size): >70% selection rate
  - HML (value): significant selection rate
  - UMD/WML (momentum): significant
  - RMW (profitability): significant but partially subsumed
  - CMA (investment): significant
  - A small number of anomaly-based factors beyond FF5
- Double-selection LASSO: two-stage approach
  - Stage 1: LASSO selects which factors are related to the anomaly
  - Stage 2: LASSO selects which factors are related to the test asset returns
  - Only factors selected in BOTH stages are included → removes spurious correlations
- Key result: after proper variable selection, the "factor zoo" collapses to ~5-10 truly independent factors
- Most characteristic-sorted portfolios are explained by this small core factor set
- Publication bias check: factors published in top journals still mostly redundant — journal prestige does NOT guarantee independent factor content

## Factors That SURVIVE Post-Publication
- **SMB (Size)**: most robust single factor
- **HML (Value)**: survives in most specifications
- **UMD/Momentum**: survives but weaker than SMB/HML
- **RMW (Profitability)**: survives with some redundancy vs momentum
- **CMA (Investment/Conservative Minus Aggressive)**: survives as distinct from profitability
- A small number of anomaly-based factors beyond FF5 (specific list from full paper needed)

## Factors That DECAY Post-Publication
- The majority of the 150 factors — redundant, subsumed by core factor set
- Most industry-specific anomalies: subsumed by sector + size effects
- Most technical analysis signals: subsumed by momentum
- Most accounting ratios beyond gross profitability and ROA: subsumed by RMW
- Liquidity/illiquidity anomalies: subsumed by SMB (size) effect

## Korea/International Applicability
- Method is directly applicable: can run double-selection LASSO on Korean factor zoo
- Korean market may have different ranking — SMB may be even MORE dominant given Korea's market structure (large-cap premium historically weaker, small-cap premium stronger in 2000s)
- Recommendation: run Feng-Giglio-Xiu methodology on Korean factor candidates to identify truly independent signals
- The 5-factor model (FF5: market, SMB, HML, RMW, CMA) is the minimum viable factor set for Korea before adding anomalies
- Any new factor should be tested: "does it remain significant after controlling for FF5 + momentum?"
- LASSO approach is ideal for Korean data where sample size is smaller (N=350 vs N=3000+ in US)

## Actionable Implications for Our Pipeline
1. **Stop adding factors** until existing factors are orthogonalized — factor zoo problem already present in our pipeline
2. **FF5 + Momentum as baseline**: any new factor must survive double-selection LASSO vs these 6 factors
3. **Implement LASSO factor selection** in R using `glmnet` package:
   - Monthly cross-sectional LASSO regression of returns on all candidate factors
   - Select only consistently significant factors
4. **SMB dominance**: size exposure (KOSDAQ vs KOSPI, small vs large within KOSPI200) is more important than most exotic factors
5. **Factor orthogonalization**: compute residuals of new factors vs FF5 before using them — this is the "residual decomposition" step already in our pipeline philosophy
6. Priority hierarchy from this paper: Size > Value > Momentum > Profitability > Investment > (everything else)
7. For ensemble_engine.R: use LASSO-based factor weighting rather than equal weighting

## Notes
- Paper: Feng G., Giglio S., Xiu D. (2020), "Taming the Factor Zoo: A Test of New Factors", Journal of Finance 75(3), pp. 1327-1370
- Title in registry: "Testing_Zoo_Factors.pdf"
- Double-selection LASSO methodology is from Belloni, Chernozhukov, Hansen (2014) — adapted for asset pricing
- R implementation: `hdm` package implements double-selection LASSO (rlassoEffects function)
- Critical methodological note: standard LASSO overfits in financial data due to fat tails — double-selection corrects this by using the factor structure
- Complements Hou et al. (P080): P080 tests factors one at a time with corrected methodology; P092 tests factors jointly — both reduce the zoo, different approaches
- The LASSO approach is particularly valuable for Korean market where data length is ~20-25 years (limited sample for classical t-test based approaches)
