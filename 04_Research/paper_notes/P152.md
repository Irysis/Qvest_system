# P152: The Arbitrage Theory of Capital Asset Pricing (1976)
**Category:** Portfolio_Theory | **Priority:** 3
**Author:** Stephen A. Ross | **Journal:** Journal of Economic Theory, Vol. 13, No. 3, pp. 341-360

## Core Contribution to Factor Investing
APT is the **direct theoretical ancestor of all multi-factor models** including Fama-French, and is the most important paper for our pipeline after Markowitz. Ross proved that if asset returns can be expressed as a linear factor model, then the absence of arbitrage forces expected returns to be approximately linear in the factor loadings (betas) — without requiring market portfolio efficiency, quadratic utility, or normality of returns. This was revolutionary: it freed factor investing from CAPM's restrictive assumptions and opened the door for any set of empirically motivated factors (value, quality, momentum, etc.) to be priced risk factors.

## Key Formula/Result
Return generating process (single factor case):
```
x̃_i = E_i + β_i · δ̃ + ε̃_i
```
where δ̃ is mean-zero common factor, ε̃_i are idiosyncratic (diversifiable) errors.

APT pricing equation (arbitrage-free equilibrium):
```
E_i = ρ + λ · β_i
```
Multi-factor generalization:
```
E_i = ρ + Σ_k λ_k · β_{ik}
```
where ρ is the zero-beta (riskless) rate, λ_k is the risk premium for factor k, and β_{ik} is the loading of asset i on factor k.

Key proof mechanism: Form a well-diversified arbitrage portfolio η with ηβ = 0 (zero systematic risk) and ηe = 0 (zero cost). By law of large numbers idiosyncratic risk vanishes, so ηx̃ ≈ ηE must equal zero (no arbitrage). This forces E = ρ + λβ.
