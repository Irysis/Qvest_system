# P125: MSCI Minimum Volatility Indexes Methodology (MSCI 2018)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Low_Volatility / Portfolio_Construction
**Relevance to STR_011 improvement:** High

## Core Claim
MSCI's institutional-grade methodology for constructing minimum-volatility indexes uses constrained Markowitz optimization with the Barra risk model. The methodology balances volatility minimization against benchmark-relative risk, sector/country concentration, factor exposures, and turnover — directly applicable to production portfolio construction.

## Factor Construction (exact formula if different from basic idio-vol)
Optimization objective: Minimize portfolio total variance using Barra Equity Model covariance matrix

Constraints:
- Max weight per stock: min(1.5%, 20x parent index weight)
- Min weight per stock: 0.05%
- Country deviation from parent index: +/-5% (or 3x parent weight if parent country weight < 2.5%)
- Sector deviation from parent index: +/-5%
- Factor exposures (except Beta and Residual Volatility): +/-0.25 standard deviations from parent index
- Beta and Residual Volatility: UNCONSTRAINED (explicitly left free)
- One-way turnover cap: 10% per semi-annual review
- Full investment constraint: weights sum to 100%
- Long-only constraint

Rebalancing: Semi-annual (May and November)
Risk aversion parameters: Common factor risk aversion = 0.0075, Specific risk aversion = 0.075

## Key Results
- The MSCI methodology produces portfolios with systematically lower Beta, lower total volatility, lower idiosyncratic risk than parent index
- The constraint structure prevents excessive sector/country concentration (a common failure mode of naive min-var)
- Turnover constraint of 10% semi-annual (20% annual) is dramatically lower than unconstrained min-var (~143% annual per Clarke et al. 2006)
- Leaving Beta and Residual Volatility unconstrained is deliberate: these are the target risk factors, so constraining them would defeat the purpose
- The 0.0075/0.075 risk aversion ratio (1:10 systematic to idiosyncratic) reflects that idiosyncratic risk is more controllable and thus penalized more heavily

## STR_011 Improvement Insights
- CAGR improvement: The semi-annual rebalancing frequency (vs. STR_011's quarterly) may reduce unnecessary turnover; the turnover constraint (10% per review) could be applied to limit STR_011's rebalancing churn
- MDD improvement: The sector +/-5% constraint prevents dangerous sector concentration (e.g., all-in financials or utilities); for KOSPI200 universe this is critical given Korea's tech/sector concentration
- Sharpe improvement: The Barra-style covariance matrix optimization should theoretically dominate pure volatility ranking; however for a 350-stock universe (KOSPI200 + KOSDAQ150) a simplified covariance approach may be feasible

## Korea Applicability
- MSCI has Korea-specific Minimum Volatility Index (MSCI Korea Minimum Volatility) using this exact methodology
- Korean market has high sector concentration (semiconductors, auto, financials) making sector constraints particularly important
- The +/-5% sector constraint applied to IKS200 benchmark would prevent over-concentration in low-vol defensive sectors (utilities, healthcare, consumer staples)
- For STR_011: currently uses Idio-Vol + Beta composite; upgrading to a Barra-lite covariance approach with sector constraints would align with institutional best practice

## Actionable Modifications for STR_011
1. Add sector deviation constraint: limit each sector weight to benchmark weight +/-10% (Korean version of MSCI's +/-5%; wider given smaller universe)
2. Cap individual stock weight at min(5%, 20x benchmark weight) — MSCI's 1.5% cap is too tight for a 20-30 stock concentrated portfolio
3. Implement turnover constraint: limit one-way turnover per quarterly rebalance to 15% (scaled from MSCI's 10% semi-annual)
4. Consider semi-annual rebalancing (March and September) instead of quarterly to reduce transaction costs

## Notes
- This is the official MSCI methodology document (May 2018 version) — the most up-to-date publicly available specification
- The Beta and Residual Volatility being unconstrained is a critical insight: this means the optimizer naturally selects low-beta, low-IVOL stocks WITHOUT needing to force them
- The 0.075 specific risk aversion (10x the systematic risk aversion) implies MSCI heavily penalizes idiosyncratic volatility relative to factor risk — consistent with STR_011's dual Idio-Vol + Beta composite approach
- The Barra Equity Model uses a multi-factor structure (Value, Size, Momentum, Growth, Quality, Leverage, etc.) as risk factors — for Korean implementation, KOSPI/KOSDAQ-specific factors would be needed
