# P166: Smart Beta Mirage (2020) — SSRN 3687156
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
The largest dataset on smart beta ETF performance: 238 US-domicile smart beta ETFs listed 2000–2018. Pre-listing CAPM alpha = 2.77%/yr; post-listing alpha = -0.44%/yr (decline = 3.22%, highly significant). Returns to investors: -0.59%/yr before fees, -0.87%/yr after fees. Multi-factor smart beta ETFs are the worst case: 4.11% pre-listing alpha collapses to -0.79% post-listing. Smaller ETF sponsors show larger deterioration. A 1-SD increase in pre-listing alpha associates with +6%/month inflows — investors systematically chase backtested performance. Primary mechanism: data mining and overexploitation of backtested indices.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
Korean smart beta ETF launches follow the same pattern: backtested track records are prominently featured, backtests use the full historical period (survivorship-bias and look-ahead-bias are common), and the pre-listing period always shows strong alpha. The 3.22% average alpha decay implies that any Korean strategy showing >3% annualized alpha in backtests should be treated with deep skepticism — at least 50% of that alpha is likely data mining rather than structural edge.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. Apply the "mirage discount" in hurdle_gate.R: multiply backtest alpha by 0.5 as the out-of-sample expectation for multi-factor strategies; require that the discounted alpha still clears the 100bps hurdle threshold.
2. Implement walk-forward out-of-sample testing as a mandatory step: no strategy proceeds to strategy_registry.json with status="backtest_approved" without at least 2 years of true hold-out (post-model-development) performance data.
