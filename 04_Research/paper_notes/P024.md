# P024: Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk (1979)

## Core Finding
Kahneman & Tversky's landmark paper demonstrates that expected utility theory fails as a descriptive model of risky choice, and proposes Prospect Theory as an alternative. Key features: (1) value function defined over gains/losses from a reference point — concave for gains (risk aversion), convex for losses (risk seeking); (2) loss aversion: losses loom larger than equivalent gains (~2x asymmetry); (3) probability weighting function overweights small probabilities and underweights moderate/high probabilities; (4) certainty effect: certain outcomes receive disproportionate weight vs. probable outcomes.

## Key Metrics
- Alpha/Return: N/A (descriptive decision theory)
- Statistical strength: Systematic preference patterns across dozens of choice problems; replicated across Israeli and Swedish university faculty and students
- Sample: Multiple experiments, Econometrica March 1979

## Korea Applicability
HIGH — Prospect Theory is the most directly actionable behavioral finance foundation. Loss aversion (2:1 asymmetry) drives the disposition effect, creates momentum in assets held at gains, and explains why investors hold declining stocks too long. All major behavioral factors (momentum, value, low beta) have Prospect Theory foundations.

## Strategy Implication
Three directly codeable signals from Prospect Theory: (1) LOSS AVERSION: stocks trading below cost basis (reference point) of retail holders are held longer than optimal — these have higher subsequent volatility; (2) CERTAINTY EFFECT: dividend-paying stocks are systematically overpriced because certain dividend income is over-valued relative to equivalent capital gains — short high-dividend-yield stocks in growth regimes; (3) PROBABILITY WEIGHTING: lottery stocks (high MAX return, high skewness) are overpriced — short high-skewness KOSDAQ names. Most critical: use reference point (52-week high / 52-week low) as anchor for measuring loss/gain position of each stock in universe.
