# P151: Equilibrium in a Capital Asset Market (1966)
**Category:** Portfolio_Theory | **Priority:** 3
**Author:** Jan Mossin | **Journal:** Econometrica, Vol. 34, No. 4, pp. 768-783

## Core Contribution to Factor Investing
Mossin provided the mathematically rigorous general equilibrium derivation of CAPM that Sharpe had left partly verbal. He showed that the "market line" — relating per-dollar expected yield to standard deviation of yield — **must exist in equilibrium** when investors all maximize preference functions over (expected yield, variance of yield). His key innovation over Sharpe: he made precise the unit-of-measurement problem in portfolio analysis (Markowitz had implicitly worked in dollar units; Mossin unified this). He also formally proved that equilibrium prices are uniquely determined by the requirement that supply equals demand for every asset simultaneously. For factor investing: Mossin's equilibrium derivation establishes why **systematic risk** is the only priced risk in a competitive market — diversifiable (idiosyncratic) risk earns zero premium in equilibrium. This is the theoretical underpinning for why factor alphas must be orthogonal to the market factor to be genuine.

## Key Formula/Result
Individual's portfolio expected yield (n-1 risky assets + 1 riskless money):
```
y^i_1 = Σ_{j=1}^{n-1} μ_j · x^i_j + x^i_n    (expected yield)
y^i_2 = Σ_{j=1}^{n-1} Σ_{α=1}^{n-1} σ_{jα} · x^i_j · x^i_α    (variance)
```

Market equilibrium condition (market line slope = price of risk):
```
μ_j - q = (slope of market line) · σ_{jm}
```
where q is the riskless return and σ_{jm} is the covariance of asset j with the market portfolio. The "slope" is the market price of risk, identical across all assets in equilibrium.

Key result: The riskless asset price q serves as **numeraire** for the entire system — a point Mossin notes has profound implications for measuring equilibrium prices when risk-free rates change.

## Modern Relevance for Korea Strategy
Mossin's general equilibrium result has a direct implication for Korean factor strategies: because the KOSPI market return is the relevant pricing factor for KOSPI200 stocks, any factor signal (GPA, Accrual, ICR) must be tested **after** removing market beta exposure. Mossin's framework also alerts us to a Korean-specific issue: the Korean riskless rate (KTB 3-month) has structurally declined from ~5% (2000s) to ~3.5% (2020s), shifting q and therefore shifting the entire pricing of all risk assets. Regime changes in the risk-free rate may partially explain structural breaks in Korean factor premia.
