# P016: Knee-Deep in the Big Muddy: A Study of Escalating Commitment (1976)

## Core Finding
Staw's classic escalation-of-commitment study: 240 business students making simulated investment decisions committed MORE resources to a failing course of action when they were personally responsible for the initial decision (vs. when another person made the initial decision). The effect is largest when personal responsibility and negative consequences coincide — demonstrating the psychological mechanism behind "throwing good money after bad."

## Key Metrics
- Alpha/Return: N/A (organizational behavior experiment)
- Statistical strength: N = 240 business school students; ANOVA with significant personal responsibility x outcome interaction
- Sample: Northwestern University, 1976

## Korea Applicability
HIGH — Escalation bias is extremely common in Korean corporate decision-making (continued investment in loss-making subsidiaries, zombie companies supported by conglomerates). For investors: holding Korean conglomerate (chaebol) stocks where group-level escalation bias creates capital misallocation is a systematic drag on returns.

## Strategy Implication
Escalation bias at the corporate level manifests as continued capital investment in low-ROIC businesses. Exploitable signal: companies with declining ROIC but increasing capex (escalation pattern) are overvalued — capital is being committed to failing strategies. Signal: (capex/assets_t - capex/assets_{t-2}) > 0 AND (ROIC_t - ROIC_{t-2}) < 0. Short these escalating-commitment names; long companies that cut capex when ROIC falls (rational capital allocators). This aligns with the investment factor (asset growth) documented in Fama-French five-factor model.
