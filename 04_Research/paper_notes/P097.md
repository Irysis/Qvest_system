# P097: Smart Money, Dumb Money, and Capital Market Anomalies (Akbas, Armstrong, Sorescu, Subrahmanyam 2015)
**Category:** Anomaly_Research / Institutional_Flows
**Relevance to Korea pipeline:** High

## Core Claim
Capital flows from "dumb money" (mutual funds) EXACERBATE anomalies by flowing into mispriced stocks in the wrong direction, while "smart money" (hedge funds) ATTENUATES anomalies by trading in the correction direction. The net effect on anomaly magnitude depends on which flow type dominates. Critically, this mechanism affects GROWTH-TYPE and ACCRUAL-TYPE anomalies but NOT real investment anomalies (asset growth, net operating assets, composite equity issues, investment-to-assets).

## Key Findings (specific percentages, survival rates, which anomalies)
- **11 anomalies** from Stambaugh, Yu, Yuan (2012) composite mispricing metric
- **All 11 anomalies have significant alpha** post-publication (Table 2 Panel B):
  - Gross profitability: FF3 alpha t=3.14, FF4 alpha t=3.72
  - Momentum: FF4 alpha t=4.37
  - Return on assets: FF3 t=1.82, FF4 t=2.45
  - Net stock issues: significant
  - Total accruals: significant
  - Ohlson O-score (financial distress): significant
  - Failure probability: significant
  - Investment-to-assets: significant
  - Net operating assets: significant
  - Asset growth: significant
  - Composite equity issues: significant
- **Mutual fund flows (dumb money)** — negatively and significantly related to anomaly returns for:
  - ROA, Ohlson O-score, failure probability, gross profitability, net stock issues, total accruals, **momentum** (7 of 11)
  - These are the GROWTH-TYPE and ACCRUAL-TYPE anomalies
- **Mutual fund flows NOT significant** for real investment anomalies:
  - Investment-to-assets, net operating assets, asset growth, composite equity issues (4 of 11)
- **Hedge fund flows (smart money)**:
  - NOT significantly related to individual anomalies
  - ARE significantly related to the **composite mispricing metric** (they trade on the basket)
  - Trade more on anomalies known BEFORE 1997 (old, established anomalies) than recently published ones
- Composite mispricing metric (equally-weighted rank of all 11 anomalies) outperforms individual anomaly selection
- Dumb money effect is strongest in periods of HIGH mutual fund inflows (growth/tech boom periods)
- When dumb money inflows are high: anomalies are LARGER (mispricing is more extreme)
- When dumb money inflows are low: anomalies are moderate
- Hedge funds: trade on composites, partial correction only (don't fully eliminate mispricing)

## Factors That SURVIVE Post-Publication
- **All 11 Stambaugh-Yu-Yuan anomalies survive** (all have significant alpha in FF3/FF4 models)
- **Real investment anomalies** are most robust (immune to dumb money effects):
  - Investment-to-assets (capex/assets)
  - Net operating assets (balance sheet bloat)
  - Asset growth
  - Composite equity issues
- **Gross profitability** (t=3.14 FF3, 3.72 FF4): robust, significant
- **Momentum** (t=4.37 FF4): strong individual anomaly alpha
- Composite signal: most robust of all (hedge funds trade composites, which partially corrects them, but not fully)

## Factors That DECAY Post-Publication
- Accrual-type anomalies: partially corrected by dumb-money withdrawal during down markets
- Growth-type anomalies: dumb money flows in wrong direction, making them larger initially then potentially more volatile
- No anomaly in this paper's 11 fully decays to zero — all remain significant

## Korea/International Applicability
- Korea has HIGH mutual fund retail flows (bank-sold funds, ETF growth) — "dumb money" effect may be larger
- Korean mutual fund industry: mostly equity long-only funds with retail money → classic "dumb money" behavior
- This means: **GROWTH-TYPE and ACCRUAL-TYPE anomalies may be LARGER in Korea** (more dumb money exacerbating mispricing)
- Korean hedge fund sector: small relative to total market → composite mispricing signal less corrected
- IMPLICATION: the Stambaugh-Yu-Yuan composite is likely STRONGER in Korea than in US
- Specific Korean factors: accruals, ROA, gross profitability should be tested — dumb money in Korea likely flows to high-accrual, growth-story stocks
- DART data provides quarterly accruals and ROA → directly applicable
- Composite mispricing approach: build Korean equivalent with all 9-11 signals available from DART

## Actionable Implications for Our Pipeline
1. **Build Stambaugh-Yu-Yuan composite** as the primary fundamental factor layer:
   - Average percentile ranks of: ROA, gross profitability, accruals, net stock issues, investment-to-assets, asset growth, O-score (financial distress), momentum
   - This directly addresses the "smart money trades composites" finding
2. **Real investment anomalies** (asset growth, capex/assets, net operating assets) are the MOST STABLE factors:
   - Not affected by dumb money flows → more predictable
   - Include these as "core stable" layer vs "cyclical layer" of growth/accrual anomalies
3. **Composite vs individual**: always use composite of fundamentals rather than single factor
4. **Mutual fund flow regime**: when Korean equity fund inflows are high (growth phase), accrual/growth anomalies are larger — use as timing signal
5. **Gross profitability** (t=3.72 FF4 adjusted): strongest individual signal — calculate as (Revenue - COGS) / Total Assets from DART
6. For fundamental_dart.parquet: ensure accruals (balance sheet method: NOA change), net issuance (diluted shares change), and gross profitability are all computed
7. **Financial distress signal (O-score or Z-score)**: already partially captured; ensure it's in composite
8. Hedge funds trade OLD anomalies (pre-1997) on composite basis — these are the classic factors; don't over-engineer
