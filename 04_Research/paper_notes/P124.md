# P124: Are Hedge Funds on the Other Side of the Low-Volatility Trade? (Blitz 2018)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Low_Volatility
**Relevance to STR_011 improvement:** High

## Core Claim
Hedge funds systematically bet AGAINST low-volatility stocks (i.e., they are long high-vol and short low-vol), not exploiting the anomaly. This is strong evidence that the LowVol anomaly is NOT overcrowded and will persist. The LV-HV factor is among the strongest explanatory factors for aggregate hedge fund returns.

## Factor Construction (exact formula if different from basic idio-vol)
LV-HV factor construction:
- Sorting variable: 36-month total return volatility (3-year lookback, monthly returns)
- Portfolio: value-weighted
- Cutoffs: bottom 30% (lowest vol) vs. top 30% (highest vol) of each month's cross-section
- Long: low-volatility stocks (bottom 30%)
- Short: high-volatility stocks (top 30%)
- Rebalancing: monthly
- Factor return: LV portfolio return minus HV portfolio return

Key result: HFRI Fund Weighted Composite Index has t-statistic of -7.75 for LV-HV factor exposure — meaning hedge funds load NEGATIVELY on this factor (they are on the short-LV, long-HV side).

## Key Results
- Hedge funds have significant negative exposure to LV-HV factor (t = -7.75 overall)
- This negative exposure is stable across subsamples: 2000-2008 (t = -5.13) and 2008-2016 (t = -5.72)
- Result holds for multiple HFRI sub-strategies:
  - Fund Weighted Composite: t = -7.75
  - Equity Hedge: t = -6.19
  - Event Driven: t = -4.55
  - Relative Value: t = -3.89
  - Macro (weakest): still negative
- The LV-HV factor is one of the strongest factors explaining hedge fund returns (alongside market, size, value, momentum)
- Since hedge funds represent sophisticated "smart money", their systematic bet against LowVol confirms the anomaly is NOT arbitraged away
- Implication: Institutional demand for high-vol stocks from hedge funds keeps high-vol stocks overpriced and low-vol stocks underpriced

## STR_011 Improvement Insights
- CAGR improvement: The anti-crowding evidence supports maintaining concentrated LowVol positions — there is no alpha decay risk from crowding that would justify reducing the factor tilt
- MDD improvement: During stress periods when hedge funds deleverage (e.g., 2008-2009), their forced selling of high-vol / covering of low-vol positions BENEFITS low-vol strategies — natural MDD hedge
- Sharpe improvement: The persistence of hedge fund anti-LowVol positioning is a structural supply-demand dynamic that will continue to provide premium to LowVol strategies
