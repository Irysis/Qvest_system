# P203: Dynamic Portfolio Management with Views at Multiple Horizons (2015)
**Category:** Risk_Portfolio | **Priority:** 3
**Authors:** Attilio Meucci, Marco Nicolosi | **Revision:** April 16, 2015

## Core Thesis
Introduces **Dynamic Entropy Pooling (DEP)** — a framework for combining views with different time horizons (minutes to years) into a single dynamic portfolio allocation. The key advance over existing methods: prior approaches (Grinold 1989, Black-Litterman 1990, Entropy Pooling 2008) handle either discretionary views OR multi-period optimization but not both simultaneously. DEP handles all three dimensions of complexity: discretionary (non-systematic) views, multi-period optimization, and market impact.

The approach: (1) Model market risk drivers as a multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (prior). (2) Express views/stress-tests as constraints on the distribution of risk drivers at arbitrary times. (3) Use Entropy Pooling (minimum relative entropy / Kullback-Leibler divergence) to compute the posterior distribution. (4) Optimize a discounted P&L objective function via dynamic programming with time-dependent Bellman equations.

**Key insight:** The framework can embed signals with lifespans from intraday to macroeconomic — a short-term mean-reversion signal (days) and a long-term value signal (years) can coexist in the same portfolio optimization without contradiction.

## Key Quantitative Results
- For no-market-impact case: DEP reduces to a sequence of simple mean-variance optimizations, each reflecting a different multi-horizon view set — analytically tractable
- With market impact: Solve backward Bellman recursion with time-dependent coefficients; coincides with Garleanu-Pedersen (2013) in the special case of no discretionary views
- Multi-period objective: Discounted expected P&L penalized by risk aversion (γ) and market impact term
- Optimal position without market impact:
  ```
  b*_t = (1/2γ)(σ²)^{-1} · μ
  ```
  (standard Markowitz) when views are absent; distorted toward views when Entropy Pooling posterior differs from prior

## Application to Korean Factor Strategy
The DEP framework has direct applicability to `ensemble_engine.R` for combining signals with different refresh frequencies:

1. **Multi-horizon signal blending**: Our pipeline currently uses monthly rebalancing with all signals evaluated at the same horizon. DEP enables formally combining:
   - **Short horizon (1-4 weeks)**: Price momentum, short-term reversal, technical signals
   - **Medium horizon (1-3 months)**: Earnings momentum, analyst revision signals
   - **Long horizon (6-12 months)**: Value (GPA, ROE, Accrual), quality (ICR), structural regime

2. **Entropy Pooling for view integration**: The macro regime signals from `macro_regime.parquet` (VIX, YC, HY Spread, KRW) can be treated as "views" in the Entropy Pooling sense — they constrain the distribution of future factor returns rather than providing point forecasts. This is more principled than the current hard-gate regime logic.

3. **Ornstein-Uhlenbeck as factor return model**: Model each Korean factor return as a mean-reverting OU process:
   ```
   dF_t = θ(μ - F_t)dt + σ dW_t
   ```
   where θ is the mean-reversion speed estimated from Korean factor time series. This gives a principled forecast of factor returns at different horizons.

4. **Dynamic rebalancing with transaction costs**: The Bellman-equation framework for market impact is directly applicable to Korean market where transaction costs (bid-ask + market impact) are significant for a 20-30 stock concentrated portfolio.
