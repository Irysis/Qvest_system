## 1. Core Thesis

This is a practitioner interview documenting the intellectual origins of the BARRA risk model and Barr Rosenberg's broader contributions to quantitative asset management. The key thesis is that fundamental accounting descriptors — leverage, earnings quality, growth, payout, B/P, E/P — systematically predict both systematic risk (beta) and specific risk (idiosyncratic volatility), and that this prediction can be operationalized into a multi-factor risk model for portfolio construction, performance attribution, and risk budgeting.

Rosenberg's intellectual arc: from theoretical derivation of beta as a function of firm characteristics (JFQA 1973) → building the commercial BARRA model (1975) → portable alpha and factor-based portfolio engineering.

---

## 2. Factor Construction (BARRA Architecture)

**Original 39-factor risk model (1975 BARRA)**:
- Fundamental risk factors: financial leverage, earnings variability, earnings growth, earnings payout ratio, book-to-price, earnings-to-price, dividend yield, sales growth, assets
- Market-based risk factors: historical beta (60-month rolling), residual risk (specific volatility), price momentum, trading volume / share turnover
- Industry dummies: approximately 55 industries as binary indicator variables
- Factor exposures (betas / loadings) estimated via cross-sectional regression of returns on standardized descriptors

**Risk model equation**:
```
r_i = alpha_i + sum_k(X_ik * f_k) + u_i

where:
  r_i = return on stock i
  X_ik = factor exposure (standardized descriptor) of stock i on factor k
  f_k = factor return (estimated cross-sectionally)
  u_i = specific return (idiosyncratic)
```

**Covariance matrix decomposition**:
```
Sigma = X * F * X' + Delta

where:
  F = factor covariance matrix (k x k)
  Delta = diagonal matrix of specific variances
```

**Factor return estimation**: Weighted least squares (WLS) cross-sectional regression each period, weighting by market cap or inverse specific variance.

**Key insight**: By decomposing risk into factor exposures + specific, the model dramatically reduces the dimensionality of the covariance matrix estimation problem — from N*(N-1)/2 elements to k*(k-1)/2 + N.

---

## 3. Key Results / Insights

- The 39-factor model explained approximately 30-50% of cross-sectional return variance in the US (far more than a single-factor CAPM)
- Industry factors accounted for roughly 15-20% of variance, fundamental factors 15-25%
- Specific risk was found to be highly predictable from accounting descriptors (leverage, earnings variability, firm size) — confirming the JFQA 1973 results
- Performance attribution: decompose portfolio alpha into factor contributions (style tilts) + security selection (residual alpha) + interaction terms
- Portable alpha concept: separate alpha generation (active stock selection) from beta exposure (passive factor replication); combine via derivatives overlay

**Rosenberg's key philosophical point**: Risk models should be built on economic theory about what drives covariation between stocks (common economic exposures), not purely on statistical factor extraction. Statistical factors are unstable across time and hard to interpret.

## 4. Korea / International Applicability

**Directly applicable**:
- BARRA-style fundamental factor model is the gold standard for Korean quant; this interview explains the construction logic
- For KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150: can build a simplified 15-25 factor model with DART fundamentals + market descriptors
- Korea-specific considerations:
  - Chaebol structure means industry/group effects are large (horizontal conglomerates share correlated cash flows)
  - Holding company discount is a systematic factor in Korean market
  - Turnover and liquidity factors are more important in KOSDAQ than NYSE
- Performance attribution framework directly applicable to backtest attribution in `backtest_harness.R`

**Practical construction guide** (simplified BARRA for Korea):
1. Fundamental exposures: B/P, E/P, ROE, GPA, leverage (D/E), earnings variability (5yr EPS std), accrual ratio, ICR
2. Market exposures: 60-month rolling beta, 12-month residual volatility, 1-month turnover, 12-month momentum (with caution)
3. Industry: GICS sector dummies (11 sectors) or KRX industry classification
4. Factor returns: monthly WLS cross-section; Newey-West standard errors

## 5. Actionable Implications for Pipeline

1. **Factor model architecture**: Follow BARRA structure: X matrix (exposures) × F (factor covariance) × X' + Delta (specific). Store in monthly panel format in Parquet.
2. **WLS cross-sectional regression**: Use market cap as weights (or inverse of squared specific volatility) for factor return estimation. Avoid equal weighting which over-weights small illiquid stocks.
3. **Risk budgeting**: Use the BARRA framework to allocate risk budget across style factors (value, quality, low-vol) vs. industry bets. Target specific risk < 3% annualized per stock.
4. **Performance attribution**: Decompose realized alpha into: (a) factor timing contribution (systematic), (b) stock selection (residual), (c) interaction (active factor tilts × factor returns). This maps directly to `summarise_perf()` in `backtest_harness.R`.

## 6. Strategy Ideas Extracted

- **Fundamental Factor Model (Korea BARRA)**: Build a KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 factor risk model with 20 exposures (8 fundamental + 3 market + 9 industry) using DART data. Use it for both risk decomposition and alpha signal generation.
- **Risk-Aware Stock Selection**: Select stocks with high alpha (based on quality/value composite) AND low predicted specific risk (from accounting descriptors). This is the Rosenberg "residual-first" approach: maximize information ratio = alpha / specific risk.
- **Portable Alpha Construction**: Run a concentrated 20-30 stock long-short portfolio hedged to be factor-neutral (zero beta, zero industry tilts). Capture pure stock selection alpha without systematic factor exposures.
