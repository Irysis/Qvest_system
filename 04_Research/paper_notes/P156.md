# P156: Distilling the Risks of Smart Beta — Lazard Insights (2018)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Lazard identifies five distinct risk categories embedded in smart beta: (1) factor risk — the chosen factor may not persist; (2) implementation risk — index construction choices dramatically alter realized exposure; (3) valuation risk — factor premiums vary with relative cheapness; (4) crowding risk — widespread adoption erodes returns; (5) regime risk — factor performance is highly regime-dependent (value underperforms in momentum regimes, low-vol underperforms in risk-on regimes). The paper argues investors must monitor all five simultaneously.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
Korean markets exhibit stronger regime dependency than the US: factor reversals around earnings seasons (April, August) and index rebalancing windows (MSCI, KOSPI200 semi-annual) are sharper. Valuation risk is acute post-2020 growth-tech bubble in Korea, where value factor P/B spread reached historical lows. Crowding risk is amplified by the concentrated institutional base (pension funds, insurance companies) using similar smart beta products.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. Implement all five Lazard risk dimensions as monitoring checks in the ensemble_engine.R: factor return z-score vs. 3-year history, valuation ratio z-score, crowding proxy (factor AUM concentration), and regime alignment score.
2. Use the macro_regime.parquet signals (VIX, YC, HY spread) to anticipate regime shifts and conditionally reduce exposure to regime-vulnerable factors (e.g., reduce value weight when credit spreads widen sharply).
