# P102 — Amihud, Mendelson & Pedersen (2005): Liquidity and Asset Prices

**Citation:** Amihud, Y., Mendelson, H., & Pedersen, L.H. (2005). Liquidity and asset prices. *Foundations and Trends in Finance*, 1(4), 269–364.

---

## Core Thesis

Comprehensive survey of the theoretical and empirical literature linking liquidity to asset prices. Unified framework showing how illiquidity affects expected returns through three channels:
1. **Transaction cost channel** — direct cost increases required return (Amihud-Mendelson 1986)
2. **Liquidity risk channel** — covariance of returns with aggregate liquidity shocks (Pastor-Stambaugh 2003, Acharya-Pedersen 2005)
3. **Search friction channel** — OTC markets, dealer networks, counterparty search costs

Four sources of illiquidity: (a) exogenous transaction costs, (b) demand pressure and inventory risk, (c) private information/asymmetric information, (d) search frictions.

Fundamental pricing equation in liquidity-adjusted model:
    p_t = E_t[ (p_{t+1} + d_{t+1}) * m_{t+1} ]

where liquidity costs enter through the stochastic discount factor m.

---

## Factor Construction

This is a survey paper, not a primary empirical study. Synthesizes:
- **Amihud (2002) ILLIQ** = |ret| / DollarVolume (daily, averaged annually)
- **Bid-ask spread** (Amihud-Mendelson 1986)
- **Kyle (1985) lambda** — price impact per unit order flow
- **Pastor-Stambaugh gamma** — volume-related return reversal
- **Sadka (2006) permanent price impact** — decomposed into permanent and transitory components
- **Turnover** (Datar et al. 1998, Ibbotson et al. 2013)

Liquidity premia found across asset classes:
- Stocks: 3–8% per year depending on measure
- Corporate bonds: illiquid bonds yield 0.5–1.5% more
- Closed-end funds: discounts related to fund illiquidity
- Hedge funds: liquidity risk priced (Sadka 2010)

---

## Key Results

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Level effect | Less liquid stocks have higher returns (compensating illiquidity) |
| Risk effect | Stocks with high beta to aggregate liquidity earn +7.5%/yr (PS 2003) |
| Time-series | Market illiquidity predicts future market returns positively |
| Cross-asset | Illiquidity priced in bonds, equities, hedge funds |

- Amihud ILLIQ: more liquid stocks have lower ILLIQ values; premium ~3.5% annually for NYSE stocks
- Small-firm effect partly explained by illiquidity (small = illiquid)
- Equity premium puzzle partly explained: equities illiquid vs. risk-free Treasuries

---

## Korea-Specific Implications

- Survey framework applies to Korean market: KOSDAQ has historically 3-5x higher illiquidity than KOSPI200
- Both level (characteristic) and risk (covariance with market liquidity) dimensions should be tested for Korean context
- Korean liquidity crises (1997, 2008, 2020 COVID) provide natural out-of-sample tests for liquidity risk pricing
- Short-selling restrictions in Korea reduce the "flight to quality" dynamic — illiquidity premium may be more persistent

## Actionable Implementation

This paper provides the theoretical scaffolding for the entire liquidity factor module. Three implementation tiers:

1. **Screen tier** (Amihud 1986-style): exclude top illiquidity decile from universe
2. **Signal tier** (Amihud 2002-style): ILLIQ as cross-sectional return predictor
3. **Risk tier** (PS 2003-style): sensitivity to aggregate liquidity innovations as a priced risk factor

```r
# Three-tier liquidity framework
# Tier 1: Screen — remove microcap/penny stocks from universe
# Tier 2: Signal — sort on monthly ILLIQ (low ILLIQ = more liquid = lower premium)
# Tier 3: Risk — estimate beta_L = cov(r_i, delta_LIQ) / var(delta_LIQ)

compute_illiq <- function(ret, vol_krw) {
  # Amihud ILLIQ: |ret| / KRW volume (adjust scale for Korean market)
  abs(ret) / (vol_krw / 1e8)  # scale by 100M KRW
}
```
