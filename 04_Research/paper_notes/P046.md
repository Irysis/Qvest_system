# Paper Note: P046 — Leippold & Lohre (2012)
# "Data snooping and the global accrual anomaly"

**Authors:** Markus Leippold (U. Zurich), Harald Lohre (Deka Investment)
**Journal:** Applied Financial Economics (2012) — Clive Granger Best Paper Prize 2011
**Category:** Factor_Return / Accrual_Factors
**Priority:** 1

## Core Thesis
The global accrual anomaly is WEAKER than US studies suggest due to data snooping bias. Bootstrap-corrected multiple testing (Romano & Wolf 2005) across 26 countries reveals: anomaly is PERSISTENT in common-law countries (US, UK, Australia), but WEAK/ABSENT in code-law countries (Germany, France, Japan, **Korea**).

## Key Finding for Korea
**Korea monthly alpha = 0.85% (t=2.17)** — statistically significant but below US (t=3.39).

However:
- Korea = code-law country → anomaly structurally weaker
- High institutional ownership in KOSPI200 → further attenuation
- Post-2012 decay likely for code-law countries

## Method: Balance Sheet Accrual
```
ACC_bs = (ΔCA - ΔCash) - (ΔCL - ΔSTD - ΔTaxPayable) - Dep
```
Alternative CFS method (Collins & Hribar 2002):
```
ACC_cf = NI - OCF
```
CFS method is SUPERIOR for K-IFRS post-2011 (less measurement error).

## Key Numbers
- USA: α = 0.66%/mo (t=3.39) — strongest
- Korea: α = 0.85%/mo (t=2.17) — moderate, code-law discount
- Denmark: α = 1.44%/mo (t=4.10) — best in Europe
- After bootstrap correction: many countries lose significance; Korea borderline
