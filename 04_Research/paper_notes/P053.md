# P053 — Thomas & Zhang (2002)

**File:** accrual6.pdf
**Citation:** Thomas, J.K., Zhang, H. (2002). Inventory changes and future returns. *Review of Accounting Studies*, 7, 163–187.

---

## Core Claim

Within Sloan's (1996) total accrual measure, **inventory changes (ΔInventory) are the single component most strongly and consistently related to future abnormal returns.** The inventory component's hedge return (11.39%) exceeds the total accrual hedge return (9.28%). The market misinterprets inventory changes as more permanent than they actually are — consistent with demand shifts and earnings management masking impending profitability reversals.

---

## Factor Definition

**Primary Factor — Change in Total Inventory (ΔInvent):**
```
ΔInvent = Change in total inventory / Average Total Assets
```
(Compustat: inventory change from balance sheet, scaled by average TA)

**Component decomposition of Sloan's accrual:**
```
Accruals = ΔCurAss − ΔCurLia − Deprec
         = ΔAccRec + ΔInvent + OtherAcc − ΔAccPay − Deprec
```

**Sub-components of inventory:**
- ΔRM_Invent = Change in raw material inventory (hedge return: 10.82%)
- ΔWIP_Invent = Change in work-in-process inventory (hedge return: 6.45%)
- ΔFG_Invent = Change in finished goods inventory (hedge return: 9.35%)

**ΔRM (raw material) is the strongest single sub-component.**

**Mishkin (1983) test coefficients:**
- Earnings persistence of ΔInvent: 0.0236 (actual, low)
- Market-implied persistence of ΔInvent: 0.183 (market assumes much higher)
- Ratio of overestimation: 7.8× — the largest overestimation among all accrual components

**Alternative explanations tested and rejected:**
- Growth in PPE (negative, separate effect: 7.67% hedge)
- Growth in other net assets (ONA) (6.05% hedge)
- Neither subsumes the inventory effect in multivariate regressions

---

## Key Empirical Results

- Sample: 39,315 firm-years, 1970–1997, NYSE/AMEX only
- Total accrual hedge return: 9.28% (consistent with Sloan 1996)
- **ΔInventory hedge return: 11.39%** (positive in 27/28 years — remarkably consistent)
- ΔAccRec hedge: 3.67% (much weaker and less consistent)
- ΔDepreciation hedge: 4.74%
- OtherAcc (non-inventory, non-receivables accruals) hedge: 3.78%
- **ΔAccPay: −2.74%** (negative! Change in accounts payable increases accruals but predicts HIGHER future returns — opposite to simple Sloan story)
- In multivariate regression, ΔInvent dominates all other components
- Empirical regularities for extreme inventory decile firms:
  - Pre-year sales growth reverses sharply after year 0
  - Profitability reversals are masked in year 0 by earnings management
  - COGS/Sales and SG&A/Sales show unusual fourth-quarter patterns consistent with EM
  - Abnormal returns concentrated at quarterly earnings announcement windows (30%+)
- LIFO vs non-LIFO finding: LIFO firms with inventory INCREASES show abnormal patterns — LIFO liquidations partially identified by market (consistent with transparency argument)

## Korea Applicability

- **High** — Korean manufacturing companies (chaebol and mid-cap) carry large inventories, especially in electronics, autos, chemicals, and steel sectors
- Inventory management is a key operational signal for Korean manufacturers
- DART K-IFRS financial statements include separate inventory line items (raw materials, WIP, finished goods) for most large manufacturers
- The demand-shift explanation fits Korea's export-oriented economy: demand shocks from global markets create inventory imbalances that take 1–2 years to resolve
- Korean investors are likely to misread inventory buildups as demand strength rather than demand weakness

## Actionable Strategy Ideas

1. **Standalone Inventory Change Factor (highest priority new factor from this paper):** Compute ΔInvent separately and use as a standalone signal, NOT just as a component of total accruals. This is directly implementable and goes beyond STR_012.

```r
# ΔInventory factor
# Requires: inventory data from fundamental_dart.parquet
inventory_factor <- fundamentals %>%
  arrange(ticker, year) %>%
  group_by(ticker) %>%
  mutate(
    # Total inventory change scaled by average assets
    d_invent = (invt - lag(invt)) / ((at + lag(at)) / 2),
    # Sub-components if available (K-IFRS)
    d_raw_mat = (raw_material - lag(raw_material)) / ((at + lag(at)) / 2),
    d_wip = (wip - lag(wip)) / ((at + lag(at)) / 2),
    d_fg = (finished_goods - lag(finished_goods)) / ((at + lag(at)) / 2)
  ) %>%
  ungroup() %>%
  # Winsorize at 1%/99%
  mutate(across(starts_with("d_"), ~winsorize(., probs = c(0.01, 0.99))))
```

2. **Raw Material Inventory Focus:** Among sub-components, ΔRM (raw materials) has the strongest signal (10.82% hedge). For Korean manufacturers, raw material inventory changes are especially informative about demand expectations. Use ΔRM as a standalone factor where data permits.

3. **Inventory-to-Sales Ratio Change:** Supplement ΔInvent with Δ(Inventory/Sales) ratio to capture demand-mismatch signal:
```r
d_inv_sales = (invt/sale) - lag(invt/sale)
# High d_inv_sales = growing inventory relative to sales = bearish
```

4. **Earnings Announcement Interaction:** Since abnormal returns from inventory signal are concentrated at quarterly earnings announcements (30%+ of annual return in 3-day windows), time portfolio rebalancing to pre-earnings announcement windows for high-inventory-change stocks.

5. **Sector-Conditional Application:** Apply the inventory signal primarily to manufacturing sectors (SIC 2000–3999 equivalent in Korea: KSIC codes for manufacturing). For service/finance sectors, inventory is irrelevant.

## Notes

- **This paper provides the most actionable NEW factor idea in the batch:** standalone ΔInventory, not previously isolated in STR_012
- The accounts payable finding (ΔAccPay is NEGATIVE predictor) is counterintuitive — rising payables (which increase the numerator of some accrual measures) actually predict higher future returns. This suggests some accrual measures include ΔAccPay incorrectly signed.
- The LIFO/non-LIFO heterogeneity finding is US-specific — K-IFRS prohibits LIFO, so all Korean firms use FIFO or weighted average. This actually SIMPLIFIES the Korean application.
- The demand-shift explanation is consistent with Korean export-cycle dynamics
- **Feasibility: High** (inventory data available in DART)
- **Novelty vs STR_012: High** (standalone inventory change factor is new and incremental)
