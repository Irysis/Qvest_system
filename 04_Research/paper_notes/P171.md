# P171: Smart Beta versus Smart Alpha — Jacobs & Levy, JPM Summer (2014)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Systematic comparison across 8 dimensions: (1) Theory support — mixed for smart beta, stronger for smart alpha; (2) Forward-looking — smart beta is backward-looking by design, smart alpha is dynamic; (3) Diversification — smart beta is concentrated in 1–4 factors, smart alpha uses dozens of factors; (4) Unintended risks — smart beta creates sector/style biases (e.g., momentum loaded on tech pre-2000); (5) Turnover — smart alpha higher but with net gain after optimization; (6) Crowding — smart beta more exposed due to transparency and predictable rebalancing dates; (7) Transparency — smart beta advantage for governance; (8) Cost — smart alpha charges more but opportunity cost of smart beta's limited factor set is substantial.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
The transparency risk is particularly acute in Korea: KOSPI200 smart beta indices publish their constituent lists and rebalancing schedules in advance. This allows front-running by HFTs and arbitrageurs at every reconstitution — a demonstrable cost that does not appear in net-of-fee performance statistics. The concentrated factor exposure risk (dimension 3) is amplified because Korean quant strategies overwhelmingly cluster in the same 3–4 factors (value, quality, low-vol).

## Actionable Takeaway
1. Our pipeline's proprietary factor construction (residual decomposition, transfer entropy, permutation entropy) constitutes genuine "smart alpha" elements — maintain this distinctiveness and do not revert to generic published factor definitions that can be front-run.
2. Use the Jacobs-Levy framework as a positioning statement: our Maxwell Protocol strategies are designed as smart alpha (dynamic, multi-factor, regime-conditional), not smart beta — this justifies higher computational complexity and should be stated explicitly in strategy documentation.
