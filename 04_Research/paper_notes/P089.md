# P089 — An Empirical Investigation of the Arbitrage Pricing Theory

**Authors**: Richard Roll and Stephen A. Ross
**Source**: Journal of Finance, Vol. 35, No. 5 (December 1980), pp. 1073-1103
**Year**: 1980
**Category**: Factor_Return_Articles / General_Factor_Articles
**File**: `Roll_Ross_JoF_1980.pdf`

---

## 1. Core Thesis

This is the first empirical test of Ross's (1976) Arbitrage Pricing Theory (APT). The APT postulates that security returns are generated by a linear factor model, and that in the absence of arbitrage, expected returns are linear functions of factor loadings (betas). Roll and Ross apply maximum likelihood factor analysis to the covariance matrix of daily stock returns, identifying 5 empirical factors and testing whether any are significantly "priced" (carry a non-zero risk premium). The central finding is that at least 3-4 factors are significantly priced, the own-variance of a stock is NOT priced after controlling for factor loadings, and the APT cannot be rejected on this sample.

**Critical methodological innovation**: The two-pass approach — (1) estimate factor loadings via factor analysis, (2) cross-sectionally regress expected returns on loadings to estimate risk premia — became the standard template for all subsequent factor pricing tests.

---

## 2. Factor Construction

**Data**: 1,260 NYSE/AMEX stocks, daily returns July 1962 – December 1972 (approximately 2,600 trading days). Stocks grouped into 42 groups of 30 stocks each.

**Step 1 — Factor extraction (maximum likelihood factor analysis)**:
```
r_i = E_i + b_i1*delta_1 + b_i2*delta_2 + ... + b_ik*delta_k + epsilon_i

where:
  r_i = return on stock i
  E_i = expected return on stock i
  b_ij = factor loading of stock i on factor j
  delta_j = factor realization (mean zero, unit variance)
  epsilon_i = idiosyncratic return (mean zero, uncorrelated with all delta_j)
```

For each group of 30 stocks, the 30×30 covariance matrix is estimated from daily returns. Maximum likelihood factor analysis extracts loadings b_ij. Number of factors k selected by chi-square test of covariance matrix rank.

**Step 2 — Cross-sectional pricing test**:
```
E_i - E_0 = lambda_1*b_i1 + lambda_2*b_i2 + ... + lambda_k*b_ik

where:
  lambda_j = risk premium for factor j (to be estimated)
  E_0 = expected return on zero-beta portfolio
```

Test: H0: lambda_j = 0 (factor j is not priced) vs H1: lambda_j ≠ 0.
Also tests: H0: own variance (sigma^2_epsilon_i) carries additional premium beyond factor loadings.

**Statistical approach**: GLS cross-sectional regression; t-tests on lambda_j. Correlation-robust standard errors (Shanken correction not yet developed, but group structure provides partial robustness).

---

## 3. Key Results

- **Number of priced factors**: Chi-square tests support at least 3-4 priced factors across most of the 42 groups
- **Factor 1**: Virtually always significant (likely a market-like factor)
- **Factors 2-4**: Significant in majority of groups; correspond loosely to economic forces (inflation, industrial production, yield curve — not labeled in paper, later identified in Chen, Roll, Ross 1986)
- **Factor 5**: Borderline in most groups
- **Own variance**: NOT significantly priced after controlling for factor loadings in 88.1% of groups — this supports APT over total-risk pricing models
- **APT vs CAPM**: APT with 3-4 factors provides better cross-sectional fit than single-factor CAPM

**Quantitative result**: Across 42 groups, mean lambda_1 ≈ 9.9%/yr, lambda_2 ≈ 3.3%/yr, lambda_3 ≈ 4.1%/yr (annualized, approximate from reported monthly values).

## 4. Korea / International Applicability

**Foundational for Korean factor model design**:
- The APT framework is more general than CAPM — it does not require identification of the "true market portfolio." For Korean market where the market proxy (KOSPI) has chaebol concentration issues, APT is more appropriate.
- Number of factors: If 3-4 factors are priced in the US (1962-1972), Korean market likely has similar structure. A 3-5 factor model is appropriate for KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150.
- Macroeconomic factor candidates for Korea (following Chen-Roll-Ross 1986 logic):
  1. KOSPI market return (market risk)
  2. Industrial production growth (PMI-based)
  3. Yield curve (KRX 10yr - 1yr)
  4. KRW/USD exchange rate change
  5. Credit spread (KRX corporate bond - government bond)
- The maximum likelihood factor analysis methodology can be directly applied to Korean stock return covariance matrices to empirically identify the number of priced factors

## 5. Actionable Implications for Pipeline

1. **APT factor model**: Implement a macro APT factor model using FRED series (already in `macro_fred.parquet`). Candidate factors: industrial production, yield curve, credit spread, VIX. These are economically motivated, not purely statistical.
2. **Factor number test**: Before finalizing the Korean factor model, run a chi-square rank test on the sample covariance matrix of KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 returns to empirically determine the number of priced factors. Expect 3-5.
3. **Idiosyncratic return extraction**: The Roll-Ross two-pass procedure is the computational template for extracting residuals in our pipeline. In `backtest_harness.R`: (1) estimate loadings via time-series regression, (2) extract specific returns as residuals.
4. **Own-variance not priced**: Do NOT include idiosyncratic volatility (IVOL) as a positive factor in the expected return model. Consistent with IVOL puzzle literature — high IVOL stocks actually underperform in US (Ang et al. 2006). The Roll-Ross finding that own-variance is unpriced is the theoretical foundation for this.

## 6. Strategy Ideas Extracted

- **Macro APT Strategy**: Build a Korean APT model with 5 macro factors (industrial production, yield curve, credit spread, FX, VIX). Estimate monthly factor loadings via rolling 36-month time-series regression. Select stocks with high loading on positively-priced factors (e.g., high loading on yield curve in steepening regime) and low loading on negatively-priced or unpriced factors.
- **APT Residual Momentum**: After extracting APT factor returns, compute residual returns (r_i - predicted return from APT). Use residual momentum (not raw return momentum) to avoid confounding with priced macro factors. This is the "residual-first" approach.
- **Cross-Sectional APT Pricing Test (Korean Validation)**: Annually rerun the Roll-Ross two-pass procedure on Korean data to validate which factors are currently priced. If the macro regime changes (e.g., from yield-curve normal to inverted), factor risk premia will shift. Dynamic factor selection based on current pricing significance.
