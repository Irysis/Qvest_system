# P031: Salience Theory of Choice Under Risk
**Year:** 2012 | **Authors:** Pedro Bordalo, Nicola Gennaioli, Andrei Shleifer | **Category:** Behavioral

---

## Core Finding

Bordalo, Gennaioli & Shleifer (2012) propose that investors' attention is drawn "bottom-up" to the most salient — i.e., contextually extreme — payoffs within a lottery. When a stock has experienced an extreme positive daily return in recent memory, that payoff becomes disproportionately salient and distorts the decision weight assigned to it far above its true probability. The result is systematic **overpricing of right-skewed, lottery-like assets**: investors pay a premium for the small chance of a large gain, and these stocks subsequently earn **below-average returns**. This provides the behavioral micro-foundation for the empirically observed **MAX effect** (Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw 2011): the maximum daily return over the prior month (MAX) negatively predicts subsequent monthly returns. The salience mechanism is distinct from prospect theory — it is context-dependent and driven by the contrast of extreme payoffs relative to their surroundings rather than fixed probability weighting functions.

---

## Methodology

- **Theoretical**: Formal axiomatization of salience in choice under risk. Decision weights are endogenized as a function of payoff contrast: a payoff is more salient the more it deviates from the average payoff of the available lotteries in its "state."
- **Salience Ordering**: A function satisfies "ordering" if the decision weight on a payoff increases with its distance from the reference payoff (average state payoff). This produces overweighting of extreme payoffs.
- **Asset Pricing Extension**: Applied to the standard pricing kernel. If a stock's payoff is salient (extreme relative to the market), investors overvalue it. This yields overpricing of growth stocks (high upside salience) and explains the equity premium (aversion to the market's salient downside in crashes).
- **Empirical Proxy** (via related literature): MAX = maximum daily return in the prior calendar month. MAX5 = average of the 5 highest daily returns in the prior month. Published results show decile spreads exceeding 1% per month in the US; **1.87% per month in Korea** (equal-weighted), with Carhart alpha spreads of approximately 1.41% per month.

---

## Korean Market Application

**Evidence from Korea (KOSPI + KOSDAQ, ~2000–2020):**
- Kang & Sim (2013, 2014) confirm a **significant negative MAX premium** in Korea, consistent with the US finding.
- The effect magnitude in Korea (-1.87% EW monthly spread) exceeds the US magnitude, consistent with Korea's high retail investor participation (~70% of turnover is retail).
- Individual investors are the primary buyers of high-MAX (lottery) stocks; institutional and foreign investors are net sellers — the selling pressure gradually unwinds the overpricing.
- The MAX effect is most pronounced for **overpriced stocks** (composite mispricing index), and is amplified by **arbitrage risk** (limits to arbitrage + short-selling constraints in Korea).
- The MAX-IVOL correlation is ~90% cross-sectionally, but MAX has independent information content beyond IVOL in Korea.
- Pre-holiday effect: high-MAX retail-dominated stocks show pronounced run-ups before Korean market holidays, followed by gradual post-holiday reversal.

**Data availability in our system:**
- MAX can be computed **entirely from OHLCVS Close prices** — no DART data required.
- Daily returns are available for 804 tickers from 2001 to 2026 in RAWDATA.parquet.
- MAX = `max(daily_ret)` over the prior month per ticker. MAX5 = mean of top-5 daily returns.

**Feasibility:** HIGH

## Strategy Ideas

### IDEA-PRIMARY: STR_019 — Anti-Lottery (Short-MAX) Strategy

**Thesis:** Exploit the salience-driven overpricing of lottery stocks in Korea. Long low-MAX stocks (boring, non-salient), short/underweight high-MAX stocks (lottery preference). The Korea-specific magnitude (1.87% EW monthly) and retail dominance make this actionable.

**Construction:**
```
1. Universe: KOSPI200 + KOSDAQ150, liquidity filter (price >= 1000 KRW, ADV filter)
2. Compute MAX = max(daily_ret_t) for t in [month-1, current month end]
   Alternatively MAX5 = mean(top5 daily_ret over prior month)
3. Monthly rebalance: form quintile portfolios on MAX
4. Long: bottom quintile (LOW-MAX, non-lottery)
5. Short/exclude: top quintile (HIGH-MAX, lottery stocks)
6. Long-only version: simply underweight/exclude high-MAX stocks
7. Sector neutralization recommended (avoid sector bets)
8. Size control: size-neutral within sectors (high-MAX is concentrated in small-cap)
```

**Expected alpha in Korea:** ~1.4% per month Carhart alpha (decile), ~0.7-0.9% for quintile long-only tilt
**Data needed:** OHLCVS Close only (already cached in RAWDATA.parquet)
**DART needed:** No

**Risk controls specific to Korea:**
- Short-selling constraints: run long-only (exclude high-MAX, overweight low-MAX) rather than pure long-short
- High-MAX stocks cluster in KOSDAQ small-cap — apply price floor (>= 1000 KRW) and min ADV filter
- VIX/market volatility regime: MAX premium is time-varying and **stronger during high-volatility regimes** — consider regime scaling (consistent with STR_011 architecture)
- Pre-holiday period: MAX effect reverses pre-holiday → avoid rebalancing in the week before Chuseok/Seollal

**Composite signal (enhanced):**
```
anti_lottery_score = Z(-MAX) * 0.5 + Z(-MAX5) * 0.3 + Z(-IVOL) * 0.2
# Note: IVOL and MAX are 90% correlated — use MAX as primary to avoid double-counting
# Or: use MAX alone (simpler, more robust)
```

**Interaction with existing strategies:**
- **STR_011 (LowVol)**: MAX is 90% correlated with IVOL — MAX factor is essentially a behavioral re-interpretation of the low-volatility anomaly. A MAX-LowVol composite may improve explanatory power.
- **STR_016 (Composite Mispricing)**: MAX effect is strongest for already-overpriced stocks (per composite mispricing index). MAX can serve as a component signal.
- **Target**: STR_019 (standalone Anti-Lottery), STR_020 (MAX + LowVol composite blend)

### IDEA-SECONDARY: Salience-Weighted Sector Factor

**Thesis:** Within each sector, the most "salient" stock (highest recent extreme return, highest news salience) is overpriced relative to fundamentally similar peers. Construct an intra-sector anti-salience signal.

```
salience_rank = within_sector_rank(MAX) + within_sector_rank(price_gain_1m)
long: bottom salience within sector AND strong fundamentals
short/underweight: top salience within sector AND weak fundamentals
```
