# P158: How Can Smart Beta Go Horribly Wrong? — Arnott et al., Research Affiliates (2016)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Decomposes smart beta excess returns into Structural Alpha (return at neutral valuation) and Revaluation Alpha (return from getting more expensive over the backtest period). Low-volatility strategies have structural alpha near zero or negative; their backtest outperformance is almost entirely revaluation alpha from a one-time P/E re-rating. After publication, average factor alpha declines by 3.3% (from 5.8% to 2.4%). The paper warns: if a factor's backtested return was achieved partly by rising valuations, live performance will be lower once valuations mean-revert.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
Korean low-vol stocks experienced a significant valuation re-rating 2012–2018 (as defensive mandates grew post-2008). Any backtest over this period will overstate structural alpha for low-vol. Similarly, quality/profitability factors benefited from a valuation tailwind as Korean institutional flows shifted toward quality post-2016 stewardship code. Both factors face headwinds if their relative P/B mean-reverts.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. Implement structural alpha calculation in the backtest_harness.R: regress cumulative factor excess return against log(valuation_ratio) over time; structural alpha = intercept; revaluation alpha = fitted component. Flag any factor where revaluation alpha > 40% of total alpha.
2. Never use low-vol as a standalone factor in the Korean pipeline without valuation adjustment; combine with cheapness screen (P/E < sector median) to ensure structural underpinning.
