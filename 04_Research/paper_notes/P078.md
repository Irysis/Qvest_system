# P078: Anomalies and Their Short-Lived Returns (Calluzzo, Moneta, Topaloglu 2019)
**Category:** Anomaly_Research / Institutional_Arbitrage
**Relevance to Korea pipeline:** High

## Core Claim
Post-publication anomaly decay is driven by specific types of institutional investors, not the market broadly. Hedge funds and transient institutional investors (high-frequency traders, momentum funds) increase their trading in published anomaly directions immediately after publication, accelerating the decay. Long-term institutions and mutual funds follow slowly or not at all. The decay is front-loaded: most occurs within 2-3 years of publication.

## Key Findings (specific percentages, survival rates, which anomalies)
- **14 anomalies** studied (subset of McLean & Pontiff 97 — chosen for institutional holding data availability)
- Institutional trading increases post-publication:
  - **Hedge funds**: increase anomaly-aligned positions significantly after publication
  - **Transient institutions** (quasi-indexers, high-turnover institutions): increase anomaly-aligned positions
  - **Dedicated (long-term) institutions**: NO significant change — do not trade on published anomalies
  - **Mutual funds (index)**: minimal response
- Decay timeline: front-loaded in first 1-3 years post-publication
- Institutional arbitrage is the primary mechanism for post-publication decay (not retail)
- Return decay is larger when:
  - Hedge fund ownership is higher in the anomaly stocks
  - Stocks are large-cap and liquid (easy to arbitrage)
  - Anomaly is in top-cited academic journals
- The 14 anomalies tested include: accruals, asset growth, net stock issues, gross profitability, investment-to-assets, momentum, ROA (subset of Stambaugh et al. 9)
- Cross-sectional pattern: anomalies in stocks with HIGH hedge fund ownership decay fastest

## Factors That SURVIVE Post-Publication
- Anomalies in stocks with **LOW hedge fund ownership** (small/mid-cap, illiquid)
- **Investment-factor type anomalies**: slower institutional adoption because less arbitrageable
- Anomalies requiring **complex information processing** (e.g., accruals decomposition — harder for machines to trade)
- **Profitability anomalies** with long revision cycles (fundamental data releases quarterly, slower arbitrage)

## Factors That DECAY Post-Publication
- **Momentum**: hedge funds heavily exploit, large decay
- **Accruals**: widely known since 1996, institutional arbitrage fully deployed
- **Net stock issues**: straightforward signal, hedge funds exploit quickly
- **Asset growth**: simple metric, fast institutional adoption
- Anomalies in **large-cap liquid stocks** — hedge fund natural habitat

## Korea/International Applicability
- KEY implication: Korean hedge fund sector is much smaller than US
  - Korean hedge fund AUM ~20-30 trillion KRW vs US ~$4 trillion — roughly 1/200th the scale
  - Institutional arbitrage mechanism that drives US decay is structurally weaker in Korea
- Transient institutions (HFT) also less prevalent in Korean equity market
- Korean institutional landscape: dominated by NPS (long-term), insurance companies (long-term), retail — these are NOT the arbitrage channels that drive decay
- KOSDAQ150 stocks have even lower hedge fund coverage → anomaly returns more persistent
- For KOSPI200: hedge fund activity is higher in large-cap names, so value/profitability decay may be faster for mega-caps; mid-cap KOSPI200 still well-protected
- Structural implication: Korean anomaly returns are conserved longer by the ABSENCE of active hedge fund arbitrage

## Actionable Implications for Our Pipeline
1. **Focus on factors with low hedge fund penetration**: less crowding, more alpha persistence
2. **KOSDAQ150 component** of universe: fewer institutional arbitrageurs → better factor alpha
3. **Quarterly fundamental data signals** (accruals, investment, profitability): slower to arbitrage in Korea than US
4. For monitoring decay: track % of KOSPI200 stocks held by foreign hedge funds — rising coverage = alpha decay signal
5. **Complex factor constructions** (e.g., accruals decomposition, q-factor residuals) are MORE decay-resistant than simple signals
6. Consider a "hedge fund crowding score" using foreign ownership data + institutional trading data as a factor FILTER (avoid heavily crowded names)
7. **Two-year exploitation window** assumption in US does NOT apply in Korea — can safely use published factors with longer alpha expectations

## Notes
- Paper: Calluzzo P., Moneta F., Topaloglu S. (2019), "Anomalies Are Publicized Broadly, Institutions Do Not Respond", Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis (JFQA)
- Title in registry: "Anomalies_Fabio.pdf" — "Fabio" refers to Fabio Moneta (co-author)
- 14 anomalies is a smaller sample than McLean & Pontiff (97) or Hou et al. (452) — results directionally consistent but less comprehensive
- Key institutional data source: Thomson Reuters 13F filings (US-specific) — Korean equivalent would be DART institutional holding disclosures
- Mechanism confirmed by Akbas et al. (P097): hedge funds trade composite signals (smarter than individual anomaly signals)
- This paper + P097 (Akbas) together: hedge funds exploit known composite signals, NOT recently published individual anomalies → suggests COMPOSITE strategies are more vulnerable to crowding than individual factors
