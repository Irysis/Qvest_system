# P059: What Is Behind the Asset Growth and Investment Growth Anomalies? (Fu 2011)

**Category:** Factor_Return / Asset_Growth
**Feasibility:** medium
**Novelty:** medium

---

## Core Claim

This conference paper (Singapore Management University, presented at FMA 2011) challenges the asset growth anomaly. Fu argues that the return patterns attributed to asset growth are largely explained by two previously documented effects:

1. **Delisting bias** (Shumway 1997): The superior returns of the LOW asset growth portfolio are driven by the omission of delisting returns for distressed, asset-shrinking firms in CRSP monthly stock return data. Firms with very negative asset growth are ~4x more likely to be delisted for poor performance in the following year (12.48% vs. 3.14%). After correcting for delisting returns, the low-AG portfolio's alpha is no longer different from deciles 2-7.

2. **External financing anomaly**: The poor returns of the HIGH asset growth portfolio are driven entirely by the subsample of firms that issued large amounts of debt or equity in the prior year (Spearman rank correlation between AG and external financing = 0.70). 87% of top-decile AG firms conducted large equity or debt offerings. Firms in the high-AG decile that did NOT use large external financing do NOT underperform. After controlling for both effects, there is NO independent AG effect.

**In summary:** The AG anomaly = (Shumway delisting bias on the low end) + (external financing anomaly on the high end). Asset growth itself has no independent return predictability once these are controlled for.

This is a CONTRARIAN paper relative to Cooper et al. (2008) and is a conference paper (not yet peer-reviewed as of writing date). Its findings should be treated as a robustness concern, not a definitive refutation.

## Factor Definition (exact formulas)

**Asset Growth Rate (AG) — same as Cooper et al. 2008:**
```
AG(t-1) = A(t-1)/A(t-2) - 1
```
Where A = Total Assets (CRSP/Compustat merged, annual fundamentals file).

**Investment Growth Rate (IG) — from Titman, Wei & Xie (2004):**
```
IG(t-1) = (CAPX/Sales)_{t-1} / [(CAPX/Sales)_{t-2} + (CAPX/Sales)_{t-3} + (CAPX/Sales)_{t-4})/3] - 1
```
Or alternatively (Anderson & Garcia-Feijoo 2006):
```
IG(t-1) = CAPX_{t-1}/CAPX_{t-3} - 1
```

**External Financing measure:** Net external financing = equity issuance + debt issuance - equity repurchase - debt repayment (comprehensive measure following Bradshaw, Richardson & Sloan 2006).

**Delisting correction methodology:**
- For 90% of delisted stocks missing delisting returns in CRSP.msf: compound last reported return with delisting return from CRSP.mse (stock events file)
- For performance-related delistings (codes 500-588) with no identified return: replace with -44.5% (NYSE/AMEX average) or -21.0% (NASDAQ average)
- For non-performance delistings with no return: replace with +3.34%

## Key Results (returns, t-stats, IC)

**Data:** NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ nonfinancial firms, July 1968 - December 2009 (498 months). 19,885 firms total.

**Replication of Cooper et al. (with extended sample):**
- EW: D1 (Low AG) = 1.79%/month, D10 (High AG) = 0.17%/month, Spread = 1.62%/month (t = 9.03)
- VW: D1 = 1.26%/month, D10 = 0.27%/month, Spread = 0.99%/month (t = 4.63)
- 3-factor EW alpha: D1 = +0.59% (t = 2.65), D10 = -0.82% (t = -5.68), Spread = 1.41% (t = 8.87)
- 3-factor VW alpha: D1 = +0.15% (t = 1.15), D10 = -0.49% (t = -4.68), Spread = 0.64% (t = 3.64)

**After delisting correction:**
- EW: D1 drops from 1.79% to 1.50%/month (-29 bps)
- Spread remains at 1.40%/month (t = 8.01) — STILL significant
- BUT: alpha of D1 is no longer statistically different from alphas of D2 through D7
- The LOW end of the anomaly is confirmed as driven by delisting bias
- HIGH end remains: D8, D9, D10 still show significant negative alphas (-0.91% for D10 EW, -0.52% VW after correction)
- **Key implication:** After delisting correction, the anomaly becomes ONE-SIDED — only the high-growth underperformance is real

**External financing analysis:**
- Probability of large equity or debt offering in top-AG decile (D10): **87%**
- Probability in D1-D7: **3-5%** only
- Spearman rank correlation AG and external financing (for positive AG subsample): **0.70**
- Firms in D8-D10 that did NOT use large external financing: their returns are NOT significantly different from D1-D7
- Firms in D8-D10 that DID use large external financing: returns significantly lower
- Regression analysis confirms: after including external financing dummy × AG interaction, AG coefficient becomes insignificant

**Investment Growth Anomaly parallel findings:**
- Delisting correction: D1 (low IG) returns no longer different from D2-D7; only D10 underperforms
- External financing: D10 firms that used large external financing significantly underperform; those that did not do NOT underperform
- Same conclusion: investment growth anomaly = subset of external financing anomaly + delisting bias

## Korea Applicability

**Applicability: MEDIUM — primarily as a RISK CONTROL framework, not as a primary factor.**

1. **Delisting bias is a real concern for Korea.** The Korean Stock Exchange delists firms for performance reasons (going concern issues, continuous losses, etc.). If the KOSDAQ small-cap universe includes such firms in the low-AG portfolio, the same bias exists. However, Korea's delisting patterns and the availability of delisting return data in Korean databases need to be verified. This is a data quality issue for the RAWDATA.parquet.

2. **External financing angle is HIGHLY relevant for Korea.** Korean firms, especially chaebol affiliates, frequently issue large amounts of debt (bank loans, bonds) and equity (rights offerings, CBs) to fund expansion. The 0.70 Spearman correlation between AG and external financing likely applies to Korea as well. This means:
   - High AG in Korean firms is often driven by debt/equity issuance, not organic growth
   - The return underperformance of high-AG Korean firms may simply reflect the external financing anomaly
   - **Actionable:** When implementing AG, exclude the debt/equity issuance component or control for it

3. **Implications for STR_AG implementation:** The clean AG factor for Korea should probably be "organic asset growth" = AG minus asset growth attributable to external financing. DART data provides equity issuance (유상증자) and debt issuance (차입금증가) that can be used to construct this adjustment.

4. **The one-sidedness finding:** If Fu's corrected version is right, the AG factor works primarily through HIGH-growth underperformance (not low-growth outperformance). For a LONG-ONLY Korean portfolio, this means:
   - AG is useful as an EXCLUSION criterion (avoid high-AG firms) rather than a selection criterion (seek low-AG firms)
   - In a long-only portfolio: exclude top quintile AG firms from the investable universe
   - Do not specifically tilt toward very low (negative) AG firms, as their outperformance may be delisting-bias-driven

5. **Methodological caution:** This is a conference paper that has not been through full peer review. Cooper et al. (2008) and Watanabe et al. (2013) are published in top journals with replications confirming the effect. Fu's delisting correction only eliminates the LOW-growth outperformance — the HIGH-growth underperformance survives. So the AG factor remains useful as an avoidance signal.

## Actionable Ideas

- NEW IDEA: **External Financing-Adjusted AG (EFA_AG)** — compute an "organic" asset growth measure that removes the asset growth attributable to net external financing:
  ```
  EFA_AG = AG - Net_External_Financing_Growth
  ```
  Where Net_External_Financing_Growth = (equity issuance + new debt - equity repurchase - debt repayment) / Total Assets_{t-1}

  DART data items needed: 유상증자 (equity issuance), 차입금증가 (debt increase), 자사주취득 (buybacks), 차입금상환 (debt repayment). This isolates organic investment-driven growth from financing-driven growth and should be a cleaner predictor.

- NEW IDEA: **AG as EXCLUSION Filter (Long-Only Mode)** — in a long-only KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 portfolio, use AG to EXCLUDE the top quintile of asset growers from the investable universe rather than creating a long-short AG spread. This implementation is consistent with Fu's finding that the underperformance is real but concentrated in high-AG firms with large external financing.

  ```r
  # Long-only AG filter: exclude top 20% asset growers
  ag_exclude <- dart_fundamentals %>%
    group_by(year) %>%
    mutate(ag_percentile = percent_rank(AG)) %>%
    filter(ag_percentile < 0.80) %>%  # Exclude top 20% asset growers
    ungroup()
  ```

- SKIP: The full delisting bias correction methodology is specific to CRSP data structure and does not directly apply to Korean market data analysis.

## Notes

- This paper is a critical "challenge" paper for the AG anomaly — useful for building a robust understanding but should NOT lead to abandoning AG as a factor. The high-growth underperformance survives all corrections.
- The key takeaway for Korean implementation: **control for external financing when using AG**. An AG-based strategy should check whether high AG is accompanied by large equity/debt issuance (if so, the poor returns may be attributable to the issuance, not the growth per se — but the predictive power remains regardless of mechanism).
- Feasibility is medium because the external financing adjustment requires additional DART data items beyond total assets.
- The paper cites Cooper et al. (2008) alpha spread of 19.5% annual (EW) which is their equal-weighted result. The value-weighted results in both papers are more modest and more relevant for large-cap-focused Korean implementation.
- Note the partial overlap with the Watanabe et al. (2013) finding that the Korean VW result is weaker than EW — consistent with Fu's point that the effect is concentrated in smaller, more distressed firms.
- **Conference paper status:** FMA 2011. Should be weighted lower than the peer-reviewed Cooper et al. and Watanabe et al. papers for strategy development purposes.
