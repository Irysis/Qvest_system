# P160: Is Smart Beta Really Smart? — Malkiel, JPM (2014)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Smart beta is active management in passive clothing. Excess returns are explained by higher systematic risk (higher beta, size tilt, value tilt) rather than skill. Using Fama-French 4-factor regressions, most smart beta strategies show insignificant alpha after risk adjustment. The paper argues investors pay active management fees for passive factor exposure and that any true anomaly exploited by smart beta will eventually be arbitraged away as AUM grows.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
Korean smart beta products (ETFs tracking KOSPI200 value/quality/momentum indices) are priced as passive (management fee ~10–15bps) but deliver returns that are 80–90% explained by the same Fama-French factors available in index form. Investors mistake risk premia for alpha. More critically, Malkiel's arbitrage argument is particularly sharp in Korea where ETF AUM in smart beta strategies has grown 400%+ since 2016, compressing factor spreads measurably.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. For every strategy in the pipeline, compute the 4-factor (MKT, SMB, HML, MOM) regression and require that unexplained alpha (intercept) is at least 50bps/year at p < 0.10; strategies where alpha is entirely factor-explained should be classified as "factor beta" not "alpha" in strategy_registry.json.
2. Distinguish between factor beta strategies (appropriate for low-fee execution) and proprietary alpha strategies (justify higher turnover and transaction costs); size the portfolio accordingly.
