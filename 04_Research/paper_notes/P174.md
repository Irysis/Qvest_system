# P174: Smart Beta: Too Good to Be True? — Jacobs & Levy (2015)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Debunks 8 common smart beta myths: (1) Smart beta is passive — FALSE, requires active factor selection and rebalancing decisions; (2) Targets most significant factors — FALSE, book-to-price and small size were NOT among the most statistically significant in Green et al. (2014)'s 25-factor study; (3) Well diversified — FALSE, creates incidental sector bets (momentum loaded on tech 1999, low-vol underweight financials 2009); (4) Factors perform consistently — FALSE, all factors have multi-year underperformance episodes; (5) Benefits from mean-reversion — NOT SUPPORTED empirically; (6) Can be combined efficiently — DIFFICULT due to overlap and transaction cost conflicts; (7) Transparency is a benefit — transparency enables front-running and crowding; (8) Costs less than active — HIDDEN costs in opportunity cost and factor timing decisions transferred to investors.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
The front-running risk (Myth 7) is amplified in Korea by the fact that all KOSPI200 constituent changes and index rebalancing are publicly announced 5–10 trading days before implementation, enabling anticipatory trading. The myth about consistent performance (Myth 4) is critical: the 2017–2020 Korean value drawdown exceeded 30% relative to the KOSPI200, a multi-year episode that many smart beta investors could not withstand behaviorally.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. Document the expected maximum drawdown and maximum underperformance streak for each factor in the Korean pipeline; any factor with historical max relative drawdown > 25% requires a regime-conditional gate to reduce exposure during identified hostile regimes.
2. The front-running mitigation: use randomized or staggered rebalancing dates (not calendar-based) and trade size-blind to the index reconstitution calendar to avoid being front-run around predictable windows.
