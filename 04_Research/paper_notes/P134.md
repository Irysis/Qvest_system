# P134: Risk and the Volatility Anomaly (De Jong & Palkar 2016)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Low_Volatility
**Relevance to STR_011 improvement:** Medium

## Core Claim
The long-term volatility anomaly (low-vol stocks earn higher returns) is CONFIRMED using symmetric risk measures (variance, beta), but the anomaly REVERSES or WEAKENS when using asymmetric/downside risk measures (relative variance = downside variance minus variance, relative beta = downside beta minus beta). This challenges whether the anomaly represents genuine mispricing or is partly a statistical artifact of symmetric risk measurement.

## Factor Construction (exact formula if different from basic idio-vol)

**Risk proxy definitions:**
1. Variance: trailing standard deviation of daily returns, 250 days (at least 120 valid observations)
2. Beta: β^ts_i = ρ * (σ_i / σ_m), using trailing 1,250 days (750+ observations); Vasicek-shrunk: β^adj = 0.4 + 0.6 * β^ts
3. Relative Variance (downside variance minus variance): calculated for days where returns are NEGATIVE
4. Relative Beta (downside beta minus regular beta): downside beta calculated where market return is NEGATIVE

**Portfolio construction:**
- Value-weighted quintile portfolios
- One-month-ahead returns computed
- Rebalancing: monthly
- Zero-cost beta-neutral portfolios for risk-adjustment regressions

**Beta shrinkage formula:** β^adj = 0.4 + 0.6 * β^ts (Vasicek 1973, Frazzini-Pedersen 2014 style)

## Key Results
**U.S. Results (Jan 1963 - Dec 2013, 552 months):**
Variance-sorted portfolios (Panel A of Exhibit 2):
| Quintile | Mean Monthly Return | Value of $1 (held 1963-2013) |
|----------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| Q1 (Low Var) | 0.97% | $136.21 |
| Q5 (High Var) | -0.18% | $0.02 |
Spread: Q1 outperforms by 1.15%/month; $136 vs $0.02 over 46 years

Beta-sorted portfolios (Panel B of Exhibit 2):
- Q1 (Low Beta): $166.73; Q5 (High Beta): $10.44 — Low beta outperforms

**International Results (21 developed markets):**
- Variance sorts: 15 of 21 markets show highest return in Q1 (lowest variance); 18 of 21 show lowest return in Q5 (Exhibit 3)
- Beta sorts: 6 of 21 markets show highest return in Q1; 15 of 21 show lowest return in Q5 — weaker than variance sort

**DOWNSIDE RISK MEASURES (the key finding):**
Relative Variance sorts (U.S.):
- Volatility anomaly REVERSES: Q5 (high relative variance = high downside risk) earns HIGHEST return
- Value of $1 grows to $134.04 in Q5 vs only $0.78 in Q1 — OPPOSITE of regular variance sort
- This means: stocks with high downside risk (negative semi-variance) are COMPENSATED

Relative Beta sorts (U.S.):
- Q1 earns second-lowest return; Q5 (high relative beta) earns second-highest
- Less dramatic reversal but expected positive risk-return relation is partially restored

**International downside risk results (Exhibit 5):**
- Relative variance: volatility anomaly reverses in 18 of 21 global markets
- Relative beta: anomaly reversed in only 9 of 21 markets — weaker evidence

**Risk-adjustment regressions (Exhibit 7 - FF4 alphas):**
- Variance-sorted zero-cost portfolio: positive and significant alpha in ~1/3 of markets (including U.S. t-stat = 3.02)
- Relative variance-sorted zero-cost portfolio: NEGATIVE and significant alpha in majority of markets (including U.S. t-stat = -5.84)
- Beta-sorted: alpha insignificant in nearly all markets
- Relative beta-sorted: negative alpha in U.S. (t-stat = -2.39)

**Conclusion:** The long-term volatility anomaly is attributable mainly to the compounding effect — high-volatility stocks suffer from variance drag, not from a true pricing premium for low risk.

## STR_011 Improvement Insights
- CAGR improvement: The compound return benefit of low variance (lower variance drag = higher geometric mean) is a STRUCTURAL, mathematically guaranteed advantage for low-vol strategies — provides theoretical certainty for CAGR improvement
- MDD improvement: Sorting on RELATIVE VARIANCE (downside semi-variance component) may improve downside protection because these stocks are COMPENSATED for downside risk — stocks in Q1 of relative variance have the LOWEST downside risk AND the lowest return, so they provide capital preservation
- Sharpe improvement: The FF4-adjusted alpha for variance-sorted portfolios is positive and significant (U.S. t=3.02, about 1/3 of global markets) — confirming genuine alpha beyond pure compounding effect

## Korea Applicability
- Korea is among the 21 global markets in the international sample
- The variance sort likely shows the anomaly in Korea (consistent with Baker & Haugen 2012 showing strong Korean LowVol effect)
- Relative variance / downside beta measures could be computed from daily Korean data in RAWDATA.parquet
- For STR_011: adding a downside risk component to the composite may identify stocks that protect capital during drawdowns (low relative variance = genuine downside protection)

## Actionable Modifications for STR_011
1. Compute RELATIVE VARIANCE (downside semi-variance minus total variance) for each stock and ADD it to the factor composite — stocks with LOW relative variance have the most symmetric volatility profile, while stocks with HIGH relative variance have asymmetric downside risk
2. Do NOT use relative variance as the PRIMARY sort (it reverses the anomaly), but USE IT as a RISK SCREEN: exclude stocks with very high relative variance (systematic downside risk) from the low-vol candidate pool
3. The compounding effect (variance drag): for the 20-30 stock concentrated portfolio, minimizing portfolio-level variance directly maximizes the expected geometric mean return — confirms covariance optimization over ranking

## Notes
- Journal of Investing, Fall 2016, Vol. 25, No. 3, pp. 17-26
- Authors: Jack De Jong (Nova Southeastern University) and Darshana D. Palkar (Nova Southeastern University)
- IMPORTANT: This paper (P134) is THE SAME as P126 (Vol_Anomaly.pdf) — both are the De Jong & Palkar (2016) paper
- The beta shrinkage formula used (β^adj = 0.4 + 0.6 * β^ts) is slightly different from the standard Blume adjustment (0.33 + 0.67 * β); the Frazzini-Pedersen (2014) formulation
- The finding that downside risk is compensated while symmetric risk is not is consistent with prospect theory and loss aversion — behavioral finance framework
- This paper does NOT refute the low-vol anomaly; it clarifies that the anomaly is primarily a compounding/variance drag benefit, and that when you USE downside risk as the sorting measure, you restore the standard risk-return relationship
