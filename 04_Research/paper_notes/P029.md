# P029: Saliency — Nisbett (1977)

## Core Finding
Nisbett & colleagues demonstrate the "salience effect" in attribution: people's causal attributions are disproportionately influenced by the most visually and cognitively salient features of a situation. The solo person in a group, the brightly lit object, the moving item — these receive more causal weight than their objective contribution warrants. Salience distorts both perception and probability judgment.

## Key Metrics
- Alpha/Return: N/A (social psychology)
- Statistical strength: Experimental studies with salience manipulations
- Sample: University of Michigan experimental psychology subjects, 1977

## Korea Applicability
MEDIUM — In Korean markets, salience effects drive retail overreaction to the most visible stocks: Samsung Electronics, Kakao, Hyundai — these large, salient names receive disproportionate attention and may be overpriced relative to less salient peers with equivalent or better fundamentals.

## Strategy Implication
Salience-driven pricing: the most salient stocks in each sector (highest market cap, highest news volume) are systematically overpriced relative to their less-visible peers with equivalent fundamentals. Signal: within-sector, long the lowest-salience (lowest market cap, lowest news volume, lowest analyst coverage) stocks with strong fundamentals; short the highest-salience stocks with weak fundamentals. This is the behavioral basis for the "small cap premium" — small caps are less salient and thus receive less overconfident buying pressure. Within KOSPI200/KOSDAQ150, apply a salience discount to large-cap weights when fundamentals are stretched.
