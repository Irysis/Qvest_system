# P090 — The Prediction of Systematic and Specific Risk in Common Stocks

**Authors**: Barr Rosenberg and Walt McKibben
**Source**: Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, Vol. 8, Issue 2 (March 1973), pp. 317-333
**Year**: 1973
**Category**: Factor_Return_Articles / General_Factor_Articles
**File**: `RosenbergJFQA_1973.pdf`

---

## 1. Core Thesis

This is the foundational paper of fundamental-factor risk prediction — the intellectual origin of the BARRA model. Rosenberg and McKibben show that beta (systematic risk) and specific risk (idiosyncratic variance) can be predicted substantially better from a rich set of 32 accounting and market descriptors than from naive historical beta alone. The prediction model explains future risk using a linear combination of fundamental firm characteristics, reducing MSE of beta prediction by 17% versus naive historical beta. The key insight is that accounting descriptors carry independent information about future covariance structure not contained in historical price data.

---

## 2. Factor Construction

**Data**: 576 NYSE/ASE securities, 1950-1971 (Compustat); monthly return data.

**32 descriptors used** (organized into groups):

*Market descriptors*:
1. Historical beta (60-month OLS beta) — primary market-based predictor
2. Residual risk (specific volatility, annualized std of residuals from market model)
3. Log market cap (SIZE)
4. Share price (price level)
5. Turnover (volume/shares outstanding) — liquidity proxy

*Valuation descriptors*:
6. B/P (book-to-price ratio)
7. E/P (earnings-to-price)
8. D/P (dividend yield)

*Leverage descriptors*:
9. LEV = total debt / total assets
10. Financial leverage ratio
11. Fixed asset ratio (fixed assets / total assets)

*Earnings quality / growth descriptors*:
12. Earnings variability (variance of EPS growth, 5yr) — cE+
13. Earnings growth rate (5yr avg EPS growth)
14. Earnings payout ratio
15. Return on equity (ROE)

*Accounting beta*:
16. AB = covariance of firm earnings with economy earnings — measures operating leverage / cyclicality

*Liquidity*:
17. Current ratio
18. Quick ratio

*Size / scale*:
19. Log total assets
20. Log sales

*Other*:
21-32: Various combinations of the above (interaction terms, squared terms, lagged values)

**Beta prediction model**:
```
beta_nt = b' * w_nt + xi_nt

where:
  beta_nt = true beta of stock n at time t (latent)
  w_nt = vector of 32 descriptors at time t
  b = coefficient vector (estimated via cross-sectional OLS)
  xi_nt = prediction error
```

**Specific risk prediction model**:
```
sigma^2_nt = gamma' * w_nt + nu_nt

where sigma^2_nt = specific variance (residual variance from market model)
```

**Evaluation metric**: MSE of predicted beta vs. true beta, measured out-of-sample (rolling 5-year estimation window, tested on next 5 years).

## 3. Key Results

**Beta prediction**:
- MSE (full 32-descriptor model) = 0.0650
- MSE (naive historical beta only) = 0.0786
- Improvement: 17% reduction in MSE
- Most significant beta predictors:
  1. Earnings variability (cE+): high EPS variance → higher beta
  2. Accounting beta (AB+): high earnings cyclicality → higher beta
  3. Liquidity (Liq+): higher turnover/liquidity → slightly higher beta
  4. Leverage (LEV+): higher leverage → higher beta (financial amplification)
  5. B/P (+): higher book-to-price → slightly higher beta (value stocks are riskier in this framework)

**Specific risk prediction**:
- 7 of 8 key coefficients had anticipated signs in the model
- High specific risk predictors: small firms, high earnings variability, low liquidity (turnover), high leverage, extreme valuation (high B/P or very low P/E)
- Historical residual risk is the single best predictor of future specific risk (persistence)

**Key finding**: Accounting descriptors add significant incremental predictive power beyond historical market data. The combination model is uniformly better than pure historical estimates.

## 4. Korea / International Applicability

**Directly foundational** for the Korean pipeline:
- All 32 descriptors can be constructed from DART financial statements (already available as `fundamental_dart.parquet`)
- Key Korea-specific adjustments:
  - **Chaebol leverage**: Korean chaebols have higher average leverage (LEV) than US firms; leverage's predictive power for beta is amplified
  - **Earnings variability**: KOSDAQ firms show high EPS variance; this predicts high beta AND high specific risk
  - **Accounting beta**: Korea is an export-oriented economy; earnings are highly cyclical with global trade cycle. AB will be a strong predictor.
  - **Turnover**: KOSDAQ turnover is extreme (day-trading driven). High turnover ≠ high liquidity in Korean context — need to separate retail-driven turnover from institutional liquidity.
- The residual-first approach mandated by our project protocol is directly grounded in this paper: extract market model residuals before computing any factor.

## 5. Actionable Implications for Pipeline

1. **Beta prediction from fundamentals**: In `backtest_harness.R`, implement Rosenberg-McKibben beta prediction: monthly cross-sectional regression of beta on fundamentals. Use rolling 60-month window for beta estimation, then regress on DART descriptors.

2. **R implementation**:
```r
# Beta prediction from descriptors
predict_beta <- function(returns_panel, fundamental_panel, lookback = 60) {
  # Step 1: estimate historical betas
  betas <- returns_panel %>%
    group_by(ticker) %>%
    mutate(beta_hist = rollapply(
      data = cbind(r_stock, r_market),
      width = lookback,
      FUN = function(x) coef(lm(x[,1] ~ x[,2]))[2],
      by.column = FALSE, align = "right", fill = NA
    ))

  # Step 2: predict beta from descriptors (monthly cross-section)
  beta_predicted <- betas %>%
    left_join(fundamental_panel, by = c("ticker", "date")) %>%
    group_by(date) %>%
    do(lm_fit = lm(beta_hist ~ log_bv_p + lev + eps_var + ab + liq + roe, data = .)) %>%
    mutate(beta_pred = predict(lm_fit))
}
```

3. **Specific risk prediction**: Use historical residual volatility as primary specific risk predictor. Augment with firm-size, leverage, earnings variability from DART.

4. **Descriptor normalization**: Winsorize at 1-99% percentile, then cross-sectionally standardize (mean 0, std 1) each month before regression. This prevents outliers (chaebols) from dominating.
