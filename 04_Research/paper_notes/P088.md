# P088 — Replicating Anomalies

**Authors**: Kewei Hou, Chen Xue, Lu Zhang
**Source**: Working Paper, Ohio State University / NBER (2017); published in Review of Financial Studies 2020
**Year**: 2017
**Category**: Factor_Return_Articles / General_Factor_Articles
**File**: `Review_Factor_Anomalies.pdf`

---

## 1. Core Thesis

This paper systematically attempts to replicate 447 published equity anomalies from the academic literature using CRSP/Compustat data. The central finding is that the majority (64%) fail to replicate at conventional significance levels when proper methodology is applied: NYSE-breakpoints (not all-exchange), value-weighted returns (not equal-weighted), and a higher multiple-testing-adjusted t-statistic threshold of 3.0. Liquidity anomalies are most affected: 93% fail replication. The q-factor model (investment + profitability + market + size) explains most of the anomalies that do survive.

**Key message for practitioners**: Much of the published anomaly literature is driven by microcap contamination (equal-weighting + all-exchange breakpoints) and inflated t-statistics from data mining. A robust Korean anomaly study should use large/mid-cap breakpoints and value-weighted portfolios.

---

## 2. Factor Construction (Replication Methodology)

**Standard methodology applied to all 447 anomalies**:
- **Universe**: All NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ stocks, excluding financial firms (SIC 6000-6999), utilities (SIC 4900-4999), and stocks with price < $5
- **Breakpoints**: NYSE breakpoints only (not all-exchange) — this is critical, prevents microcap stocks from setting the thresholds
- **Weighting**: Value-weighted portfolio returns (not equal-weighted)
- **Portfolio formation**: Monthly rebalancing for market-based signals; annual rebalancing for accounting-based signals (with 4-6 month lag for filing delays)
- **Risk adjustment**: Both raw returns and Fama-French 5-factor model alphas reported
- **t-statistic threshold**: Baseline = 1.96 (5%); robust threshold = 3.0 (Bonferroni-adjusted for multiple testing per Harvey, Liu, Zhu 2016)

**q-factor model (Hou-Xue-Zhang)**:
```
r_i - r_f = alpha + beta_MKT*(r_M - r_f) + beta_ME*r_ME + beta_IA*r_IA + beta_ROE*r_ROE + e

where:
  r_ME = size factor (small minus big, by market cap)
  r_IA = investment factor (low investment minus high investment)
  r_ROE = profitability factor (high ROE minus low ROE)
```

## 3. Key Results

**Overall replication rates**:
- At t ≥ 1.96: 286 of 447 (64%) fail — only 161 replicate
- At t ≥ 3.0: 380 of 447 (85%) fail — only 67 replicate

**Category breakdown**:
- **Liquidity variables**: 95 of 102 (93%) fail — the worst category
- **Momentum**: About 50% fail at t ≥ 3.0
- **Value (B/M, E/P, etc.)**: Better survival rate (~40% survive at t ≥ 3.0)
- **Investment/profitability**: Good survival (~50% at t ≥ 3.0)

**Why most anomalies fail**:
1. **Microcap contamination**: Equal-weighting + all-exchange breakpoints overweights tiny stocks that have large anomaly spreads but are uninvestable. When NYSE-breakpoints + value-weighting used, most anomalies shrink or disappear.
2. **Multiple testing**: With 447 tests, even random noise will produce 22 "significant" results at 5% level. t ≥ 3.0 is the appropriate threshold.
3. **Data mining**: Many anomalies are in-sample discoveries that don't survive out-of-sample.

**q-factor model performance**: After applying the q-factor model, most surviving anomalies are explained. The model is superior to Fama-French 5-factor for investment/profitability anomalies.

## 4. Korea / International Applicability

**Highly relevant warning**:
- Korean academic literature has analogous problems: many published Korean anomaly papers use equal-weighted returns with KRX breakpoints (including KOSDAQ microcaps). Results are likely inflated.
- For KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 universe (already liquidity-filtered): this mitigates the microcap issue — the universe selection is doing the NYSE-breakpoint equivalent work.
- Liquidity anomalies in Korea: KOSDAQ has much lower liquidity than KOSPI. Amihud illiquidity premium likely survives better in Korea because genuine illiquidity is larger (not just microcap noise).
- **Practical implication**: When testing Korean anomalies in our backtest, use:
  - KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 universe (pre-filtered for size/liquidity)
  - Value-weighted portfolio returns
  - t-threshold of 2.5-3.0 for claiming significance
  - q-factor model (IA + ROE + MKT + ME) as primary risk model, not just CAPM

## 5. Actionable Implications for Pipeline

1. **Hurdle gate calibration**: The `hurdle_gate.R` t-statistic threshold should be ≥ 2.5 for any new factor. This is consistent with Hou-Xue-Zhang's finding that most anomalies with t < 3.0 fail replication.
2. **Universe design**: Our KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 universe is well-chosen. The liquidity filter plays the role of NYSE breakpoints in eliminating uninvestable microcap effects.
3. **Value-weighting**: All factor performance analysis should use value-weighted portfolios (or market-cap-tilted equal-weight). Pure equal-weight will overstate performance.
4. **q-factor model as benchmark**: For Korean backtest attribution, use a q-factor model: MKT + ME (size) + IA (investment) + ROE (profitability). This is the appropriate baseline for claiming alpha.
5. **Liquidity factors**: Despite the 93% failure rate in US data, Korean liquidity factors (especially Amihud ILLIQ) are likely more robust due to genuinely wider spreads. Include with appropriate caution and higher t-threshold.

## 6. Strategy Ideas Extracted

- **Korean q-Factor Long-Short**: Construct Korean versions of IA and ROE factors. Long low-investment + high-ROE stocks, short high-investment + low-ROE stocks. Hedge with market beta and size. This is the most replication-robust combination per Hou-Xue-Zhang.
- **Anomaly Robustness Screening**: Before including any factor in the ensemble, run a pre-screening: (a) Does it survive value-weighting? (b) Is t > 2.5 after KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 universe filter? (c) Does it survive q-factor adjustment? Only factors passing all three survive to the pipeline.
- **Investment Anomaly (IA) as Quality Signal**: Low-investment firms (conservative asset growers) consistently outperform. In Korean context, this filters out chaebol conglomerates that aggressively expand (high IA = empire building). IA is a strong quality screen for Korean market.
