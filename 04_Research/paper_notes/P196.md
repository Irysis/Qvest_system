# Paper Note: P196 — The Trend is Our Friend: Risk Parity, Momentum and Trend Following in Global Asset Allocation (2012/2015)

**Authors:** Andrew Clare, James Seaton, Peter N. Smith, Stephen Thomas
**Source:** Cass Business School / Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Finance (2012, updated 2015)
**Category:** Asset_Allocation
**Priority:** 2

## Core Thesis

Combining risk parity (volatility-scaled weights) with trend following (price > moving average → hold, else cash) delivers the best global multi-asset portfolio. Across 95 individual markets and 5 broad asset classes (1993-2015), risk parity + trend following consistently achieves SR > 1.0 with max DD < 5%. The combination is synergistic: risk parity reduces concentration, trend following provides crash protection.

## Strategy Description

**5 Broad Asset Classes:**
1. Developed market equities (MSCI World)
2. Emerging market equities (MSCI EM)
3. Government bonds (global)
4. Commodities (GSCI)
5. Real estate (REIT index)

**95 Individual Markets:** Sub-indices within each asset class

**Trend Following Rule:**
- Signal: If price > SMA(x months) → hold; if price < SMA(x months) → 100% T-bills
- Tested x = 3, 6, 9, 12 months (12-month SMA performs best)
- Applied at both asset class level and individual market level

**Risk Parity:**
- Weights proportional to 1/volatility (1-year realized volatility lookback)
- Monthly rebalancing
- Applied within and across asset classes

**Strategy Variants Tested:**
1. Equal weight, buy-and-hold
2. Risk parity only (no trend)
3. Trend following only (no risk parity)
4. Risk parity + trend following (BEST)
5. Flexible momentum (top N markets across all 95 by 12-month return)
6. Volatility-adjusted momentum (momentum / vol)

**Performance Summary (1993-2015, 5 broad asset classes):**
- Equal weight B&H: SR ~0.5, Max DD ~-50%
- Risk parity only: SR ~0.7, Max DD ~-25%
- Trend following only: SR ~0.8, Max DD ~-12%
- Risk parity + trend following: SR ~1.1, Max DD ~-5%

**Flexible Momentum (top 20-30 of 95 markets):**
- SR ~0.85-1.0 for N=20-30 positions
- Volatility-adjusted momentum slightly better than raw momentum
- Within-asset-class trend: return ~10%, vol ~7.5%, max DD ~-12%

## Key Results

- Risk parity significantly reduces volatility vs equal weight (half the vol, similar return)
- Trend following provides the main drawdown protection
- The combination (RP + TF) is multiplicatively better than either alone
- 12-month SMA is the best single trend lookback (robust to variation 6-18m)
- Flexible momentum across 95 markets: diversification benefit is enormous
- Volatility-adjusted momentum: Sharpe ratio improves by ~0.1-0.2 vs raw momentum
- Adding cross-sectional momentum to trend following: +1.5-2.5% return at slightly higher vol
- Results robust across sub-periods and asset class universes

## Implementation in Korean Market

**Risk Parity for Korean Universe:**
- Compute 12-month rolling volatility for each Korean stock/ETF
- Weight = 1/vol, normalized to sum to 1
- Available in `RAWDATA.parquet`

**Korean Trend Following:**
```r
# Korean equity trend signal
trend_signal <- rawdata %>%
  group_by(ticker) %>%
  arrange(date) %>%
  mutate(
    sma12 = rollmean(close_adj, 12, fill=NA, align="right"),
    trend = as.integer(close_adj > sma12)
  )
```

**Feasibility: HIGH** — both risk parity and SMA trend following are simple to implement in R

**Key insight for Korean market:**
- Korean equity market has exhibited strong trend-following behavior historically
- Risk parity reduces Samsung/Hynix concentration that plagues equal-weight Korean portfolios
- Combine: RP weights + trend filter → high SR, low DD for Korean multi-asset
