# P091: Testing Factors — CFROI as a Stock Selection Strategy (Subramanian & Bernstein 2003)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Profitability / Valuation_Metrics
**Relevance to Korea pipeline:** Low

## Core Claim
CFROI (Cash Flow Return on Investment, a proprietary metric by Credit Suisse HOLT) does NOT provide clearly superior stock selection ability compared to simpler profitability and valuation metrics. Top 50 CFROI stocks delivered 15.1% annual return (1986-2002), but simpler metrics (ROA 16%, ROC 15.8%, ROE 5yr-adjusted 16.1%) outperform CFROI. The higher CFROI returns are primarily attributable to higher risk rather than superior information content. Reported earnings outperform free cash flow as a stock selection signal.

## Key Findings (specific percentages, survival rates, which anomalies)
- **Universe**: S&P500, March 1986 to December 2002 (~17 years)
- **Portfolio**: Top 50 stocks by CFROI metric, equal-weighted, monthly rebalanced
- **Absolute returns by metric**:
  - Top 50 CFROI: **15.1%** average annual return
  - S&P500 equal-weighted benchmark: **10.5%**
  - S&P500 cap-weighted benchmark: **10.4%**
  - Top 50 **ROA**: **16.0%** — beats CFROI
  - Top 50 **ROC** (Return on Capital): **15.8%** — beats CFROI
  - Top 50 **ROE** (5-year adjusted): **16.1%** — beats CFROI
  - Low **P/E** (earnings yield): approximately **~16%** — comparable or better
- **Risk-adjusted**: CFROI's excess return over S&P500 is attributable to higher portfolio risk, not informational alpha
- Reported earnings > free cash flow for stock prediction purposes
- CFROI is more complex to compute than ROA/ROC/ROE but does NOT deliver superior returns
- Key result: simplicity wins in profitability factor selection — no need for elaborate cash flow adjustments

## Factors That SURVIVE Post-Publication
- **ROA** (Return on Assets): highest tested metric (16.0%), simple, widely available
- **ROC** (Return on Capital): 15.8%, also outperforms CFROI
- **ROE** (5-year adjusted): 16.1%, highest, slight edge with longer averaging window
- **Earnings yield (P/E inverse)**: ~16%, competitive with profitability metrics
- **Profitability metrics in general**: all outperform complex cash-flow adjustments

## Factors That DECAY Post-Publication
- **CFROI as proprietary metric**: complex computation, no advantage over simpler metrics
- **Free cash flow yield**: despite conceptual appeal, less predictive than earnings-based metrics in this study
- **Complex cash flow adjustments** (inflation-adjusted, economic depreciation): add complexity without alpha

## Korea/International Applicability
- Results are US S&P500 specific (1986-2002) — limited direct applicability
- Core lesson IS applicable to Korea: use SIMPLE profitability metrics, not complex proprietary adjustments
- **ROA and ROC are superior CFROI substitutes** and both available from DART fundamentals
- DART provides quarterly financial statements → ROA = (Net Income) / (Total Assets) straightforward
- ROC = EBIT / (Total Assets - Current Liabilities) also computable from DART
- Korean accounting: K-IFRS standards make earnings somewhat comparable to US GAAP for ROA/ROC purposes
- No need to implement CFROI for Korea — ROA and ROC are sufficient and superior
- 5-year adjusted ROE finding: multi-year averaging of profitability metrics improves signal (consistent with Novy-Marx gross profitability paper)

## Actionable Implications for Our Pipeline
1. **Do NOT implement CFROI** — already confirmed unnecessary; ROA + gross profitability are superior
2. **Multi-year ROE** (3-5 year average): slightly higher signal than single-year; consider 3-year average ROE in DART calculations
3. **ROA > free cash flow yield** for stock selection: in fundamental_dart.parquet, ROA is already computed — use it, not operating cash flow yield
4. **Earnings yield (1/P/E)** as valuation factor: confirmed competitive at ~16% return; add E/P to the valuation factor alongside B/P
5. Simplicity principle: complex proprietary metrics do NOT outperform simple accounting ratios for stock selection — do not chase complexity
6. For hurdle_gate.R: any new profitability factor must beat ROA as baseline before earning a place
