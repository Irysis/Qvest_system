# P021: Overconfidence Technical Report — Fischhoff & Slovic (undated)

## Core Finding
Fischhoff & Slovic document the "overconfidence bias" in calibration tasks: when subjects say they are "99% confident" in an answer, they are correct only ~75% of the time. This systematic miscalibration is robust across domains (general knowledge, almanac questions, predictions). People's subjective confidence intervals are too narrow — they assign too little probability to the true answer lying outside their stated range.

## Key Metrics
- Alpha/Return: N/A (cognitive psychology calibration study)
- Statistical strength: Calibration curves consistently showing overconfidence across multiple studies and domains
- Sample: Multiple experimental cohorts; technical report (Fischhoff, Slovic, Lichtenstein)

## Korea Applicability
MEDIUM — Miscalibration affects risk estimation in portfolio management. Analysts and managers who are systematically overconfident about their forecasts take on more risk than intended. This is the basis for the observation that active funds have higher-than-expected tracking error.

## Strategy Implication
Portfolio construction implication: if factor signals are subject to overconfident miscalibration, then stated confidence intervals around expected returns are too narrow, and realized volatility will exceed forecasts. Practical fix: apply a "miscalibration adjustment" by widening forecast confidence intervals (Bayesian shrinkage toward prior/benchmark) and reducing position sizes by a calibration correction factor (e.g., multiply by 0.75). In R: shrunk_signal = signal * (1 - shrinkage_factor), where shrinkage_factor is calibrated on out-of-sample performance.
