# P107 — Kyle & Obizhaeva (2017): Dimensional Analysis, Leverage Neutrality, and Market Microstructure Invariance

**Citation:** Kyle, A.S., & Obizhaeva, A.A. (2017). Dimensional analysis, leverage neutrality, and market microstructure invariance. Working paper (published as "Market microstructure invariance: Empirical hypotheses," Econometrica, 2016).

---

## Core Thesis

Applies **dimensional analysis** and **leverage neutrality** to derive a universal market impact formula and a liquidity measure that scales across all assets. The central claim: all financial markets operate similarly when measured in "business time" (time scaled by trading intensity). Introduces the concept of **market microstructure invariance** — that certain ratios involving volume, volatility, and price remain constant across markets.

Two key invariance hypotheses:
1. **Transaction cost invariance**: the expected dollar cost of executing a "bet" (institutional order) is approximately constant across all markets
2. **Bet size invariance**: the distribution of bet sizes (as a fraction of daily volume) is approximately the same across markets

---

## Factor Construction

**Market impact formula derived from invariance:**

    G ≈ (σ²/PV)^(1/3) * f( (σ²/PV)^(1/3) * |PQ| )

where:
- G = percentage market impact (cost as fraction of bet value)
- σ = daily return volatility
- P = price, V = daily dollar volume, Q = bet size in shares
- f = functional form (power function in special case)

For square root model (β = 1/2):
    G = σ * sqrt(|Q|/V)

For general power model:
    G ~ (σ²/PV)^((1+β)/3) * |PQ|^β

**Liquidity measure:**
    L = (P * V * m²)^(1/3) / (σ^(2/3) * C^(1/3))

where m and C are dimensionless constants (approximately the same for all assets under invariance). Simplifying: L ∝ (PV)^(1/3) / σ^(2/3), which means:

    Bid-ask spread ∝ 1/L ∝ σ^(2/3) / (PV)^(1/3)

**Exponents:** 1/3 and 2/3 are the key scaling exponents — contrast with Amihud ILLIQ which implicitly uses 1/1 = 1 scaling.

**Empirical tests:** Russian stock market (RTS) and US stocks; finds cube-root scaling consistent with data.

---

## Key Results

- Market microstructure invariance confirmed on Russian and US stock data
- Bid-ask spread ∝ σ^(2/3) * (PV)^(-1/3): cube root relationship with dollar volume
- Square root model (G ∝ sqrt(Q/V)) is a knife-edge special case of the general formula
- Transaction cost C ≈ $2,000 per institutional bet (roughly constant across US equity markets)
- Leverage neutrality: impact formula is unaffected by stock splits, mergers, leverage changes
- The invariance constant m (moment ratio of bet size distribution) is approximately the same across assets when distributions are log-normal with same log-variance (~2.50)

---

## Korea-Specific Implications

- Invariance constants may differ in Korean market (different institutional structure, price limits, short-selling restrictions)
- The cube-root scaling (L ∝ (PV)^(1/3)) suggests Amihud ILLIQ (which uses linear dollar volume scaling) may be slightly mis-specified — but as a ranking measure, the difference is minor
- Korean market has higher σ/volume ratio for small-caps → they are more illiquid under this framework
- Portfolio construction: expected market impact for a given trade size can be estimated using L = (PV)^(1/3) / σ^(2/3), useful for implementation shortfall calculation
- For KOSPI200 large-caps with average daily volume of 50B KRW and σ = 1.5%: L is moderate; for KOSDAQ small-caps (vol 500M KRW, σ = 3%), L is much smaller → much higher market impact

## Actionable Implementation

```r
# Kyle-Obizhaeva liquidity measure
# L ∝ (P * V)^(1/3) / σ^(2/3)

compute_ko_liquidity <- function(price, vol_shares, sigma_daily, lookback = 21) {
  # price: stock price (KRW)
  # vol_shares: daily shares traded
  # sigma_daily: realized daily return volatility

  pv <- price * vol_shares  # dollar (KRW) volume
  L <- (pv^(1/3)) / (sigma_daily^(2/3))
  return(L)
}

# For implementation shortfall estimation (pre-trade):
# Expected market impact of buying Q shares:
# G = sigma * sqrt(Q / V)  [square root model, most practical]
# G = (sigma^2 / (P*V))^(1/3) * |P*Q|^(1/2)  [invariance model]

expected_impact_bps <- function(Q_shares, P, V, sigma) {
  G_sqrt <- sigma * sqrt(Q_shares / V)  # square root model
  G_bps <- G_sqrt * 10000  # convert to basis points
  return(G_bps)
}
```

**Strategic use:** Use the invariance liquidity measure L as a portfolio construction tool — size positions inversely proportional to expected market impact. Do not use as a standalone return predictor (this paper is about transaction cost estimation, not return prediction).
