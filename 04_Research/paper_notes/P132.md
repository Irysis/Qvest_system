## PAPER A: Minimum-Variance Portfolios in the U.S. Equity Market (Clarke, de Silva & Thorley 2006)

### Core Claim
Large-scale minimum-variance portfolio optimization WORKS in practice. Using the 1,000 largest U.S. stocks (1968-2005, 456 months), a constrained min-var portfolio achieves Sharpe 0.55 vs. 0.36 for the market — a 53% improvement — with about 3/4 the realized risk. The optimization approach (Bayesian shrinkage covariance matrix) is practical and does not require expected return inputs.

### Factor Construction
- Universe: 1,000 largest U.S. stocks by market cap
- Period: January 1968 to December 2005 (456 months)
- Covariance matrix: Bayesian shrinkage (Ledoit-Wolf) using 60-month rolling monthly returns, OR 5 Principal Components, OR daily returns (1-year window)
- Constraints: Long-only, max weight 3%
- Optional: Factor neutrality constraints (size, value, momentum = market exposures)
- Optimization: SAS Procedure NLP (numerical)

### Key Results (Exhibit 1)
| Portfolio | Ann. Return | Ann. Std Dev | Sharpe |
|-----------|------------|--------------|--------|
| Market (cap-weight) | 5.6% | 15.4% | 0.36 |
| Min-Var Bayesian Unconstrained | 6.5% | 11.7% | 0.55 |
| Min-Var Bayesian Factor-Constrained | 5.6% | 12.6% | 0.44 |
| Min-Var Principal Components Unconstrained | 6.7% | 11.6% | 0.58 |
| Long/Short Bayesian Unconstrained | 5.5% | 10.5% | 0.52 |

- Min-var achieves ~3/4 the risk of market (11.7% vs 15.4%) with HIGHER returns
- Ex post alpha (regression): 2.8% per annum (t = meaningful)
- Market beta of min-var portfolio: 0.65 (substantially below 1.0)
- Tech bubble essentially INVISIBLE in min-var portfolio (Exhibit 4)
- 1987 crash strongly muted

**Turnover (critical):**
- Unconstrained monthly rebalancing: 11.9%/month = 143%/year — too high
- Annual rebalancing: average turnover drops to 4.7%/month = 56%/year — more practical
- Annual rebalancing: minimal impact on returns (6.5% → 6.6%), minor risk increase (11.7% → 12.8%)

**Factor exposures:**
- Min-var has persistent SMALL-CAP bias (Exhibit 7)
- Min-var has persistent VALUE bias (Exhibit 8)
- Min-var has NO consistent momentum bias (Exhibit 9)
- Imposing factor neutrality constrains risk reduction but preserves some alpha

**Daily vs Monthly data:**
- Daily returns (1-year window): improves Sharpe modestly (0.55 → 0.54-0.59 depending on version)
- Recommends using daily data when available for covariance estimation

### STR_011 Improvement Insights
- CAGR improvement: The unconstrained min-var portfolio outperforms market by ~0.9%/year in return AND has lower risk — pure optimization beats pure ranking
- MDD improvement: Min-var portfolio almost completely avoids the tech bubble and strongly mutes the 1987 crash — the optimization-based approach provides structural drawdown protection that ranking does not
- Sharpe improvement: Sharpe 0.55 vs 0.36 market (+53%) — even a simplified covariance-based approach in Korean context could replicate a significant portion of this improvement

### Actionable Modifications for STR_011
1. Upgrade from pure vol/IVOL ranking to constrained min-var optimization: use a 24-month Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage covariance matrix of KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 stocks, constrained to top 30 by initial factor score
2. Cap individual stock weight at 5% (scaled from 3% for 1,000-stock to 5% for 20-30 stock universe)
3. Annual rebalancing is nearly as good as monthly for the optimization — confirms quarterly is sufficient
4. Daily returns for covariance estimation (use daily OHLCVS data → daily returns → 60-day rolling covariance)

## PAPER B: Managed Volatility: A New Approach to Equity Investing (Thomas & Shapiro 2009)

### Core Claim
Portfolios constructed to minimize TOTAL risk (using the Barra USE3L economic risk model) dominate the Russell 3000 cap-weighted benchmark in both risk and return. The "Enhanced Managed Volatility" (EMV) strategy achieves 10.4% return vs. 11.8% for Russell 3000 with only 16.0% vs. 11.8% std dev — DOMINATES on the efficient frontier. Key insight: avoid controlling active risk (tracking error) and instead control total risk.

### Factor Construction
- Universe: Russell 3000 (all U.S. stocks)
- Period: December 1986 to October 2007
- Risk model: Barra USE3L (economic-based, uses book/value/leverage factors)
- Optimization objective: Minimize total portfolio variance (not tracking error)
- Constraints: Max stock weight 1%, max industry weight 10%, max sector weight 25%, max turnover 30%/year, transaction costs 50bp/trade subtracted
- "PMV" (Passive Managed Volatility): pure optimization, no alpha model
- "EMV" (Enhanced Managed Volatility): optimization + dividend yield tilt alpha model
- Result (Exhibit 5): EMV = (10.4% return, 16.0% std dev); PMV = (9.8%, 12.4%); Russell 3000 = (14.5%, 11.8%)

### Key Results (Exhibit 5)
- EMV: 10.4% return, 16.0% std dev — higher return than PMV, higher vol but still lower beta (~0.7)
- PMV: 9.8% return, 12.4% std dev — lower vol, competitive return
- Russell 3000: 14.5% return, 11.8% std dev — BOTH PMV and EMV have lower return but also much lower risk
- Portfolio beta approximately 0.7 (vs. 1.0 for Russell 3000)
- High tracking error (6.44 ex post for EMV vs. CAPM benchmark) — not a tracking error strategy

### Korea Applicability
- Barra Korea Equity Model (KEM) exists and could be used for STR_011 optimization
- The sector/industry constraints directly address Korean market concentration risk
- The 0.7 portfolio beta is consistent with the LowVol expectation from Blitz/van Vliet
