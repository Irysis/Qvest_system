# P025: Regret in Decision Making under Uncertainty (1982)

## Core Finding
Bell (1982) develops a formal model of regret as an additional component of utility in decision making. Decision regret arises when the outcome of a chosen option is worse than what would have been obtained from the unchosen option. Incorporating regret aversion into utility theory improves descriptive accuracy and can explain preference reversals and the Allais Paradox without abandoning expected utility theory entirely.

## Korea Applicability
MEDIUM — Regret aversion explains investor herding: by buying what everyone else buys, an investor minimizes ex-post regret if the stock underperforms (they can blame the market, not themselves). This is a key driver of Korean institutional herding and benchmark-hugging behavior.

## Strategy Implication
Regret aversion drives two patterns: (1) Herding / benchmark-hugging: institutions hold positions close to benchmark weights to minimize regret — creates excess return opportunities in off-benchmark positions. Signal: stocks with low institutional ownership relative to their benchmark weight (e.g., KOSPI200 weight >> actual institutional holding) may be underpriced due to regret-driven avoidance. (2) Sin/controversial stocks: investors avoid socially controversial companies due to regret aversion even when fundamentals are strong — creates persistent undervaluation. Korean defense/tobacco/gaming stocks may be systematically underpriced.
