# P162: Smart Beta Crowding (2020)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Factor crowding creates systemic crash risk: the 1987 portfolio insurance crash and the August 2007 quant meltdown (Khandani and Lo 2007) are the canonical examples of factor crowding causing correlated liquidations. The paper quantifies crowding using return autocorrelation, factor loading concentration, and AUM growth rate. When multiple factors are crowded simultaneously (as in 2007 quant funds), the correlation structure of the factor zoo breaks down and diversification benefits evaporate precisely when most needed.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
Korean quant funds are highly concentrated in the same factor exposures (value + quality + low-vol composite) driven by the same DART fundamental data source. A simultaneous deleveraging event — triggered by a large fund liquidation or index rebalancing — would cause highly correlated drawdowns across all Korean factor strategies. The KOSPI200 semi-annual rebalancing (June and December) is a known crowding event where factor portfolios simultaneously rebalance, generating predictable price pressure.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. Add a crowding monitor to hurdle_gate.R: compute the pairwise return correlation of the strategy's factor portfolio with known Korean quant benchmark factors over rolling 60-day windows; if correlation > 0.75 with the KOSPI200 value or quality index, flag as crowded.
2. Build explicit rebalancing buffer around KOSPI200 index rebalancing dates (end of May/June and November/December): delay rebalancing by 5 trading days post-reconstitution to avoid crowded entry prices.
