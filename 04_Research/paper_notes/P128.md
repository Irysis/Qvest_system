# P128: The Cross-Section of Volatility and Expected Returns (Ang, Hodrick, Xing & Zhang 2006)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Low_Volatility
**Relevance to STR_011 improvement:** Critical

## Core Claim
THE foundational paper on the idiosyncratic volatility (IVOL) puzzle. Two separate findings: (1) stocks with high sensitivity to aggregate volatility innovations (VIX shocks) earn LOW returns; (2) stocks with high idiosyncratic volatility (FF-3 residual) earn ABYSMALLY LOW returns. The IVOL effect is robust, massive (-1.06%/month spread), and unexplained by standard risk factors.

## Factor Construction (exact formula if different from basic idio-vol)

**Aggregate Volatility Risk Factor (β_ΔVIX):**
Daily regression for each stock i over month t:
r_i,d = α_i + β_MKT * MKT_d + β_ΔVIX * ΔVIX_d + ε_i,d
where ΔVIX = daily change in VIX index
Portfolio: quintile sorted on β_ΔVIX, value-weighted
Strategy: 1/0/1 (1-month estimation, 0-month skip, 1-month holding)

**Idiosyncratic Volatility (IVOL):**
Daily FF-3 regression over month t-1:
r_i,d = α_i + β_MKT * MKT_d + β_SMB * SMB_d + β_HML * HML_d + ε_i,d
IVOL_i,t = sqrt(Var(ε_i,d)) = annualized standard deviation of daily residuals
Portfolio: quintile sorted on IVOL, value-weighted
Strategy: 1/0/1 (same month vol → next month return, no skip)

Also tested: 12/1/1 and 12/1/12 strategies — all confirm effect

## Key Results
**Aggregate Vol Risk:**
- Q1 (low β_ΔVIX) vs Q5 (high β_ΔVIX): raw return spread = +1.00%/month
- FF-4 alpha spread = -0.42%/month (t = -2.04)
- Price of aggregate vol risk = -0.08%/month = approximately -1% per annum
- Economic interpretation: stocks that hedge against VIX spikes are expensive (low returns); stocks that amplify VIX are cheap

**IVOL (the main puzzle):**
- Q1 (low IVOL) vs Q5 (high IVOL): raw return spread = +1.06%/month (t = -3.71)
- FF-3 alpha spread = -1.31%/month (t = -7.00) — extremely significant
- The IVOL effect is NOT explained by aggregate vol risk exposure
- Effect robust to controlling for: size, BtM, momentum, liquidity, volume, turnover, bid-ask spreads, coskewness, leverage
- Subsample analysis:
  - 1963-1980: -0.91%/month spread
  - 1980-2000: -1.10%/month spread
  - Effect stronger in STABLE markets (-1.19%) than VOLATILE markets (-0.84%)
  - Present in BOTH recessions and expansions (slightly stronger in recessions)

**Cross-sectional regression (Fama-MacBeth):**
- Aggregate vol risk (β_ΔVIX) coefficient: -0.12% (t = -2.17) — confirms negative price
- IVOL coefficient: -0.50 (cross-sectionally) — highly significant negative
- Even controlling for all standard factors, IVOL remains strongly negative

## STR_011 Improvement Insights
- CAGR improvement: The 1-month IVOL estimation window is optimal for capturing the strongest signal; using longer windows smooths the signal. STR_011 should use 1-month daily residuals for IVOL, not multi-month averages
- MDD improvement: The regime-conditioned strength of IVOL (stronger in stable regimes) directly validates STR_011's macro gating — increase LowVol tilt when VIX is low/stable (Buddha Mode off), reduce when VIX is high/volatile
- Sharpe improvement: The aggregate VIX sensitivity (β_ΔVIX) provides a SEPARATE low-vol signal from IVOL — combining both into the composite should improve factor quality beyond IVOL alone

## Korea Applicability
- Ang et al. (2009) extended this analysis to international markets including Korea — IVOL puzzle holds globally
- For Korean market: use VKOSPI (Korean VIX equivalent) instead of VIX for β_ΔVIX calculation
- KOSPI200 daily returns available in RAWDATA.parquet — can compute FF-3 residuals using Korean factors
- The 1/0/1 strategy (1-month estimation, next-month holding) is the standard implementation — consistent with monthly rebalancing in STR_011
