# P164: Smart Beta Is Disrupting Active Management — Kahn & Lemmon, FAJ (2016)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Smart beta strategies explain approximately 33% of active managers' active variance on average, rising to 50%+ for quantitative equity managers. As smart beta AUM grows, active managers who charge 2%+ management fees for exposures available at 20bps via ETFs face fee compression and redemptions. The paper frames this as a structural disruption: smart beta commoditizes systematic risk premia, forcing active managers to focus on truly proprietary alpha sources (residual returns unexplained by any factor model).

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
Korean active quant funds typically charge 50–100bps management fees. If 33% of their active variance is explainable by freely available factor ETFs, their fee justification weakens substantially. For our pipeline: this finding establishes the performance benchmark — any strategy in our registry must generate returns not explainable by the standard factor model, or it has no competitive advantage over cheap ETF alternatives.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. In the hurdle_gate.R soft score system, add a "factor decomposition" component: compute the R-squared of strategy returns against the 5-factor (MKT, SMB, HML, MOM, QMJ) model; the unexplained residual alpha must exceed 100bps/year to pass the hurdle.
2. This finding directly motivates our residual-first decomposition philosophy: strip out all standard factor exposures before claiming alpha, exactly as documented in the user's strategy evolution (DVAA → Fractal Aegis → Nirvana).
