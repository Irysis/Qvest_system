# P077: The Impact on Stock Returns of Crowding by Mutual Funds (Zhong, Ding & Tay 2017)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Crowding
**Feasibility:** high (the core metric Actratio is directly computable from Korean ownership + volume data)
**Novelty:** high (provides a clean, low-frequency crowding metric with strong return predictability)

## Core Claim
Mutual fund crowding in individual stocks creates persistent price distortions. Stocks that are most crowded by mutual funds (high Actratio) earn significantly lower subsequent returns. The crowding metric (Actratio) is constructed from publicly available data (mutual fund holdings + average turnover) and conveys information about stock returns beyond what is captured by standard liquidity measures.

## Crowding Detection Method (exact metrics if available)

**Actratio (stock-level crowding metric):**
```
Actratio_{i,t} = (MF shares held in stock i at end of quarter t-2) / (shares outstanding of stock i)
                  ÷ (average daily turnover of stock i during quarter t-1)
```
- Numerator: % of shares held by active mutual funds (using Thomson Reuters CDA/Spectrum S12 quarterly holdings, 2-quarter lag)
- Denominator: average daily trading volume / shares outstanding (turnover proxy for how easy it is to exit)
- Interpretation: How many days would it take for all mutual funds to collectively exit stock i at normal trading volume?
- High Actratio = stock is crowded (funds hold large fraction, stock is relatively illiquid)
- Data: quarterly, CRSP monthly returns, Compustat accounting, I/B/E/S analyst forecasts
- Sample: NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ common shares above 20th percentile NYSE breakpoint, 1981–2012

**Portfolio construction:**
- Sort stocks into deciles by Actratio each quarter
- Decile 1 = least crowded, Decile 10 = most crowded
- Quarterly rebalancing, equal-weighted

**Return results:**
- Monthly return in Decile 1 (least crowded): 2.162%
- Monthly return in Decile 10 (most crowded): 0.864%
- L/S strategy (Decile 1 - Decile 10): annualized raw return = 15.6%, DGTW-adjusted return = 14.53%
- Carhart 4-factor alpha: 17.28% annualized
- Abnormal returns concentrated in the LONG leg (least crowded stocks outperform)
- Most abnormal returns come from small-cap least-crowded stocks

**Signal persistence (Exhibit 5 — migration matrix):**
- 78.5% of stocks in Decile 1 stay in Decile 1 next quarter
- 81.76% of stocks in Decile 10 stay in Decile 10 next quarter
- Signal is highly sticky → low turnover strategy

**Robustness:**
- Signal survives recession dummy, liquidity risk, default risk controls
- Double sort by illiquidity: crowding signal is significant WITHIN each illiquidity quintile
- Actratio contains information beyond what turnover captures

## Key Results
- Strong negative relationship between crowding (Actratio) and subsequent quarterly returns
- Most crowded decile: less liquid, smaller cap, value-tilted, heavily analyst-followed stocks
- Least crowded decile: larger cap, growth-oriented, less analyst-covered stocks
- Annual transaction costs for the strategy: 0.75%–1.5% (conservative estimate) → still profitable after costs
- Effect persists after Carhart 4-factor adjustment: true risk-adjusted crowding premium

## Korea Applicability
- **Actratio construction for Korea:**
  - Numerator: 기관투자자 (institutional investor) shares held / 상장주식수. Source: KRX 투자자별 매매동향 (investor-type trading data) → quarterly net position estimate, or DART 대규모기업집단 reporting.
  - Better proxy: use FOREIGN + INSTITUTION combined holdings from KRX daily data (외국인+기관 보유비율) as numerator, divided by 60-day average turnover as denominator.
  - Available data: KRX publishes daily 외국인/기관 순매수 (net buy/sell) which can be accumulated to approximate holdings.
  - Alternative numerator: since exact mutual fund holdings not available in Korea, use the DART quarterly institutional holdings disclosure (fund filings) as proxy.
- **Korea constraint**: No exact equivalent of US mutual fund S12 database. But the 외국인+기관 joint ownership ratio from KRX daily trading data is a strong proxy.
- **Quarterly lag**: Korea DART disclosures are 45 days after quarter-end → use t-2 quarter data (2-quarter lag) as in original paper — consistent with Korea disclosure schedule.
- **Expected behavior**: Korean institutional funds tend to herd into the same quality/growth names, consistent with high Actratio in popular stocks. LowVol stocks that are crowded by institutions are particularly at risk.
