# P133: Minimum-Variance Portfolio Composition (Clarke, de Silva & Thorley 2011)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Low_Volatility / Portfolio_Construction
**Relevance to STR_011 improvement:** High

## Core Claim
Derives an ANALYTIC CLOSED-FORM SOLUTION for optimal security weights in a long-only minimum-variance portfolio under the single-index model. Key insight: optimal weight is determined primarily by MARKET BETA (not idiosyncratic risk), and securities with betas above a threshold beta are excluded entirely. 80-90% of min-var portfolio risk is SYSTEMATIC (market risk), not idiosyncratic.

## Factor Construction (exact formula if different from basic idio-vol)

**Analytic solution for unconstrained min-var under single-index model:**
w_i = (σ²_MV / σ²_ei) * (1 - β_i / β_LS)   [Equation 1]

where:
- σ²_MV = ex ante return variance of the minimum-variance portfolio
- σ²_ei = ex ante idiosyncratic return variance for security i
- β_i = ex ante market beta for security i
- β_LS = long-short threshold beta (slightly above average beta of all securities)

**For long-only constrained portfolio:**
w_i = (σ²_LMV / σ²_ei) * (1 - β_i / β_L) for β_i < β_L, else w_i = 0   [Equation 2]

where:
- σ²_LMV = ex ante variance of the long-only min-var portfolio
- β_L = long-only threshold beta (approximately 0.76 in January 2010 example)

**Threshold beta formulas:**
β_LS = [1/σ²_M + Σ(β²/σ²_ei)] / Σ(β/σ²_ei)   [Equation A-5]
β_L = [1/σ²_M + Σ_{β_i<β_L}(β²/σ²_ei)] / Σ_{β_i<β_L}(β/σ²_ei)   [Equation A-6]

**Portfolio beta decomposition:**
Systematic fraction of min-var risk = β²_p * σ²_M / σ²_p = β_p / β_L   [Equation 3]

## Key Results
**Empirical results (1,000 largest U.S. stocks, 1968-2009, 504 months):**
| Portfolio | Average Return | Std Dev | Sharpe | Alpha | Beta | Active Risk | IR |
|-----------|---------------|---------|--------|-------|------|-------------|-----|
| Market | 4.88% | 15.56% | 0.31 | 0% | 1.00 | 0% | 0.00 |
| Min-Var Long-Only Base Case | 5.37% | 11.90% | 0.45 | 2.17% | 0.66 | 6.10% | 0.35 |
| Min-Var Long-Short | 4.45% | 10.61% | 0.42 | 1.97% | 0.51 | 7.07% | 0.28 |
| Min-Var Single Index | 5.50% | 12.83% | 0.43 | 2.82% | 0.55 | 9.58% | 0.29 |

**Key structural findings:**
- Only ~20% of the 1,000 investable securities appear in the long-only solution (those with beta below threshold ~0.76)
- Typical long-only min-var portfolio: ~120-130 securities out of 1,000
- Long-only threshold beta typically ~0.76 (below 1.0 by construction)
- 80-90% of long-only min-var portfolio risk is SYSTEMATIC (from market factor), only 10-20% idiosyncratic
- This is OPPOSITE of individual security risk decomposition (individual stocks: ~80% idiosyncratic)
- Portfolio beta vs market: 0.66 (base case) — consistent with Clarke 2006 results

**Security selection determinants:**
- Primary: BETA (securities with beta above threshold are EXCLUDED, regardless of idiosyncratic risk)
- Secondary: idiosyncratic risk (within the selected securities, HIGH idio-risk reduces weight but does not exclude)
- This explains why min-var portfolios contain low-beta stocks overwhelmingly
- At end of 2009: 61% of MSCI World Min Vol Index was in lowest-beta quintile stocks (24% in second-lowest = 85% total)

**Dynamic range of threshold beta:**
- Long-only threshold beta: ranges from ~0.6 to ~0.8 (Exhibit 11), averaging ~0.7
- Long-short threshold beta: slightly higher, ~0.8 to ~1.0
- The threshold beta declined after the 2000 tech bubble and again around 2008-2009

## STR_011 Improvement Insights
- CAGR improvement: The analytic formula w_i = (σ²_LMV/σ²_ei) * (1 - β_i/β_L) provides a direct, model-justified weighting scheme for STR_011's portfolio. High-idiosyncratic-risk stocks get DOWN-weighted, high-beta stocks get EXCLUDED — this is more principled than equal-weighting or simple score-rank weighting
- MDD improvement: Since 80-90% of min-var risk is systematic, reducing PORTFOLIO BETA is the key lever for MDD reduction — not reducing individual stock idiosyncratic risk. This implies beta-targeting at the portfolio level is more effective than individual IVOL ranking
- Sharpe improvement: Sharpe 0.45 vs 0.31 for market (45% improvement) using pure covariance-based optimization — implementing Equation 2 in STR_011 would be a direct upgrade path

## Notes
- Journal of Portfolio Management, Winter 2011, Vol. 37, No. 2, pp. 31-45
- Authors: Roger Clarke (Analytic Investors), Harindra de Silva (Analytic Investors), Steven Thorley (BYU)
- This is the theoretical companion to their 2006 paper — provides the mathematical foundation for why min-var works
- The key practical insight: beta screening (exclude β > 0.8) does 80-90% of the work; idiosyncratic risk weighting does the remaining 10-20%
- The finding that 80-90% of min-var portfolio risk is SYSTEMATIC (not idiosyncratic) is counterintuitive but important: diversification eliminates idiosyncratic risk; only systematic (beta) risk remains; therefore beta control IS the primary mechanism of min-var
