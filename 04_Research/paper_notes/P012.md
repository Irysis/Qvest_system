# P012: A Serial Position Effect in Recall of United States Presidents (1976)

## Core Finding
Roediger & Crowder demonstrate a robust serial position effect in semantic (long-term) memory: the first few and last few items in an ordered series are best recalled (primacy and recency effects), with the nadir at mid-series. College students recalling US presidents show the classic bowed curve — high recall for Washington/Adams (primacy) and recent presidents (recency), with lowest recall for mid-19th-century presidents.

## Key Metrics
- Alpha/Return: N/A (cognitive psychology experiment)
- Statistical strength: N = 159 subjects (Yale and Purdue); Bulletin of the Psychonomic Society 1976
- Sample: US college students, 1970s

## Korea Applicability
MEDIUM — Recency bias (a manifestation of the serial position effect) is extensively documented in financial decision-making. Korean retail investors' heavy reliance on recent price performance (last 1-3 months) reflects the recency effect. This underpins the availability heuristic and is the mechanism behind short-term momentum and subsequent reversal.

## Strategy Implication
Recency bias creates momentum at short horizons (1-3 months) and reversal at longer horizons (12-36 months) as markets eventually correct the overweighting of recent information. In the factor model: construct a recency-adjusted momentum signal by using 12-month momentum minus 1-month momentum (standard Jegadeesh-Titman intermediate momentum). This removes the short-term reversal component while capturing the behavioral momentum premium.
