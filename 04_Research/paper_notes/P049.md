# P049 — Bhojraj & Swaminathan (2009)

**File:** accrual10.pdf
**Citation:** Bhojraj, S., Swaminathan, B. (2009). How does the corporate bond market value capital investments and accruals? *Review of Accounting Studies*, 14, 31–62.

---

## Core Claim

The accrual anomaly extends beyond equity markets to corporate bond markets. Both the Richardson et al. (2005) comprehensive accrual measure (NNI = Net New Investment) and the Sloan (1996) working capital accrual measure (WCA) predict future bond returns with similar Sharpe ratios to equity strategies. This implies the mispricing is not equity-specific and is unlikely to be explained by risk factors unique to equity markets.

---

## Factor Definition

**Measure 1 — Net New Investment (NNI):**
```
NNI(t) = [IC(t) - IC(t-1)] / IC(t-1)
```
Where IC = Invested Capital = Net Working Capital + Net Fixed Capital
- Net WC = Current Assets − Current Liabilities − Cash
- Net FC = PP&E net + Capitalized Leases − Long-term Debt
- This is the Richardson et al. (2005) comprehensive accrual measure applied as a growth rate in invested capital

**Measure 2 — Working Capital Accruals (WCA):**
```
WCA(t) = WCI(t) / TA(t-1)
```
Where:
- WCI = ΔWC − Depreciation (Sloan 1996 measure)
- TA = Total Assets (beginning of year)
- ΔWC = (ΔCA − ΔCash) − (ΔCL − ΔSTD)

**Bond Return Measurement:**
- Buy-and-hold bond returns over 12-month holding periods
- Holding period begins 4 months after fiscal year-end
- Source: Lehman Brothers Fixed Income (LBFI) database
- Benchmark: matched maturity/rating bond portfolio

---

## Key Empirical Results

- Sample: Lehman Brothers LBFI database, 1973–1997, ~2,300 bonds, ~540 firms/year
- Quintile sort on NNI: P5−P1 spread = **−115 bps** (Year 1), monotone pattern
- Quintile sort on WCA: P5−P1 spread = **−93 bps** (Year 1), monotone pattern
- Bond Sharpe ratios: 0.65 (NNI) and 0.49 (WCA) — comparable to equity Sharpe ratios of 0.71 and 0.41
- Results hold after controlling for bond rating, maturity, duration, and equity factor exposures
- Mispricing is concentrated in low-rated (speculative grade) bonds — consistent with institutional impediments argument
- Returns persist into Year 2 (NNI: −62 bps, WCA: −51 bps)
- The bond market fails to anticipate the same earnings quality signal that equity investors miss

---

## Korea Applicability

- **Low-Medium for direct replication** — Korean corporate bond market is less developed and less liquid than the US, with limited high-yield segment
- **High for conceptual support** — The bond market evidence strengthens the case that accrual mispricing is a genuine earnings quality phenomenon, not an equity risk premium. This supports using accrual factors in Korean equity portfolios without risk-based alternative explanations
- Korean corporate bond market is dominated by investment-grade issues; speculative-grade segment is thin — the channel through which the effect is strongest in the US does not fully exist in Korea
- However, the NNI measure itself is directly applicable to Korean equities

## Actionable Strategy Ideas

1. **NNI as Alternative Accrual Signal for Korea Equities:** The Net New Investment measure is a growth-in-capital ratio that is cleanly defined and avoids depreciation estimation issues. Compute for KOSPI200 + KOSDAQ150 and compare to STR_012's WCA-based signal.

```r
# NNI implementation
# IC = Net Working Capital + Net PP&E
nni_factor <- fundamentals %>%
  mutate(
    net_wc = (act - che) - (lct - dlc),
    net_fc = ppent - dltt,
    IC = net_wc + net_fc,
    NNI = (IC - lag(IC)) / lag(IC)
  ) %>%
  filter(!is.na(NNI), is.finite(NNI)) %>%
  # Winsorize at 1%/99%
  mutate(NNI_w = winsorize(NNI, probs = c(0.01, 0.99)))
```
