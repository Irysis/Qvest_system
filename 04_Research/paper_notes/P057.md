# P057: Asset Growth and the Cross-Section of Stock Returns (Cooper, Gulen & Schill 2008)

**Category:** Factor_Return / Asset_Growth
**Feasibility:** high
**Novelty:** high

---

## Core Claim

Firm-level **total asset growth** is a strong and robust negative predictor of future cross-sectional stock returns. This is the foundational "asset growth anomaly" paper. Asset growth subsumes and outperforms all previously documented growth-related return predictors (accruals, capital investment, sales growth, net operating assets) in head-to-head Fama-MacBeth regressions. The effect persists for up to 5 years, is present in all size groups (including large-cap), is robust across all sub-periods, and cannot be explained by standard risk factors (3-factor, 4-factor Carhart).

The paper argues this is most consistent with **investor overextrapolation** of past growth (behavioral mispricing) but cannot rule out risk-based explanations. Key evidence for mispricing: (1) low-growth firms outperform in earnings announcement windows; (2) effect is weaker during periods of heightened corporate oversight (Sarbanes-Oxley era).

The magnitude: Low-growth firms earn **18%** annualized VW returns vs. **5%** for high-growth firms = **13% spread** (Sharpe ratio 1.07, vs 0.37 for BM, 0.13 for size, 0.73 for momentum). After 3-factor adjustment: EW alpha spread = -1.63% per month (t = -8.33), VW alpha spread = -0.70% per month (t = -3.84).

## Factor Definition (exact formula)

**Asset Growth Rate (ASSETG):**
```
ASSETG(t) = [Data6(t-1) - Data6(t-2)] / Data6(t-2)
```
Where Data6 = Total Assets (Compustat item 6).

In plain terms:
```
AG = (Total Assets_t - Total Assets_{t-1}) / Total Assets_{t-1}
   = ΔTotal Assets / Total Assets_{t-1}
```

**Portfolio Formation:**
- Sort annually at end of June year t based on AG from fiscal year t-2 to t-1
- Hold from July year t to June year t+1
- Rebalanced annually
- Require nonzero total assets in both years t-1 and t-2
- Exclude financials (SIC 6000-6999)

**Balance Sheet Subcomponent Decomposition (for understanding the driver):**
The paper decomposes total assets into components from the left-hand (investment) and right-hand (financing) sides:
- Investment side (assets): operating assets (noncash current assets + PPE), financial assets
- Financing side (liabilities + equity): debt growth, stock financing
- On the investment side: changes in operating assets (noncash current assets + PPE) have the strongest effect
- On the financing side: growth in debt financing (strongest for small/medium firms) and stock financing (strongest for large firms)

**Competing factors horse race:**
- ASSETG absorbs and dominates: Sloan accruals, Titman-Wei-Xie capex growth, sales growth (LSV), Hirshleifer et al. net operating assets growth
- All competing factors become weaker when ASSETG is included; ASSETG remains highly significant

## Korea Applicability

**Applicability: HIGH** — this is the single most actionable factor in this batch.

1. **Direct computation from DART data.** AG = ΔTotal Assets / Total Assets_{t-1} requires only one balance sheet line item. DART provides total assets (자산총계) for all listed Korean firms from 2001 onward.

2. **Korea-specific calibration needed.** The US evidence shows a 13% annualized VW spread. For Korea (code-law, weaker investor protection), expect 40-60% attenuation → roughly 5-8% annualized spread. The Watanabe et al. (2013) international evidence (P058) directly addresses this.

3. **Chaebol interaction.** Korean chaebol expand assets aggressively via cross-subsidiary investments and empire building. This is precisely the agency-based overinvestment mechanism that Cooper et al. identify as one possible driver. Expect the AG anomaly to be STRONGER for non-chaebol firms where empire-building by controlling shareholders is less monitored.

4. **Korean market structure considerations:**
   - AG is not a microstructure-sensitive factor — works for large-cap firms too (critical for Korea's concentrated KOSPI200 universe)
   - Annual rebalancing (June) is natural for DART fiscal-year data (December year-end is standard in Korea, reports available by April)
   - Both debt issuance and equity issuance drive high asset growth in Korea — relevant given the external financing connection (see P059/Fu 2011)

5. **DART data items:**
   - `자산총계` (total_assets) → AG factor
   - Fiscal year lag: use December year-end data, published April/May → portfolio formed June/July

6. **Key concern:** Korean firms with high asset growth from acquisitions of related-party companies (intra-chaebol transactions) may contaminate the AG signal. Filter: require consolidated vs. standalone (연결재무제표) statements consistently.

## Actionable Ideas

- NEW IDEA: **STR_AG: Asset Growth Factor for KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150** — implement AG = ΔTotal Assets / Total Assets_{t-1} as a standalone factor. Short high-AG firms (top quintile), long low-AG firms (bottom quintile). Annual rebalancing. This is the core Cooper et al. finding and the most straightforward implementation.

  R implementation sketch:
  ```r
  dart_fundamentals %>%
    arrange(ticker, year) %>%
    group_by(ticker) %>%
    mutate(
      AG = (total_assets - lag(total_assets)) / lag(total_assets)
    ) %>%
    filter(!is.na(AG), is.finite(AG)) %>%
    ungroup() %>%
    group_by(year) %>%
    mutate(AG_rank = percent_rank(AG)) %>%  # 0=low growth, 1=high growth
    ungroup()
  # Signal direction: SHORT high AG (rank near 1), LONG low AG (rank near 0)
  # i.e., AG_factor = -AG_rank (higher factor = more attractive)
  ```

- NEW IDEA: **AG × Governance Interaction** — condition the AG factor on corporate governance quality (e.g., foreign investor ownership ratio, audit quality). The investment discretion mechanism predicts stronger effect where managers have more empire-building discretion. In Korea, this means stronger AG effect among companies with concentrated controlling shareholders and lower foreign ownership.

- NEW IDEA: **Component AG Decomposition** — decompose AG into operating asset growth vs. financial asset growth vs. intangible growth. The paper shows operating asset growth (noncash current assets + PPE) has strongest effect. In Korean context, intangible asset growth may be especially informative (goodwill from acquisitions).

- SKIP: The long-run (5-year) return persistence is noted but not directly tradeable given the 20-30 stock concentrated portfolio approach.

## Notes

- This is the seminal asset growth paper. AG is now a standard factor in most multi-factor models.
- The Sharpe ratio of 1.07 for the AG spread portfolio (VW) is exceptional relative to BM (0.37) — this is why AG is compelling even as a standalone factor.
- The paper explicitly tests whether AG is subsumed by equity issuance effects and finds it is NOT (after removing firms with equity offerings and acquisitions, effect persists with large-cap VW alpha = -0.92%, t = -3.72).
- Annual rebalancing at June is standard US practice. For Korea with December fiscal year-ends and April/May reporting, June rebalancing is natural.
- The effect is remarkably stable: positive (low > high) in 91% of years EW, 71% of years VW over 35 years. This consistency is essential for a concentrated portfolio (reduces drawdown risk from timing).
- Implementation in QEPM context: AG should be one of the "hard" factors in the ensemble. Its t-statistic dominance in cross-sectional regressions (exceeding BM, size, momentum) suggests it should receive substantial weight.
