**Priority:** 2

## Core Thesis

Using Moody's Manuals accounting data (1926-1963) — a **pre-sample** period where anomalies could not have been data-mined — tests whether 36 well-known anomalies are robust. The verdict is stark: **most anomalies disappear pre-sample**, consistent with data snooping / publication bias.

**Key finding:** Average anomaly return = 29 bps in-sample vs **8 bps pre-sample** — a 74% shrinkage. The in-sample period is statistically exceptional relative to both pre- and post-sample periods.

---

## Data and Sample

- **Pre-sample**: 1926-1963 (Moody's Manuals — balance sheet and income statement data)
- **In-sample**: 1963-2003 (Compustat CRSP standard sample)
- **Post-sample**: 2003-2015 (post-publication period)
- **36 anomalies** tested across all three periods
- Universe: NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq stocks (matching original paper samples)

## Section 5 — Conclusions on Which Anomalies Survive:

**ROBUST (significant in pre-sample):**
- Accounting returns and financial distress risk (ROA, distress, O-score, Z-score)
- Value (B/M, E/P, CF/P, D/P) — significant across all eras
- Momentum — robust pre-sample but NOT post-sample

**NOT ROBUST (only in-sample):**
- Physical investment anomalies: **Asset growth, Investment-to-assets** — significant in-sample but near-zero pre-sample AND post-sample
- Equity financing anomalies: Net stock issuance, composite equity issuance
- R&D-based anomalies

**NEWLY ROBUST (post-sample but not pre-sample):**
- Income statement anomalies: Revenue growth, earnings quality
- Some short-term trading signals

---

## Implications for Factor Research

1. **Publication bias is real and large**: The in-sample period inflates factor returns by ~3.5x relative to surrounding periods
2. **Profitability factors are NOT fully robust**: GP/A disappears pre-sample (t=-0.01) — its premium may be partially mined
3. **Value IS robust**: Book-to-market premium exists across all three periods
4. **Investment premium is questionable**: Asset growth premium is in-sample-only
5. **The "pre-sample" test is the gold standard** — Moody's data is not in Compustat and cannot have been mined

---

## Korean Market Implications

**Critical warnings for STR_013 development:**

1. **GP/A premium uncertainty**: Pre-sample t-stat of -0.01 means the gross profitability premium may be severely overstated for Korean KOSPI/KOSDAQ. Use with caution as standalone factor.
2. **QMJ Profitability pre-sample = flat** (t=0.17): The composite QMJ profitability score also fails the pre-sample test — data snooping risk is HIGH
3. **Safer profitability bets**: ROA (Pre t=-1.03, but at least directionally positive in post-sample) and distress risk are more robust
4. **Asset growth (I/A) is NOT robust**: Do NOT rely on CMA/investment factor as Korea-robust premium
5. **Accruals ARE more robust** (Pre t=1.02, directionally correct) — accruals quality signal more defensible
6. **Value premium IS robust** — include B/M as anchor; it has the strongest cross-era evidence

**Practical adjustment for STR_013:**
- Apply **50% haircut** to expected alpha from GP/A-based signals (in-sample → pre/post shrinkage ≈ 70%)
- Apply **80% haircut** to asset growth / investment signals
- Keep distress measures (O-score equivalent) — these are genuinely robust
- Weight ROA and accruals over GP/A margin
