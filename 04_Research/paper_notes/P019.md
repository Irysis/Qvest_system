# P019: Volume, Volatility, Price, and Profit When All Traders Are Above Average (1998)

## Core Finding
Odean's theoretical model of overconfident traders shows: (1) overconfidence increases trading volume — the most robust effect; (2) overconfident traders cause markets to underreact to abstract statistical information and overreact to salient, anecdotal information; (3) overconfidence reduces expected utility of overconfident traders; (4) overconfident price takers worsen market quality while overconfident insiders improve price efficiency. Positive serial correlation of returns emerges when overconfident traders dominate.

## Key Metrics
- Alpha/Return: Barber & Odean (1998) empirical counterpart: high-turnover retail investors underperform by ~3-5% annually
- Statistical strength: Formal theoretical model (Diamond-Verrecchia, Kyle, Grossman-Stiglitz modifications)
- Sample: Theoretical; Journal of Finance, December 1998

## Korea Applicability
HIGH — Korean individual investors are among the most overconfident globally, with extremely high turnover rates on KOSDAQ. The "trading is hazardous to your wealth" effect is well-documented in Korean market data. Overconfidence-driven excess trading creates predictable patterns: stocks heavily traded by retail investors exhibit higher subsequent volatility and lower subsequent returns.

## Strategy Implication
Overconfidence signal: use trading volume as a proxy for overconfident retail activity. High retail-volume stocks (measured as daily volume/market cap, or abnormal volume relative to 90-day average) are systematically overvalued due to overconfident buying pressure. Signal construction: abnormal_volume = log(volume_5d / volume_90d). High abnormal volume combined with recent positive returns signals overconfidence-driven overvaluation. Short candidates with high abnormal volume + positive recent returns + deteriorating fundamentals. This is the behavioral basis for the "volume anomaly" factor.
