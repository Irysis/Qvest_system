# P009: Availability: A Heuristic for Judging Frequency and Probability (1973)

## Core Finding
Tversky and Kahneman demonstrate that people judge the frequency of a class or probability of an event by the ease with which instances come to mind ("availability heuristic"). Availability correlates with ecological frequency but is also distorted by salience, recency, and vividness, leading to systematic biases in probability estimation. Illusory correlations and overestimation of dramatic events are documented.

## Korea Applicability
MEDIUM — The availability heuristic explains why Korean retail investors overweight recently salient stocks (K-pop related, recent IPOs, high-media-coverage names), creating temporary mispricing that mean-reverts. Short-term reversal strategies and contrarian signals exploit availability-driven overreaction.

## Strategy Implication
Availability bias creates two exploitable patterns: (1) short-term overreaction to salient news events — fade recent extreme winners/losers at 1-4 week horizon; (2) overestimation of repeated dramatic events (earnings misses after prior misses) — buy beaten-down quality stocks after availability-driven selloffs. In the factor framework: short-term reversal factor (return_1w or return_1m with negative sign) captures availability-driven mispricing. Combine with quality/profitability filter to avoid value traps.
