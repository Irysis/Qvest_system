# P079: Anomalies Abroad: Beyond Data Mining (Jacobs & Muller 2017)
**Category:** Anomaly_Research / International / Publication_Effect
**Relevance to Korea pipeline:** High

## Core Claim
The United States is the ONLY country where anomaly returns reliably decline after academic publication. In 38 other international stock markets, there is NO statistically significant post-publication decline in anomaly returns. This directly refutes the hypothesis that anomaly returns are US-specific data mining artifacts — anomalies are genuine global phenomena. The US post-publication decay is specific to the US due to its concentration of quantitative arbitrage capital.

## Key Findings (specific percentages, survival rates, which anomalies)
- **231 anomalies** tested across **39 stock markets**, January 1980 to December 2015
- **2.096 million anomaly-country-month observations**
- US post-publication decline: **-60% EW, -65% VW** (replicates McLean & Pontiff 2016)
- International post-publication change: **-6% to +36%** — range is neither consistently negative nor significant
- Average monthly anomaly returns:
  - International: **49 bps/month EW, 38 bps/month VW**
  - US: **58 bps/month EW, 33 bps/month VW**
  - International anomalies are comparable to US (not inferior)
- Most recent subperiod (2004-2015):
  - US: only **16 bps/month VW** (t=1.67, barely significant) — factor decay in progress
  - International: **38 bps/month VW** — still highly significant
- **Country-level variation**:
  - Hong Kong: shows negative post-publication returns (unusual for international)
  - China VW: shows negative post-publication (also unusual)
  - Most other markets: flat or positive post-publication (anomalies persist or strengthen)
- Explanation for US uniqueness: concentration of quantitative arbitrage capital and HFT in US
- Anomaly returns are HIGHEST in countries with highest limits-to-arbitrage (short selling restrictions, low liquidity)
- No evidence that international anomalies are data-mined: they were discovered using US data but work internationally

## Factors That SURVIVE Post-Publication
- ALL categories of anomalies in international markets (no category shows reliable decline)
- Anomalies in countries with high short-selling restrictions
- Anomalies in smaller, less liquid markets (limits-to-arbitrage channel)
- Momentum, Value, Profitability, Investment anomalies: all survive internationally post-publication
- Recent period (2004-2015): international anomaly returns remain strong while US weakens

## Factors That DECAY Post-Publication
- US anomaly returns: reliable decay post-publication (60-65% decline)
- US recent period: only 16 bps/month VW remaining — approaching insignificance
- Hong Kong and China show some decay (unusual for international)

## Korea/International Applicability
- MOST DIRECTLY RELEVANT paper for Korea pipeline
- Korea is NOT in the US exception group → Korean anomaly returns should NOT decay post-publication
- Korean short-selling environment: historically restricted (KRX short-selling bans, limited stock lending)
  - This directly increases limits-to-arbitrage → stronger anomaly persistence
- Korean quantitative arbitrage capital: far smaller than US → less publication-driven decay
- The 38 bps/month international average (2004-2015 recent period) is the CORRECT baseline for Korea
  - This is roughly 4.6% annual gross alpha from a diversified anomaly portfolio
  - In Korea, potentially higher given smaller QA capital base
- Conservative estimate for Korean anomaly alpha: 30-50 bps/month gross (vs 16 bps in recent US)
- Strategy implication: published factors from academic literature are MORE actionable in Korea than in US
- No need to worry about "is this paper too old/well-known?" for Korean implementation — post-publication decay does not apply

## Actionable Implications for Our Pipeline
1. **Use published academic anomalies with confidence**: US publication decay does NOT apply to Korea
2. **Baseline return expectation**: 38 bps/month gross alpha is achievable with diversified anomaly composite
3. **Short-selling limitations in Korea are ADVANTAGES** for our long-only strategy:
   - Short side of anomalies is constrained → long side pricing not fully corrected
   - Long-only strategy captures full long-side premium without competition from short arbitrage
4. **Historical factor papers** (pre-2000 discoveries) are still fully exploitable in Korea
5. This paper is the strongest single piece of evidence for Korean factor strategy viability
6. For universe selection: smaller, less liquid stocks (KOSDAQ150) have HIGHER limits-to-arbitrage → stronger anomaly returns → KOSDAQ component is valuable
7. **Monitor competitor landscape**: if Korean QA industry grows (more factor funds launched), Korean anomaly returns will start to decay — track annually
8. **No urgency to use unpublished/proprietary signals**: well-known academic signals work fine in Korea
