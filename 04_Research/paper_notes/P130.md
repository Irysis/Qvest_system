# P130: Benchmarks as Limits to Arbitrage: Understanding the Low-Volatility Anomaly (Baker, Bradley & Wurgler 2011)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Low_Volatility
**Relevance to STR_011 improvement:** High

## Core Claim
The low-volatility anomaly persists because institutional investors with benchmark mandates (e.g., tracking error constraints vs. S&P 500) CANNOT exploit it — the information ratio of low-vol stocks is too low relative to the benchmark IR, even though the total Sharpe ratio is high. This structural persistence argument is reinforced by behavioral explanations (lottery preference, overconfidence).

## Factor Construction (exact formula if different from basic idio-vol)
- Universe: CRSP, top 1,000 stocks by market cap
- Period: 1968-2008 (41 years)
- Sorting variable: 5-year trailing volatility (60-month) AND 5-year beta
- Portfolio: quintile portfolios, value-weighted
- Rebalancing: annually (yearly rebalancing shown to have no material effect)
- Returns: total (not excess) in nominal and real terms

**Optimization comparison:**
- Quintile sort only: Sharpe 0.38
- Diagonal covariance matrix (volatility only, no correlations): Sharpe 0.40
- Full covariance matrix (Markowitz): Sharpe 0.47
- Levered min-variance (beta = 1, leverage applied): Sharpe 0.47, IR 0.45, Alpha 4.70%

## Key Results
**Long-horizon performance (1968-2008):**
| Quintile | $1 grows to | Geometric Avg Excess Return | Sharpe |
|----------|-------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Q1 (Low Vol) | $59.55 | 4.38% | 0.39 |
| Q5 (High Vol) | $0.58 | -6.78% | -0.05 |

- Low-vol quintile grew $1 to $59.55 over 41 years; high-vol quintile $1 → $0.58 (in REAL terms)
- Geometric mean excess return spread: 4.38% - (-6.78%) = 11.16% per annum

**Why the anomaly persists (the key theoretical contribution):**
The benchmark mandate creates a structural impediment:
- Low-vol stocks have Information Ratio (IR) of only 0.08-0.17 vs. the S&P 500 benchmark
- The S&P 500 itself has Sharpe of 0.38 (which IS the benchmark IR for a perfectly tracking manager)
- Managers with IR mandates prefer HIGH-BETA stocks (higher IR relative to benchmark) even though these have lower total Sharpe
- Formula: For a benchmark-constrained manager, expected IR = alpha / tracking error
  - Low-vol stock with positive alpha but low beta has LOW tracking error contribution, thus LOW IR
  - Managers rationally ignore low-vol stocks even though they are inefficiently priced

**Behavioral amplifiers:**
- Lottery preference: individual investors overpay for high-vol stocks (lottery tickets)
- Overconfidence: analysts are more overconfident about high-vol stocks with high information flow
- Representativeness: media/analyst attention concentrates on volatile, newsworthy companies

**Portfolio construction findings:**
- Full covariance matrix adds +0.07 Sharpe vs pure vol sort (0.47 vs 0.40)
- Beta tilt matters more than raw vol for large-cap stocks specifically
- Annual rebalancing vs monthly: no material difference in results (5-year lookback absorbs noise)

## STR_011 Improvement Insights
- CAGR improvement: The 5-year lookback for vol/beta sorting captures the STRUCTURAL low-vol characteristic (companies that are consistently stable). Adding a 5-year vol sort alongside current 1-month IVOL may identify more persistent low-vol stocks
- MDD improvement: The levered min-variance approach (beta-neutral, leveraged to market beta) eliminates the market exposure component of drawdowns; however leverage is not part of STR_011's mandate
- Sharpe improvement: Switching from pure vol ranking to full covariance matrix optimization improves Sharpe from 0.40 to 0.47 (+17.5%); for Korean market, even a simplified factor covariance matrix (market + 2-3 sector factors) could replicate most of this gain

## Korea Applicability
- The benchmark mandate explanation is directly applicable to Korean institutional investors
- Korean pension funds (NPS, KEPCO, etc.) and mutual funds are measured against KOSPI200 (IKS200)
- The IR argument explains why domestic Korean institutional managers underweight defensive low-vol stocks — they are IR-inefficient relative to the growth/momentum-heavy KOSPI200
- For STR_011: as a non-benchmark-constrained quant strategy, it can exploit exactly the inefficiency that benchmark-constrained managers cannot
- Annual rebalancing finding: supports quarterly rebalancing being frequent ENOUGH — no need to go more frequent than quarterly

## Actionable Modifications for STR_011
1. Add a 5-year (60-month) trailing volatility as a PERSISTENCE signal — stocks in the bottom vol quintile on BOTH 1-month and 60-month measures are "doubly confirmed" low-vol
2. The full covariance matrix vs. diagonal matrix improvement (+0.07 Sharpe): implement a simple Korean factor covariance model (market factor + 3 sector factors) to capture stock-stock correlations; estimate with 24-month rolling daily returns
3. Use beta as a complementary signal to vol for large-cap KOSPI200 stocks (Baker et al. finding that beta is more central for large caps)
