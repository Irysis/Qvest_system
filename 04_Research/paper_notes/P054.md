# P054 — Xie (2001)

**File:** accrual7.pdf
**Citation:** Xie, H. (2001). The mispricing of abnormal accruals. *The Accounting Review*, 76(3), 357–373.

---

## Core Claim

Total accrual mispricing (Sloan 1996) is driven primarily by the **abnormal accrual component** (discretionary accruals estimated by the Jones 1991 model). Normal accruals are also overpriced (Mishkin test), but hedge portfolio returns for normal accruals are not statistically significant. The market specifically overprices the discretionary/abnormal portion of accruals — consistent with investor inability to detect earnings manipulation, not just accrual accounting mechanics.

---

## Factor Definition

**Jones (1991) Model — Cross-sectional estimation by 2-digit SIC code and year:**
```
ACCR_t / TA_{t-1} = a1 * (1/TA_{t-1}) + a2 * (ΔREV_t/TA_{t-1}) + a3 * (PPE_t/TA_{t-1}) + e_t
```
Where:
- ΔREV = change in sales revenues (Compustat #12)
- PPE = gross property, plant, and equipment (#7)
- Estimated in cross-section for each 2-digit SIC × year

**Normal Accruals (NAC):** Predicted values from Jones model
**Abnormal Accruals (ABNAC):** Residuals from Jones model

**Total Accruals:**
```
ACCR_t = EARN_t - CFO_t
```
Where:
- EARN = income before extraordinary items (Compustat #18)
- CFO = Compustat #308 (post-1988); FFO − ΔCA + ΔCASH + ΔCL − ΔSTDEBT (pre-1988)
- All deflated by beginning-of-year total assets (#6)

**Mishkin Test Results (Table 2):**
| Component | Forecasting coeff (γ) | Valuation coeff (γ*) | Overpricing? |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|--------------|
| CFO       | 0.73                 | 0.67                 | Underpriced  |
| NAC (normal) | 0.70             | 0.78                 | Overpriced   |
| ABNAC (abnormal) | 0.57         | 0.69                 | Most overpriced |

**Hedge Portfolio Results (Table 3):**
- ABNAC hedge: 11.0% (t=8.43) Year+1, 7.4% (t=5.78) Year+2, 1.9% (t=1.58) Year+3
- NAC hedge: 2.3% (t=0.78) Year+1, 1.1% (t=0.53) Year+2 — NOT statistically significant
- **Conclusion: Abnormal accruals are robustly mispriced; normal accruals are not**

## Key Empirical Results

- Sample: 56,692 firm-years, 1971–1992, NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ (excluding NASDAQ pre-1982)
- Jones model estimated cross-sectionally (not time-series) — avoids data requirements
- Mean abnormal accruals ≈ −0.004 (near zero by construction)
- Abnormal accrual hedge return positive in all 22 sample years
- Result robust to 5 alternative abnormal accrual models
- **Refined measure (ACCR2 / ABNAC2)** — after removing special items and M&A observations:
  - Still shows market overpricing of abnormal accruals: hedge return 11.54% (t=5.35) Year+1
  - Normal accruals still not significantly mispriced (t=0.61)
  - Result is NOT driven by unusual nondiscretionary accruals from M&A events
- Both Mishkin test and hedge portfolio converge: abnormal accruals are the primary source of accrual mispricing

## Korea Applicability

- **Medium-High** — K-IFRS provides sufficient data to estimate Jones model cross-sectionally
- Korean earnings management literature is extensive — Korean firms have strong incentives to manage earnings (chaebol bonus structures, debt covenant compliance, IPO/SEO timing)
- Discretionary accruals in Korea may be LARGER than in US (code-law accounting, auditor independence concerns)
- Cross-sectional Jones model requires minimum 5–6 observations per SIC-year group — KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 may have thin industry-year cells; broader universe or 3-digit KSIC may be needed
- The abnormal accrual signal should be STRONGER in Korea due to higher earnings management prevalence

## Actionable Strategy Ideas

1. **Abnormal Accrual Factor (ABNAC) — New Factor Not in STR_012:** Estimate the Jones model cross-sectionally by KSIC industry-year. Use residuals as ABNAC factor. This isolates discretionary manipulation from normal business accruals.

```r
# Jones model cross-sectional estimation
# Requires: fundamentals with sale, ppegt (gross PPE), at
estimate_jones_model <- function(df_group) {
  # df_group = one industry-year group (min 5 obs)
  tryCatch({
    mod <- lm(
      I(accruals / lag_at) ~
        I(1 / lag_at) +
        I(d_sale / lag_at) +
        I(ppegt / lag_at) - 1,  # no intercept per Jones (1991)
      data = df_group
    )
    df_group %>%
      mutate(
        NAC   = fitted(mod),    # Normal accruals
        ABNAC = residuals(mod)  # Abnormal accruals
      )
  }, error = function(e) {
    df_group %>% mutate(NAC = NA_real_, ABNAC = NA_real_)
  })
}

jones_factors <- fundamentals %>%
  arrange(ticker, year) %>%
  group_by(ticker) %>%
  mutate(
    accruals = (ni - cfo) / lag(at),
    d_sale = (sale - lag(sale)) / lag(at),
    lag_at = lag(at)
  ) %>%
  ungroup() %>%
  filter(!is.na(accruals), !is.na(d_sale), !is.na(ppegt)) %>%
  group_by(ksic_2digit, year) %>%
  filter(n() >= 5) %>%
  group_modify(~estimate_jones_model(.x)) %>%
  ungroup()
```

2. **Modified Jones Model (Dechow, Sloan & Sweeney 1995):** The modified version adjusts for change in receivables to better isolate sales manipulation:
```r
# Modified Jones: replace ΔSales with ΔSales - ΔAR
d_sale_adj = (sale - lag(sale) - (rect - lag(rect))) / lag(at)
```

3. **ABNAC × STR_012 Composite:** Combine ABNAC with the existing Sloan CFS_ACC measure. ABNAC captures manipulation while CFS_ACC captures mechanical accrual effects. The intersection (high ABNAC AND high CFS_ACC) = maximum short signal.

```r
# Combined signal
combo_short <- factors %>%
  mutate(
    # Both signals in top 20%
    double_high = (rank(ABNAC) / n() > 0.80) & (rank(CFS_ACC) / n() > 0.80)
  )
```

## Notes

- **Most important new factor from this paper:** ABNAC (abnormal/discretionary accruals from Jones model). This is NOT currently in STR_012.
- The finding that NORMAL accruals are not significantly mispriced via hedge portfolios is surprising — it suggests the Sloan (1996) result is primarily driven by discretionary manipulation, not mechanical accrual accounting
- For Korea, earnings management research (Kwon 2005, Bae & Jeong 2007 etc.) confirms high ABNAC magnitudes in Korean firms
- Cross-sectional Jones model estimation works better in Korea than time-series due to limited history for many KOSDAQ firms
- The Dechow, Sloan & Sweeney (1995) modified Jones model is preferred over the original Jones for robustness
- **Feasibility: Medium-High** (requires industry-year grouping with adequate sample size; KSIC 2-digit for KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 is marginal for some industries)
- **Novelty vs STR_012: High** (ABNAC is a distinct new factor)
