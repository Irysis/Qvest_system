# P153: Capital Asset Prices: A Theory of Market Equilibrium under Conditions of Risk (1964)
**Category:** Portfolio_Theory | **Priority:** 3
**Author:** William F. Sharpe | **Journal:** Journal of Finance, Vol. 19, No. 3, pp. 425-442

## Core Contribution to Factor Investing
Sharpe created the CAPM by extending Markowitz's portfolio theory to market equilibrium, deriving the **Capital Market Line** (CML) and the **Security Market Line** (SML). The CML describes efficient portfolios mixing risky assets and the riskless asset; the SML prices individual securities by their beta (systematic risk contribution). The key insight: since all investors hold Markowitz-efficient portfolios and can borrow/lend at the riskless rate, in equilibrium all risky assets lie on the SML — and only **non-diversifiable (systematic) risk** is rewarded. For factor investing: Sharpe's SML is the null hypothesis against which all factor alphas are measured. A factor premium exists if and only if stocks with high factor scores earn returns **above** the SML prediction — i.e., positive alpha relative to the market.

## Key Formula/Result
Capital Market Line (efficient portfolios only):
```
E(R_p) = r_f + [(E(R_m) - r_f) / σ_m] · σ_p
```
The slope (E(R_m) - r_f) / σ_m is the **price of risk** (Sharpe ratio of the market).

Security Market Line (all assets):
```
E(R_i) = r_f + β_i · (E(R_m) - r_f)
```
where β_i = Cov(R_i, R_m) / Var(R_m)

Utility function framework:
```
U = f(E_w, σ_w)
```
with dU/dE_w > 0 and dU/dσ_w < 0 (risk aversion)

Note: Sharpe himself acknowledged Treynor's independent (unpublished) work; see footnote 7 in the original paper: "Mr. Jack L. Treynor of Arthur D. Little, Inc., has independently developed a model similar in many respects to the one described here."

## Modern Relevance for Korea Strategy
The SML provides the benchmark for evaluating Korean factor strategies: our `hurdle_gate.R` implicitly tests whether simulated strategies produce CAPM alphas (Jensen's alpha) significantly above zero. For KOSPI200, using IKS200 as the benchmark, the market risk premium has averaged approximately 4-6% annually 2001-2025. Any factor portfolio producing returns in excess of the SML prediction (positive Jensen's alpha) represents genuine mispricing or compensation for non-market risk — consistent with our APT-based multi-factor approach. The CAPM's single-factor restriction is too tight for Korean market structure (large holding company discounts, government-linked entities, chaebol cross-holdings), which further validates the APT/multi-factor approach.
