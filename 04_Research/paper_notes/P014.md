# P014: Endowment Disagreement Plott (2007)

## Core Finding
Plott et al. demonstrate that the endowment effect (Thaler 1980) — the tendency to demand more to give up an object than to acquire it — can be attributed to "misconceptions" or "false beliefs" about the exchange institution rather than pure loss aversion. When subjects fully understand the Becker-DeGroot-Marschak mechanism, the endowment effect largely disappears, suggesting it is partly a cognitive artifact of unfamiliarity.

- Alpha/Return: N/A (experimental economics)

- Sample: Caltech experimental economics lab, 2007

## Korea Applicability
LOW — Primarily relevant as a theoretical counterpoint to pure prospect theory. In Korean markets, the endowment effect is real but partially mediated by familiarity with the asset.

## Strategy Implication
The endowment effect implies that investors demand a premium to sell assets they already own vs. assets they don't hold. This creates momentum in stocks that are widely held by domestic institutions — institutions are reluctant to sell even as fundamentals deteriorate. Contrarian signal: stocks with high institutional ownership and deteriorating fundamentals are overpriced due to endowment bias. Long fundamentally improving stocks with low institutional ownership; short fundamentally deteriorating stocks with high institutional ownership.
