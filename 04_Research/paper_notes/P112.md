# P112 — Sadka (2010): Liquidity Risk and the Cross-Section of Hedge-Fund Returns

**Citation:** Sadka, R. (2010). Liquidity risk and the cross-section of hedge-fund returns. *Journal of Financial Economics*, 98(1), 54–71.

---

## Core Thesis

Liquidity risk — specifically the **permanent-variable component** of price impact — is a significant predictor of hedge fund returns. Hedge funds with higher loadings on systematic liquidity risk earn ~6% higher annual returns than low-loading funds. The liquidity risk premium survives after controlling for standard risk factors. The paper also documents that high-liquidity-beta funds performed dramatically worse during liquidity crises (1998 LTCM, August 2007, Fall 2008), consistent with the risk-based interpretation.

---

## Factor Construction

**Sadka (2006) liquidity factor:** Decomposes intraday price impact into two components:
1. **Permanent price impact** (λ^perm): due to informed trading; permanent shift in equilibrium price
2. **Transitory price impact** (λ^trans): due to inventory effects; price reverts after trade

The **permanent-variable component** (λ^V,perm) is the key risk factor:
- Estimated using trade-by-trade data with signed trades
- High λ^V,perm = stock is highly sensitive to informed order flow
- Aggregate: market-wide Sadka liquidity factor = innovations in cross-sectional average of λ^V,perm

**Hedge fund analysis:**
- Database: CISDM, 1994–2008, 1,610 funds
- Estimate fund-level liquidity beta: β^sadka = cov(r_fund, ΔLiq_sadka) / var(ΔLiq_sadka)
- Sort funds into deciles by β^sadka; examine return spreads
- Control: Fung-Hsieh 7-factor model (equity, bond, FX, commodity, trend-following)

---

## Key Results

| Beta Decile | Avg Annual Return | Fung-Hsieh Alpha |
|---|---|---|
| D1 (lowest β) | 6.2% | 1.2% |
| D5 | 9.1% | 3.8% |
| D10 (highest β) | 12.4% | 7.1%*** |

- D10 minus D1 annual spread: **~6.2%** (statistically significant)
- 8 of 11 hedge fund investment styles load significantly on Sadka liquidity factor
- Directional trading styles (long/short equity, managed futures) have highest loadings
- Market neutral, fixed income arbitrage: lower loadings

**Crisis performance:**
| Event | D10 Return | D1 Return |
|---|---|---|
| LTCM (Fall 1998) | -15.3% | -3.2% |
| Quant Crisis (Aug 2007) | -8.7% | -0.9% |
| GFC (Fall 2008) | -18.1% | -5.4% |

High-beta funds perform poorly exactly when liquidity dries up — confirms risk-based story, not mispricing.

## Korea-Specific Implications

- Korean hedge fund industry is small and data is limited; direct replication not feasible
- **Applicable angle**: the permanent vs. transitory decomposition of price impact is conceptually important
  - Permanent component: informed trading in Korean market (대주주 거래, 기관 블록딜)
  - Transitory component: Korean retail day-traders creating temporary price pressure
- During Korean market stress events (1997, 2008, 2011 ELS unwinding, 2020 COVID): high-β strategies would suffer precisely when drawdowns are already large — tail risk concentration
- For Moltbot's portfolio: need to ensure the strategy does NOT have high Sadka β (i.e., doesn't require liquidity at the worst times)

**Korean liquidity crisis events to monitor:**
- KRW/USD spike above 1,300 (1997, 2008 levels): aggregate ILLIQ rises dramatically
- CDS spread blowout: institutional deleveraging forces
- Net foreign outflows: foreigners tend to sell liquid KOSPI200 first → relative KOSDAQ illiquidity worsens

## Actionable Implementation

```r
# Sadka liquidity beta approximation (without intraday data)
# Use aggregate Amihud ILLIQ innovations as proxy for Sadka factor

compute_liquidity_beta <- function(fund_returns, market_illiq_innovations, lookback_months = 36) {
  # Estimate rolling liquidity beta
  # fund_returns: monthly returns
  # market_illiq_innovations: monthly innovations in aggregate ILLIQ

  roll_cov <- slider::slide2_dbl(
    .x = fund_returns,
    .y = market_illiq_innovations,
    .f = ~ cov(.x, .y, use = "complete.obs"),
    .before = lookback_months - 1,
    .complete = TRUE
  )

  roll_var <- slider::slide_dbl(
    market_illiq_innovations,
    ~ var(., na.rm = TRUE),
    .before = lookback_months - 1,
    .complete = TRUE
  )

  beta_liq <- roll_cov / roll_var
  return(beta_liq)
}

# For portfolio-level: monitor cumulative liquidity beta
# If beta_liq > threshold, reduce position sizes (regime-conditional sizing)
```

**Key insight for strategy design:** High Sadka-beta strategies (long illiquid stocks, short liquid) earn premium in normal times but crash during liquidity crises. The 6% premium is compensation for this tail risk. For risk management, monitor the portfolio's aggregate liquidity beta and reduce exposure when VIX > 30 or KRW/USD rises sharply.
