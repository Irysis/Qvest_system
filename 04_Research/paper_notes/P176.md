# P176: An Evaluation of Alternative Equity Indices Part 2: Fundamental Weighting Schemes — Clare, Motson & Thomas (2013)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Companion paper to P175 testing 5 fundamentally-weighted indices (Dividend, Cashflow, Book Value, Sales, Composite) vs. cap-weight for 1,000 US stocks, 1968–2011. All fundamental indices outperform cap-weight in Sharpe ratio: Sales-weighted Sharpe = 0.42, Market cap = 0.32. Sharpe ratios are statistically significantly different from cap-weight for Cashflow, Sales, and Composite (Table 2). Key attribution: Book-to-market value tilt explains by far the greatest share of outperformance (Table 3: B/M contribution = 1.61%–2.02%/yr across all fundamental indices). Turnover = 11–13%/yr vs. 5% for cap-weight — manageable but non-trivial. Monte Carlo: Sales-weighted index outperforms 99% of randomly weighted portfolios on Sharpe ratio, confirming genuine alpha not luck.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
The dominance of Book-to-market tilt as the source of fundamental index outperformance means that Korean fundamental weighting is exposed to the same risks as pure value investing: multi-year underperformance in growth regimes (confirmed: 1990s cap-weight dominance in Table 2, Panel B). Korean fundamental indices (dividend-weighted, book-value-weighted ETFs) have underperformed significantly during 2017–2021 Korean tech/growth rally — precisely as predicted.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. The Sales-weighted index (best performer: Sharpe 0.42, 99th percentile vs. random, turnover only 12%/yr) suggests that revenue-based weighting is robust — implement a Sales-rank weighting as an alternative to pure P/B ranking in the Korean value factor construction using DART annual sales data.
2. Add a 10-month moving average trend filter to the fundamental index strategy (as tested in P176 Table 4): applying this filter reduces maximum drawdown by 28–33 percentage points while maintaining comparable returns — implement as a regime gate in backtest_harness.R.
