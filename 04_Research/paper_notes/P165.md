# P165: Smart Beta Is Not Monkey Business — Amenc, Goltz & Lodh, JII Spring (2016)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Rebuts the "monkey portfolio" argument (that all smart beta is just random deviation from cap-weighting). Tests 16 factor-tilted strategies using explicit factor tilts (value, momentum, size, low-vol, profitability, low-investment) with two weighting schemes. Diversified multi-strategy outperforms benchmark by 2.95%–4.72%. Critically, inverting the strategies produces lower performance than the original strategies, which falsifies the monkey argument. Correlation matrix of smart beta strategies shows low and sometimes negative pairwise correlations (Exhibit 3), confirming genuine factor diversity.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
The monkey portfolio argument is sometimes used to dismiss Korean quant factor strategies as random size/value tilts. This paper's counter-evidence is relevant: explicit factor-tilted strategies with robust construction DO earn genuine alpha beyond simple cap-weighting deviations. However, the paper's validity depends on careful factor construction — ad-hoc multi-factor composites without theoretical grounding remain susceptible to the original critique.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. When justifying factor choices in strategy documentation, reference the Amenc/Goltz inversion test: demonstrate that the anti-portfolio (shorting the factor tilt) underperforms, confirming the factor signal is directional, not random.
2. Use the low inter-factor correlation finding to support multi-factor composite construction in ensemble_engine.R: combining factors with pairwise correlation < 0.30 provides genuine diversification, not just factor blending.
