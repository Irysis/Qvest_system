**Source**: Journal of Financial Economics, Vol. 77 (2005), pp. 375-410
**Year**: 2005
**Category**: Factor_Return_Articles / Liquidity_Factors

## 1. Core Thesis

Acharya and Pedersen develop a full equilibrium asset pricing model that incorporates liquidity risk explicitly. The model shows that a security's required return depends not only on its market beta (standard CAPM) but also on THREE distinct liquidity risk betas, capturing different channels through which liquidity uncertainty affects prices. The empirical finding is that the total liquidity effect contributes approximately 1.1% per year to the cross-sectional risk premium difference between illiquid and liquid stocks. Critically, the most economically significant channel is cov(c^i, r^M) — stocks that become illiquid precisely when the market falls command the largest premium. This paper provides the theoretical and empirical foundation for a liquidity-adjusted CAPM (L-CAPM) that outperforms standard CAPM in cross-sectional fit.

## 2. Factor Construction

**Data**: NYSE and AMEX stocks, July 1963 – December 1999 (CRSP). 25 test portfolios sorted by Amihud illiquidity (year y-1). Also 25 illiquidity-variation portfolios and 25 size portfolios.

**Illiquidity measure (Amihud 2002)**:
```
ILLIQ^i_t = (1/Days^i_t) * sum_{d=1}^{Days^i_t} |R^i_{id}| / V^i_{id}
```
where R^i_{id} = daily return, V^i_{id} = daily dollar volume (millions), Days^i_t = trading days.

**Normalized illiquidity** (for stationarity):
```
c^i_t = min(0.25 + 0.30 * ILLIQ^i_t * P^M_{t-1}, 30.00)
```
where P^M_{t-1} = relative market capitalization at t-1 vs. July 1962. Capped at 30% to prevent extreme observations from dominating.

**The Liquidity-Adjusted CAPM (Proposition 1)**:

Expected net return equation:
```
E_t(r^i_{t+1} - c^i_{t+1}) = r^f + lambda_t * cov_t(r^i_{t+1} - c^i_{t+1}, r^M_{t+1} - c^M_{t+1}) / var_t(r^M_{t+1} - c^M_{t+1})
```

Equivalently, expected gross return:
```
E(r^i_t - r^f) = E(c^i_t) + lambda * [beta^1i + beta^2i - beta^3i - beta^4i]
```

**Four betas** (from Equations 13-16):
```
beta^1i = cov(r^i, r^M) / var(r^M - c^M)          [Standard market beta]
beta^2i = cov(c^i, c^M) / var(r^M - c^M)           [Commonality in liquidity]
beta^3i = cov(r^i, c^M) / var(r^M - c^M)           [Return sensitivity to market illiquidity]
beta^4i = cov(c^i, r^M) / var(r^M - c^M)           [Illiquidity sensitivity to market returns]
```

**Net beta**: beta^net = beta^1 + beta^2 - beta^3 - beta^4

**Liquidity risk estimation procedure**:
1. Estimate monthly illiquidity c^i_t from daily CRSP data (Eq. 17-18)
2. Estimate market illiquidity c^M_t as equal-weighted average of c^i_t
3. Extract innovations in market illiquidity via AR(2): u_t = c^M_t - E_{t-1}(c^M_t)
4. Estimate four betas via time-series regression for each portfolio (1964-1999)
5. Cross-sectional GMM regression to estimate lambda

## 3. Key Results

**Empirical liquidity risk contributions** (Table 4, model-constrained estimates):
| Channel | Mechanism | Annual Premium |
|---|---|---|
| E(c^i) — level effect | Direct illiquidity cost (spread) | ~3.5% (calibrated turnover) |
| cov(c^i, c^M) — commonality | Premium for illiquid-when-market-illiquid | 0.08%/yr |
| cov(r^i, c^M) — return sensitivity | Premium for low-return when market illiquid | 0.16%/yr |
| cov(c^i, r^M) — illiquidity sensitivity | Premium for illiquid in down market | **0.82%/yr** |
| **Total liquidity risk effect** | Sum of three channels | **1.1%/yr** |

**Dominant channel**: cov(c^i, r^M) — stocks that become harder to sell precisely when the market is falling. This "flight to liquidity" channel accounts for 75% of the total liquidity risk premium.

**Beta statistics (Table 1)**:
- More illiquid stocks (high c^i) also have: larger beta^2 (commonality), larger |beta^3| and |beta^4| (both negative/positive)
- Collinearity among the four betas is severe — empirically difficult to separately identify
- beta^1 and beta^3 are highly negatively correlated (-0.972 in Table 2)

**Model fit**:
- L-CAPM R^2 in cross-sectional fit substantially exceeds standard CAPM
- Liquidity-sorted portfolios: L-CAPM fits well
- Size-sorted portfolios: L-CAPM fits well
- BM-sorted portfolios: L-CAPM cannot explain (book-to-market effect is orthogonal to liquidity)

**Persistence**: Market illiquidity AR(1) coefficient = 0.87. Illiquidity is highly persistent — predicts future returns and co-moves with contemporaneous returns (Proposition 2 and 3 empirically validated).

**Liquidity crises visible in data**: Innovations spike in 5/1970 (Penn Central), 11/1973 (oil crisis), 10/1987 (Black Monday), 8/1990 (Gulf War), 1997-1998 (Asian crisis), 10/1998 (LTCM).

## 4. Korea / International Applicability

**Very high relevance for Korean market**:
- **KOSDAQ is genuinely illiquid**: Effective spread on KOSDAQ small-caps easily exceeds 1-3% — far larger than NYSE. The direct liquidity level effect (E(c^i)) is much larger in Korea than the 3.5% US estimate.
- **Flight to liquidity in Korea**: Korean institutional investors (pension funds, insurance) are severely constrained in holding illiquid KOSDAQ stocks. During market stress, forced selling exacerbates KOSDAQ illiquidity. cov(c^i, r^M) channel is likely even stronger in Korea than US.
- **Amihud ILLIQ for Korea**: Directly applicable using daily return and volume from OHLCVS data in `RAWDATA.parquet`. Can construct monthly ILLIQ for all 804 tickers.
- **Korean liquidity premium estimate**: Given higher absolute illiquidity, the Korean liquidity premium is likely 3-6%/yr (vs. 4.6% total US estimate), making it one of the most important factors for Korean small/mid-cap strategies.
- **Regime-conditional**: During Korean market stress (VIX spike, KRW depreciation, HY spread widening), cov(c^i, r^M) becomes extreme. Liquidity factor loading should be monitored as a regime indicator.

**R implementation of Amihud ILLIQ for Korea**:
```r
# Compute monthly Amihud ILLIQ from OHLCVS data
compute_amihud <- function(ohlcvs_panel) {
  ohlcvs_panel %>%
    # Daily return
    group_by(ticker) %>%
    arrange(date) %>%
    mutate(ret = close / lag(close) - 1) %>%
    # Daily illiquidity
    mutate(daily_illiq = abs(ret) / (volume * close / 1e6 + 1e-6)) %>%  # |R| / Volume(M KRW)
    # Monthly aggregate
    mutate(ym = floor_date(date, "month")) %>%
    group_by(ticker, ym) %>%
    summarise(
      illiq_m = mean(daily_illiq, na.rm = TRUE),
      n_days = n(),
      .groups = "drop"
    ) %>%
    filter(n_days >= 15)  # Require minimum 15 trading days
}
```

## 5. Actionable Implications for Pipeline

1. **Liquidity factor construction**: Compute monthly Amihud ILLIQ for all KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 tickers using daily volume and return from `RAWDATA.parquet`. This is the primary liquidity proxy.

2. **L-CAPM implementation for Korea**:
   - Normalize ILLIQ: c^i_t = min(0.25 + 0.30 * ILLIQ^i_t * P^M_{t-1}, 0.30)
   - Estimate market illiquidity: c^M_t = equal-weighted average of c^i_t
   - Extract illiquidity innovations via AR(2)
   - Estimate four betas per stock/portfolio via time-series regression

3. **The dominant channel (cov(c^i, r^M)) as a factor**:
   - Compute for each stock the covariance of its illiquidity with the market return (rolling 36-month)
   - Sort stocks by this covariance: high cov(c^i, r^M) = highly illiquid in down markets = high liquidity risk
   - Factor return = long low-cov(c^i, r^M) minus short high-cov(c^i, r^M)
   - Expected premium: ~0.82%/yr in US, likely 1.5-3%/yr in Korea

4. **Liquidity as regime filter**: When innovations in market illiquidity (u_t from AR(2) residuals) are positive and large (illiquidity shock), reduce exposure to high-illiquidity stocks. This is the "Buddha Mode" in `data_collector_fred.R` extended to Korean market illiquidity.

5. **Long-only adaptation**: Since our strategy is long-only, the liquidity factor becomes a positive screen: prefer stocks with low ILLIQ and low cov(c^i, r^M). Avoid stocks that become illiquid during market downturns.

## 6. Strategy Ideas Extracted

- **Liquidity-Adjusted Alpha (Korean L-CAPM)**: Compute each stock's "liquidity-adjusted expected return" using Equation (12). Rank stocks by the gap between actual expected return (from value/quality factors) and liquidity-adjusted required return. Long stocks where actual expected return >> L-CAPM required return (genuine mispricing net of liquidity cost).

- **Liquidity Risk Premium Harvesting**: Construct a portfolio that deliberately holds mid-tier liquidity stocks (KOSDAQ150 with 6-12 month Amihud ILLIQ in 2nd-4th quintile). These stocks earn the liquidity premium without the extreme trading cost of the most illiquid names. Rebalance quarterly to minimize transaction cost drag.

- **Flight-to-Liquidity Regime Strategy**: Monitor monthly innovations in Korean market illiquidity (u_t). When u_t > 2 standard deviations (liquidity crisis), rotate immediately to KOSPI200 large-cap stocks (low ILLIQ, low cov(c^i, r^M)). Return to normal allocation when u_t normalizes. This is a direct implementation of Proposition 2/3 (illiquidity predicts future returns).
