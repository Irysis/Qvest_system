# P070: Crowded Trades and Tail Risk (Brown, Howard & Lundblad 2020)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Crowding
**Feasibility:** medium (requires institutional ownership database; proxy feasible via KRX/DART)
**Novelty:** high (best paper on crowding → TAIL RISK / DRAWDOWN channel; hedge fund specific)

## Core Claim
Hedge fund crowdedness at the individual stock level is distinct from illiquidity and is a significant predictor of both average returns and downside tail risk. The preferred crowding measure is Days-ADV (how many trading days it would take the hedge fund universe to collectively exit a position). High-crowdedness stocks earn higher average returns (compensation for tail risk) but suffer disproportionately during market stress events (financial crisis 2008). Funds with higher exposure to crowded positions experience larger drawdowns during industry distress.

## Crowding Detection Method (exact metrics if available)

**Three candidate measures (all quarterly, from 13F filings):**

1. **NHF (Number of Hedge Funds)**: number of hedge fund management companies holding the stock
   - 2004: avg 15 funds/stock → 2017: avg 28 funds/stock (doubling over sample period)
   - Simple count; ignores position size

2. **PSO (Percent of Shares Outstanding)**: collective HF ownership as % of shares outstanding
   - PSO = (total value held by all HFs) / (market cap of stock)
   - 2004: avg 8% → 2008 peak: 14% → 2017: ~12%
   - Better than NHF; captures position magnitude

3. **Days-ADV (preferred measure)**: hedge fund universe's total value held divided by daily dollar volume
   ```
   Days-ADV_{i,t} = (HF total value in stock i) / (Average Daily Volume_{i,t} in dollars)
   ```
   - Interpretation: how many days at 100% ADV to exit; practitioners use 20% ADV rule → multiply by 5
   - 2004: avg 18 days → 2017: avg 25 days
   - Decomposition: Days-ADV = PSO × ILLIQ where ILLIQ = market cap / ADV
   - Captures BOTH ownership concentration AND underlying stock illiquidity

**Portfolio sorts (quintile portfolios Q1=least crowded, Q5=most crowded):**
- Q1 average Days-ADV: ~5 or fewer days
- Q5 average Days-ADV: ~56 days
- Portfolio transition probability: 77% stay in Q1 (Q5: 82%) next quarter → signal is sticky
- Higher-crowdedness stocks earn higher average returns: 8.5%/yr (Q1) to 15.6%/yr (Q5) on Days-ADV equal-weighted
- Return differential survives Fama-French 3-factor and illiquidity controls

**Tail risk analysis (2008 crisis):**
- Cumulative Abnormal Returns during 2008: crowded stocks (Q5) show significantly negative CARs that reverse in 2009
- Uncrowded stocks (Q1): CARs closer to zero (no temporary dislocation)
- Fund-level drawdown regression: higher Days-ADV portfolio weight → larger fund drawdown during crisis
- Fund disappearance (delisting) incidence is significantly higher for high-crowdedness funds

**Hedge fund vs. mutual fund comparison:**
- HF crowdedness: predicts significant return spreads AND tail risk
- MF crowdedness: weak return prediction, NO evidence of tail risk → hedge fund specificity

## Key Results
- High-crowdedness stocks earn higher average returns (reward for crowding/tail risk)
- During crisis: crowded stocks suffer disproportionate losses (temporary fire-sale dislocations) that subsequently reverse
- The Days-ADV measure is superior to PSO or NHF because it captures the exit-friction dimension
- Both security-level crowdedness AND peer crowdedness (crowdedness of the fund's OTHER holdings) predict fund drawdowns
- Funds of funds are particularly exposed to crowding despite supposed diversification
- Crowdedness at the portfolio level (not just security level) determines fund tail risk

## Korea Applicability
- **Days-ADV proxy for Korea:**
  - Numerator: 외국인+기관 보유금액 from KRX daily data (accumulated quarterly)
  - Denominator: 60-day average daily dollar volume from RAWDATA.parquet
  - `Korean_Days_ADV_{i,t} = (Foreigner+Inst holdings in KRW) / (60-day ADTV in KRW)`
  - This requires an estimate of total institutional holdings in each stock — derivable from KRX 투자자별매매동향 accumulated over time, or from DART major shareholder disclosures
- **Korea constraint**: No equivalent of Novus HF database. Korean hedge fund holdings are not publicly disclosed at the individual stock level. Best proxy: combine 외국인 지분율 (foreigner ratio) with DART institutional ownership.
- **DART shortcut**: Large institutional positions (5%+ stakes) reported via 대량보유보고 (13D/G equivalent). Use these to estimate major institutional concentration.
- **Practical Korea proxy**: Use PSO-equivalent = (외국인+기관 보유비율) from KRX daily data, then multiply by (ILLIQ = 1/ADTV ratio) to approximate Days-ADV.
- **LowVol stocks**: Typically held by pension funds and insurance companies (long-term holders) → PSO may be high but Days-ADV may be lower if they're liquid stocks. Need to verify.

## Notes
- SSRN Working Paper, July 2020 (Brown, Howard, Lundblad at UNC Chapel Hill)
- Uses Novus proprietary database of 1,466 HF management companies → not replicable externally
- 83 pages; read first 20 pages (through beginning of Section 3 — building crowdedness portfolios)
- The Days-ADV measure is practically used by quant PMs globally to gauge exit friction
- Key distinction: crowding → higher average returns (information) BUT also higher tail risk (fire sale risk)
- This differs from mutual fund crowding (P077 Zhong et al.) where crowding → lower returns — because HFs are informed, MFs are noise-trading into crowded stocks
- For STR_011 application: use Days-ADV as a TAIL RISK screen, not as an alpha signal. High Days-ADV = higher return but more crash risk during macro stress.
- The fire-sale mechanism: during 2008, hedge funds faced redemptions → sold crowded positions → price spiral → other HFs hit stop-losses → cascade. Same dynamic possible in Korea during 외국인 매도 episodes.
- Cross-reference with macro_regime.parquet: when VIX regime = HIGH or KRW regime = STRESS, high Days-ADV stocks become acutely dangerous
