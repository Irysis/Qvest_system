# P110 — Blitz, van Brakel & Vidojevic (2018): Does a Liquidity Factor Premium Exist?

**Citation:** Blitz, D., van Brakel, R., & Vidojevic, M. (2018). Does a liquidity factor premium exist in the stock market? Robeco Quantitative Research working paper.

---

## Core Thesis

A practitioner-oriented skeptical review arguing that **evidence for a standalone liquidity premium is weak**. Two main problems:
1. The liquidity premium is largely driven by **microcap stocks** — once microcaps are removed, most liquidity measures become insignificant
2. The liquidity premium is **not robust across time periods** — it varies substantially and has declined post-publication

However, liquidity remains important as a **portfolio construction tool** (implementation cost management, factor exposure management) even if it doesn't generate standalone alpha.

---

## Factor Construction

**Review of evidence across liquidity measures:**
- Hou, Xue & Zhang (2017) examined 102 published liquidity-related factors
  - Found 95 of 102 insignificant after removing microcaps
  - Only 7 measures survive with significant premiums in large-cap universe
- Li, Novy-Marx & Velikov (2017): PS factor sensitive to construction details
- Amihud (2002): ILLIQ premium partly driven by pre-2000 data and small stocks

**What survives after removing microcaps:**
- Short-term reversal (partially liquidity-driven)
- Some bid-ask spread measures in specific markets
- Amihud ILLIQ shows weaker but still positive premium in large-cap sample

**Robeco's own tests:** Using European and US data with liquidity-filtered universe (practical investable universe), liquidity factor alpha is near zero after controlling for size, value, momentum, and low-volatility factors.

---

## Key Results

- 95/102 liquidity anomalies insignificant without microcaps (Hou et al. 2017)
- Liquidity premium strongest: 1963-1990 (US); weaker post-1990; near zero post-2000 for most measures
- Amihud ILLIQ: significant cross-sectional effect, but most of premium comes from stocks with market cap < $500M
- PS factor: sensitive to construction; annual rebalancing and equal-number sorts crucial
- **Robeco conclusion**: do NOT include liquidity as a standalone long/short factor in smart beta products
- **BUT**: liquidity-constrained implementation is essential — use liquidity as a negative screen to avoid unimplementable positions

**The implementation angle:**
- In practice, trading costs can easily consume 50-100% of a liquidity factor's theoretical alpha
- The most illiquid stocks (highest theoretical premium) are precisely the ones with the highest transaction costs
- Net-of-cost liquidity premium is likely near zero for institutional investors

---

## Korea-Specific Implications

- Korean KOSPI200 universe is exactly the "non-microcap" universe where liquidity premium evidence is weakest
- For KOSDAQ150, the premium may be stronger (smaller stocks, lower institutional coverage)
- Korean retail investors are "noise traders" who tend to pile into high-turnover stocks — this could sustain an illiquidity premium in less-followed names
- The 95/102 finding should make us skeptical about standalone liquidity factors in KOSPI200 universe
- For Moltbot's concentrated 20-30 stock portfolio: liquidity matters primarily as an implementation screen, not as an alpha signal for large-cap names

## Actionable Implementation

This paper argues AGAINST building a standalone liquidity factor for the investable universe. Instead:

```r
# Robeco recommendation: use liquidity as implementation screen only

liquidity_screen <- function(candidate_stocks, min_daily_volume_krw = 5e9) {
  # Minimum 5 billion KRW average daily volume (adjust based on portfolio size)
  # This eliminates stocks that are too expensive to trade

  candidate_stocks %>%
    group_by(ticker) %>%
    mutate(
      adv_20d = slider::slide_dbl(volume_krw, mean, .before = 19, .complete = TRUE),
      max_position_krw = adv_20d * 0.10,  # max 10% of ADV as position size
      is_liquid_enough = adv_20d >= min_daily_volume_krw
    ) %>%
    filter(is_liquid_enough)
}
```

**Strategic position:** Liquidity as SCREEN (yes), liquidity as SIGNAL (weak, regime-dependent), liquidity as STANDALONE FACTOR (skeptical, especially large-cap).
