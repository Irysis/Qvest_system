# P175: An Evaluation of Alternative Equity Indices Part 1: Heuristic and Optimised Weighting Schemes — Clare, Motson & Thomas (2013)
**Category:** Smart_Beta | **Priority:** 3

## Core Finding
Comprehensive comparison of 8 alternative weighting schemes vs. cap-weight for 1,000 US stocks, 1968–2011. Results (Table 2): Cap-weight Sharpe = 0.32, worst performer. Best performers: MVP Sharpe = 0.50 (max drawdown -32.5%), Risk Efficient Sharpe = 0.43 (return 11.5%). Only Inverse Volatility and Equal Risk Contribution are statistically significantly better than cap-weight at 95% confidence. MVP has beta = 0.51, indicating genuinely different risk source. 10 million Monte Carlo simulations confirm randomly weighted portfolios also beat cap-weight — consistent with the monkey portfolio argument but NOT refuting that specific weighting schemes add value beyond randomness.

## Key Risk/Concern for Korean Implementation
The MVP's dominance (best Sharpe, lowest drawdown -32.5% vs. -48.5% for cap-weight) relies heavily on a well-estimated covariance matrix. In Korea, the covariance matrix is noisier (higher estimation error, more regime changes) than the US, potentially degrading MVP performance. The 1990s were the "market-cap decade" where cap-weight outperformed most alternatives (Table 2 Panel B, 1990s returns: cap-weight 17.6% vs. MVP 11.2%) — Korean growth regimes may show similar cap-weight dominance.

## Actionable Takeaway
1. Include MVP and Inverse Volatility as baseline comparison strategies in the harness when testing new factor strategies; if a new strategy does not outperform MVP on risk-adjusted basis, reconsider complexity justification.
2. The statistically significant alternatives (Inverse Volatility, Equal Risk Contribution) are relatively simple to implement in R without optimization; test these as low-complexity alternatives to the full Ledoit-Wolf optimized portfolio in the Korean context.
