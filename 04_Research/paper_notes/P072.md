# P072: The Failure of Long-Term Capital Management (Chincarini 1998)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Crowding
**Feasibility:** low (case study; no implementable formula)
**Novelty:** low (historical analysis)

## Core Claim
LTCM's collapse in 1998 illustrates the systemic risk from strategy crowding. When many funds replicate similar convergence trades, strategy-level return correlations that appear near-zero in normal times can jump dramatically during crisis (from ~0.078 to ~0.372), causing simultaneous losses across supposedly diversified portfolios. The "LTCM babies" (copycats) amplified systemic risk far beyond LTCM's own leverage.

## Crowding Detection Method (exact metrics if available)
No formal crowding metric proposed. The paper is primarily a forensic case study. Key observations:
- LTCM's strategy mix: fixed income relative value, equity volatility, merger arbitrage, emerging markets
- Measured correlation between LTCM strategies and hedge fund returns during crisis period
- Strategy correlations jumped from 0.078 (normal) to 0.372 (crisis) in August–September 1998
- Position concentration: LTCM held positions equal to ~10x daily volume in some markets
- The "LTCM babies" — funds that copied LTCM's known positions (via prime broker intelligence) — created a crowded trade without formal coordination

**Implicit crowding indicators discussed:**
- Unusually tight spreads in convergence trades (carry below historical norms = crowded)
- Position size relative to daily volume (ADV ratio)
- Number of funds known to hold similar positions (prime broker "club" knowledge)

## Key Results
- Correlation crisis: diversification across strategy types failed simultaneously; portfolios that appeared independent became correlated
- LTCM's own leverage (25:1) was not the sole problem — the crowding of LTCM-like strategies across the industry meant the aggregate leverage was far higher
- Rescue required Federal Reserve coordination — systemic risk from crowding at hedge fund level
- Key lesson: the risk of a trade is endogenous to the number of other investors in the same trade

## Korea Applicability
- **Direct applicability**: Low. LTCM was a specific leveraged fixed-income arbitrage fund; Korean quant strategies are long-only equity.
- **Conceptual applicability**: High. The "crowding multiplier" concept applies — if multiple Korean quant funds hold similar LowVol baskets, an external shock (e.g., sudden risk-on rotation) could cause correlated selling.
- **Korean context**: Korean quant fund AUM has grown; many domestic quants use similar KOSPI200 factor models (Barra Korea). This creates structural crowding in widely-used factors.
- **Risk model homogeneity**: Like LTCM-era, use of same commercial risk models (e.g., FactSet, MSCI Barra Korea) by multiple Korean managers creates correlated portfolio construction → same crowding dynamics as Bruno et al. (2018)

## Actionable Ideas
- SKIP as direct implementation source — no quantitative crowding metric.
- Conceptual takeaway for portfolio construction: diversify away from the most commonly traded factor in Korea (likely Momentum or Quality, based on known domestic quant activity). Maintain idiosyncratic factor exposure.
