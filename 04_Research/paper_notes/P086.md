**Source**: Multinational Finance Journal (SSRN 2611047)
**Year**: ~2014

## 1. Core Thesis

Seven equity anomalies (AG, BM, IA, MOM, NSI, SIZE, TA) are tested across 34 countries using zero-cost portfolios. The paper examines whether these anomalies persist after controlling for idiosyncratic risk and whether they differ between developed and emerging markets. The central finding is that anomaly returns are substantially attenuated once idiosyncratic risk is accounted for, and that emerging markets (including Korea) exhibit stronger BM and size effects than developed markets.

---

## 2. Factor Construction

**Portfolio formation**: 6/1/6 Jegadeesh-Titman methodology (6-month formation, 1-month skip, 6-month holding for MOM; monthly rebalancing for other anomalies).

**Anomaly definitions**:
- **AG** (Asset Growth): Annual change in total assets / lagged total assets. Short high-growers, long low-growers.
- **BM** (Book-to-Market): Book equity / market equity. Long high BM (value), short low BM (growth).
- **IA** (Investment-to-Assets): Capital expenditure / lagged total assets. Short high-investment firms.
- **MOM** (Momentum): Past 6-month return (1-month skip). Long winners, short losers.
- **NSI** (Net Stock Issues): Log change in split-adjusted shares outstanding. Short high-issuers.
- **SIZE**: Market cap. Long small-cap, short large-cap.
- **TA** (Total Accruals): (Change in current assets - change in cash) - (change in current liabilities - change in short-term debt - change in taxes payable) - depreciation, scaled by lagged total assets. Short high accruals.

**Risk adjustment**: Fama-French 4-factor model + Chen (2010) alternative 3-factor model. Idiosyncratic risk measured as residual variance from market model.

## 3. Key Results

**South Korea specific results (from Table 3)**:
- BM: H-L = 1.208%/month, t = 2.19 (significant — strongest anomaly in Korea)
- MOM: H-L = 0.232%/month, t = 0.65 (not significant — consistent with Korean MOM literature)
- SIZE: Negative premium in some subperiods
- TA, NSI: Modest effect

**Cross-country summary**:
- BM and IA anomalies are stronger in emerging markets than developed
- MOM is weaker in Asian emerging markets (consistent with Griffin et al. 2003)
- Idiosyncratic risk positively related to anomaly magnitude: stocks with high idiosyncratic risk show larger anomaly spreads but also higher unexplained volatility
- 34-country sample; about 50% of anomalies survive risk adjustment at 5% level

## 4. Korea / International Applicability

**Highly applicable**:
- Korea BM premium (1.208%/month) is economically large and statistically significant
- Korean MOM fails — this confirms well-known KOSPI momentum weakness; do NOT use raw price momentum in Korean strategies
- Emerging market context: BM and IA anomalies are more robust in Korea than in the US
- KOSDAQ not explicitly covered but KOSPI subsample supports value-over-growth tilt
- Idiosyncratic risk is higher in Korean small-caps, suggesting anomaly returns are partially compensation for specific risk — need residual decomposition before factor construction

## 5. Actionable Implications for Pipeline

1. **Value factor (BM)**: Korean BM premium is confirmed. Implement as fundamental B/P ratio (book equity from DART / market cap). Use KOSPI200+KOSDAQ150 universe. NYSE-style breakpoints inappropriate; use Korean-exchange breakpoints.
2. **Avoid raw MOM**: Korean momentum (H-L = 0.232, t=0.65) is not investable as standalone factor. If momentum is used, condition on regime (up-trending market only) or use residual momentum after market/industry neutralization.
3. **Idiosyncratic risk control**: High idiosyncratic risk inflates apparent anomaly returns. Mandatory: compute residuals from market model before ranking on any fundamental factor. This aligns with the project's residual-first protocol.
4. **IA / AG factors**: Investment growth and asset growth anomalies are present in emerging markets. Can be constructed from DART annual capex and total assets data. Rebalance annually (not monthly) to reduce turnover.

## 6. Strategy Ideas Extracted

- **Value-Quality Composite (Korea-specific)**: Long stocks with simultaneously high BM (value) and low TA (low accrual / high earnings quality). The combination of these two anomalies — both significant in Korea — provides a double-filter that removes value traps.
- **Regime-Conditional BM Strategy**: BM premium in Korea is concentrated in up-markets (risk-on regimes). Combine BM ranking with macro regime signal (VIX, credit spread) to time market exposure. Go long value only in "normal" or "risk-on" regimes; reduce allocation in distress regimes.
- **Anti-Momentum Tilt**: Given Korean MOM weakness, implement a contrarian reversal overlay (1-month return reversal is significant in Korea) on top of value portfolio to reduce short-term mean-reversion drag.
