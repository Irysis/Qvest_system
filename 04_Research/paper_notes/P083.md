# P083: Factor Investing in Practice (Koedijk, Slager, Stork 2014)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Practitioner_Implementation
**Relevance to Korea pipeline:** Medium

## Core Claim
Factor investing can be implemented in institutional portfolios using three progressive approaches: (1) Risk due diligence, (2) Factor tilts, and (3) Full factor optimization. European data from 1975 confirms that the US factor premia (size, value, momentum, low volatility) are replicated in European markets, validating factor universality. A factor is only worth implementing if it passes both economic/behavioral theory AND statistical persistence criteria.

## Key Findings (specific percentages, survival rates, which anomalies)
- Research report written for Robeco (asset manager), January 2014
- European factor performance since 1975 mirrors US results for:
  - **Size (small cap)**: positive premium in Europe (similar magnitude to US)
  - **Value**: positive premium in Europe
  - **Momentum**: positive premium in Europe
  - **Low Volatility**: positive premium in Europe
- Return decomposition framework:
  - **Total Return = Asset Class Return + Style Factor Return + Strategy Factor Return + Alpha**
  - Style factors: value, size, momentum, low volatility (systematic, long-horizon)
  - Strategy factors: carry, trend, specific anomalies (shorter-lived, requires more monitoring)
- Three implementation models:
  1. **Risk due diligence**: understand factor exposures of existing managers (no active tilting)
  2. **Factor tilts**: actively tilt portfolio toward desired factors (passive implementation)
  3. **Factor optimization**: full mean-variance optimization over factor space (active management)
- **8-point implementation checklist** (key criteria):
  1. Strong economic rationale (risk-based or behavioral)
  2. Statistical persistence over multiple periods
  3. Implementation costs (turnover, capacity)
  4. Factor cyclicality awareness (2-3 year drawdown windows)
  5. Factor correlation structure (diversification benefit)
  6. Benchmark deviation tolerance
  7. Regular review of economic rationale (adapt to changing market structure)
  8. Governance: treat factor investing as an investment belief, not a technique
- **Factor premium classification**:
  - Style premiums (persistent): value, size, momentum, low vol — backed by decades of evidence
  - Strategy premiums (less persistent): carry, trend, specific accounting anomalies — require more monitoring
- Persistence criterion: factors MUST have both economic theory AND statistical evidence; either alone is insufficient
- Implementation costs matter: high-turnover factors (momentum) have higher implementation cost drag vs low-turnover (value)

## Factors That SURVIVE Post-Publication
- **Value**: lowest turnover (20% annually per MSCI), most cost-efficient persistent factor
- **Low Volatility**: confirmed in Europe, low turnover, behavioral theory (leverage aversion)
- **Size (Small Cap)**: confirmed in Europe, but long underperformance cycles
- **Momentum**: confirmed in Europe, but high turnover (127% annually) reduces net alpha significantly

## Factors That DECAY Post-Publication
- **Strategy factors** (short-lived, require active monitoring): anomaly-based, earnings surprise, etc.
- High-turnover anomalies: gross implementation cost erodes net alpha
- Growth factors: not included as persistent factor (consistent with MSCI Foundations)
- Liquidity factors: not included as persistent factor

## Korea/International Applicability
- European evidence (1975+) confirms non-US factor premia
- Aligned with Jacobs & Muller (P079): international markets preserve factor premia
- The return decomposition framework is directly applicable to Korean portfolio construction
- Korea-specific adaptation: replace "Asset Class Return" with KOSPI200 benchmark return
- Implementation model recommendation for our pipeline: we are at "Factor tilts" level → evolving toward "Factor optimization"
- Koedijk et al. framework is essentially what backtest_harness.R implements: factor tilts applied monthly
- Style vs Strategy distinction: for Korea, Value/Size/LowVol are style (persistent); specific accounting anomalies are strategy (need more careful testing)
- Implementation cost note: Korean transaction costs ~10-20 bps/trade → affects momentum more than value
