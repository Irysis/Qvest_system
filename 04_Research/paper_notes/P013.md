# P013: The Disposition to Sell Winners Too Early and Ride Losers Too Long (1985)

## Core Finding
Shefrin & Statman document and theorize the "disposition effect": investors systematically sell winning positions too early and hold losing positions too long relative to tax-optimal and return-maximizing strategies. The framework integrates prospect theory (loss aversion), mental accounting, regret aversion, and self-control. Empirical evidence from brokerage account data shows loss realizations concentrated in December, inconsistent with rational tax optimization but consistent with the theory.

## Key Metrics
- Alpha/Return: Disposition effect generates predictable post-sale momentum: stocks sold by disposition-biased investors continue to outperform
- Statistical strength: Concentration of loss realizations in December documented empirically
- Sample: US brokerage accounts, Journal of Finance 1985

## Korea Applicability
HIGH — The disposition effect is documented in Korean retail investor data (Korea Exchange studies show Korean retail investors are among the most prone to disposition bias globally). Korean retail dominance in KOSDAQ makes this effect particularly strong. Stocks with high retail ownership that have recent gains are systematically underpriced relative to fundamental value as retail investors sell too early.

## Strategy Implication
Two exploitable signals from disposition effect: (1) Unrealized capital gain overhang (UCGO): stocks with high average cost basis relative to current price are held by loss-averse investors reluctant to sell — these experience momentum continuation; compute as (price - 52w_avg_price)/52w_avg_price. (2) Post-earnings-announcement drift is amplified by disposition effect as investors sell earnings winners too quickly. Long stocks with positive earnings surprises AND high recent returns (disposition selling creates cheap entry). In R: ucgo <- (close - rollmean(close, 252)) / rollmean(close, 252).
