# Paper Note: P044 — Allen, Larson & Sloan (2009)
# "Accrual Reversals, Earnings and Stock Returns"

**Authors:** Eric Allen, Chad Larson, Richard G. Sloan
**Year:** 2009 (working paper)
**Category:** Factor_Return / Accrual_Factors

## Core Thesis
Extreme accruals MUST mechanically reverse due to double-entry accounting. Top-decile accruals → next period low decile: 4.95% frequency (vs 1% random). This mechanical reversal explains the entire Sloan accrual anomaly.

## Key New Insight
- Accrual anomaly is NOT mispricing per se — it's **mechanical reversal predictability**
- After controlling for reversals, accruals no longer predict returns independently
- Inventory accruals show STRONGER reversals than aggregate (2.16% vs 1.90% extreme rate)

## Key Numbers
- Top decile → bottom decile next year: **-22.4% abnormal return**
- Bottom decile → top decile next year: **+11.4% abnormal return**
- Extreme reversal rate: 4.95% observed vs 1% expected

## Korean Implementation
Yes, DART implementable. Mechanical reversal property holds regardless of market. In Korea: inventory write-downs (재고자산감액) are particularly common in manufacturing (반도체 사이클) — these are strong accrual reversal signals.

## Anomaly Status
**Mechanically persistent** — reversal is inevitable by accounting rules. Cannot decay because it's not information-based mispricing but structural accounting property.
